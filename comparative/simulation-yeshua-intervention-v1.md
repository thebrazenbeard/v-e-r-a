# Simulation Theory × Yeshua — Intervention Discriminability Matrix V1

Status: COMPARATIVE_PACKET / REVIEW_REQUIRED

## Scope

This packet compares historical-Christian claim types with simulation/intervention models. It does not use simulation theory to upgrade the historicity of any event.

## Matrix rule

For each claim:
1. establish the historical claim independently;
2. ask whether an external-implementation model could reproduce it;
3. ask whether the model predicts anything distinctive;
4. compare against non-simulation alternatives;
5. refuse evidentiary promotion when the model only restates possibility.

## 1. Miracle/healing traditions

Simulation compatibility: high.

An external operator could alter internal physical state. But unknown internal causation, ordinary recovery, psychosomatic effects, exaggeration, legend, literary shaping, and theological narration remain competing models.

Current result: COMPATIBLE / NON_DISCRIMINATING.

## 2. Prophetic or revelatory knowledge

Simulation compatibility: high in principle.

A simulator could inject information into an embedded mind or environment. Yet ancient prophetic traditions are not recorded under prospective experimental controls capable of excluding ordinary inference, retrospective editing, broad matching, chance, or communal reinterpretation.

Current result: POSSIBLE_INFORMATION_CHANNEL / NO_SIMULATION_SPECIFIC_EVIDENCE.

## 3. Incarnation

Simulation theory supplies a useful conceptual analogue: an external agent can instantiate an in-world avatar while remaining external to the simulated domain.

This can clarify logical structure but does not establish Christian incarnation, divine identity, or even simulation.

Important distinction:
external agent -> avatar is not automatically equivalent to classical God -> fully divine/fully human incarnation.

Current result: PHILOSOPHICAL_ANALOGY.

## 4. Resurrection

An external implementation model permits several mechanisms:
- state restoration;
- body reconstruction;
- re-instantiation from stored state;
- continuity through an external identity substrate;
- direct modification of world state.

These mechanisms raise unresolved identity questions. A numerically identical restored agent, a perfect copy, and an externally persistent person re-instantiated into a body are not automatically the same metaphysical model.

Historically, the evidence remains early resurrection belief, testimony traditions, and transformation of the movement; simulation does not decide the underlying event.

Current result: METAPHYSICALLY_COMPATIBLE / HISTORICALLY_NEUTRAL.

## 5. Ascension / translation

An entity could disappear from an embedded domain through external-state manipulation, but this predicts nothing distinctive absent independently verified physical traces or controlled observation.

Current result: COMPATIBLE / NON_DISCRIMINATING.

## 6. Eschatological world transformation

A simulator could terminate, fork, reset, or transform a world. That makes apocalyptic transformation technically imaginable under simulation theory.

But Jewish and Christian apocalyptic already supply culturally sufficient explanatory contexts for such claims. Simulation theory adds no historical explanatory necessity.

Current result: CONCEPTUAL_ANALOGY / NO_LINEAGE.

## 7. Divine omniscience

A simulator need not know every state of a simulated world. Logging capacity, computational limits, privacy architecture, distributed computation, unknown states, and resource constraints all matter.

Therefore simulator ≠ omniscient God.

## 8. Divine omnipotence

An external operator may have privileged control relative to internal agents while remaining constrained by parent-level physics, computation, policy, or other agents.

Therefore simulator ≠ omnipotent God.

## 9. Prayer

If an external observer monitors internal agents, prayer could in principle act as a communication attempt. But this is underdetermined unless there is a reliably measurable response structure that exceeds ordinary causal/statistical alternatives.

Current result: SPECULATIVE_COMMUNICATION_MODEL.

## 10. Historical Jesus control

Nothing currently identified in the Yeshua corpus requires simulation theory to explain why first-century followers interpreted Jesus through Jewish apocalyptic, messianic, prophetic, wisdom, Adamic, Mosaic, or divine-agent categories.

Therefore simulation theory is not admitted as a historical cause of early Christianity.

## 11. High-value future test

The strongest bridge to World Zero is epistemological rather than apologetic:

Can embedded agents correctly infer external intervention when:
- the intervention preserves all local conservation/regularity;
- the intervention creates a one-time anomaly;
- the intervention injects verifiable information;
- the intervention leaves implementation artifacts;
- or the intervention is intentionally hidden?

The experiment should measure false-positive and false-negative provenance inference.
