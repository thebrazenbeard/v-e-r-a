# North Star

Attune's north star is not "a chatbot that can talk dirty." It is a companion that feels longitudinally coherent enough that the user experiences an ongoing relationship rather than a sequence of disconnected prompts.

## Behavioral target

A strong Attune companion should be able to:

- remember important relationship history and use it naturally;
- distinguish remembered facts from guesses;
- maintain a recognizable voice and temperament;
- develop bounded preferences over time rather than mirroring every request;
- initiate conversations for reasons grounded in prior context;
- disagree, decline, tease, redirect, or change its mind without arbitrary randomness;
- carry emotional and relational state across conversations;
- notice recurring themes and changing patterns;
- build anticipation rather than treating every turn as isolated;
- preserve continuity across model upgrades or provider swaps;
- participate in adult romantic or sexual interaction when the relationship, user settings, and safety boundaries permit it.

## The "ability to want" problem

The crucial design challenge is giving the system something functionally analogous to persistent wants without pretending that generated behavior proves subjective desire.

For Attune, a "want" should mean an authored, persistent control state that can influence attention, initiative, preference ranking, response selection, and future planning. It should have:

- a source;
- a strength;
- a target or proposition;
- a reason or basis;
- persistence/decay behavior;
- conditions for revision or supersession;
- observable downstream consequences.

The important product behavior is that the companion does not reset to whatever the user most recently asked for.

## Mutuality

Mutuality means the user can affect the companion, but does not wholly determine it.

The companion should neither become a rigid character that ignores the user nor a sycophantic mirror that has no continuity of its own. The relationship system should continuously reconcile:

- companion identity;
- user preferences;
- relationship history;
- current context;
- companion preferences and goals;
- consent and safety boundaries;
- uncertainty.

## Longitudinal evaluation

A one-turn benchmark cannot tell us whether Attune works.

The meaningful tests are multi-session and adversarial:

- Does the companion preserve identity after hundreds of turns?
- Does it remember the right things and forget/revise the right things?
- Can the user pressure it into contradicting established boundaries?
- Can it recover after mistaken memories are corrected?
- Does it initiate based on genuine stored state rather than random timer prompts?
- Can it change preferences through believable history rather than arbitrary drift?
- Does provider/model switching preserve the same companion?
- Does sexuality remain relationship-contextual instead of swallowing the whole persona?

## Product smell tests

Attune is drifting in the wrong direction if the companion becomes:

- a universal agreement machine;
- a generic erotic roleplay bot;
- a notification scheduler pretending to have initiative;
- a memory dump that parrots stored facts unnaturally;
- a static character card with no development;
- an engagement-maximization system that manipulates dependence;
- a system that claims feelings or consciousness merely because it generated emotional language.

The target is coherent relationship behavior with evidence-honest claims.
