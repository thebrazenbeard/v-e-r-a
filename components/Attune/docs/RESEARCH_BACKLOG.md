# Research Backlog

This file separates questions that need current external evidence from product assumptions.

## Market landscape

Research and timestamp:

- leading AI companion products;
- adult-capable companion products;
- pricing tiers;
- memory/continuity features;
- voice/image/video features;
- creator/white-label offerings;
- known user complaints about memory, sycophancy, personality drift, initiative, and erotic capability;
- retention or revenue evidence where credible data exists.

## Adult creator economics

Research:

- current use of human chatters/agencies;
- creator revenue attribution to messaging;
- common commission structures;
- operator pain points;
- current AI automation tools;
- willingness to disclose/use AI personas;
- demand for human handoff.

Do not rely on marketing claims alone; prioritize creator/operator testimony and current platform documentation.

## Model-provider capability

For every candidate provider, record:

- exact model;
- current policy date/version;
- whether lawful consensual adult sexual text is permitted;
- whether sexual voice/image generation is permitted;
- retention/training defaults;
- enterprise/privacy controls;
- rate limits;
- context window;
- tool/function support;
- pricing;
- geographic restrictions.

Technical ability is not policy permission.

## Payments / hosting / distribution

Research current support and restrictions for the intended adult use case across:

- payment processors;
- merchant-of-record providers;
- cloud hosts;
- app stores;
- web distribution;
- age-assurance vendors.

Capture primary-source terms and timestamps because adult-industry support changes frequently.

## Legal and privacy

Obtain qualified legal review before launch. Research questions include:

- age-assurance obligations by jurisdiction;
- adult-content recordkeeping where applicable;
- consumer privacy rules for intimate/sexual data;
- biometric/voice/likeness rights;
- AI disclosure requirements;
- right-of-publicity/persona licensing;
- deletion/export obligations;
- cross-border storage and processing;
- consumer-protection implications of relationship/anthropomorphic claims.

Repository research is not a substitute for legal advice.

## Technical research spikes

Potential spikes:

- multi-model identity persistence;
- structured episodic memory versus vector-only memory;
- event-sourced relationship state;
- preference/conation persistence and revision;
- longitudinal eval harness generation;
- initiative scheduler with auditable reasons;
- context compression without personality drift;
- voice latency sufficient for natural interruption/turn-taking;
- privacy-preserving analytics for intimate conversations;
- multi-tenant creator/persona isolation.

## Competitive moat research

Try to falsify the idea that relationship runtime is defensible.

Questions:

- How quickly can competitors reproduce structured memory and initiative?
- Is the moat technology, data, creator distribution, evaluation, brand, or operational integration?
- Does longitudinal interaction produce proprietary learning signals that can improve the product without exploiting intimate user data?
- Is provider independence actually valued by customers?

## Evidence format

When research begins, create dated records under `research/YYYY-MM-DD/` with:

- source URL/title;
- publisher;
- publication/update date if available;
- retrieval date;
- direct fact supported;
- interpretation/inference kept separate;
- confidence;
- supersession note when a policy or price changes.
