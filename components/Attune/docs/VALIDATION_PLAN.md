# Validation Plan

Attune is currently an idea. The next job is not to prove we can build it; it is to learn whether anyone will pay for the thing we think is valuable.

## Hypotheses to test

1. Users value continuity/mutuality enough to prefer it over a highly compliant companion.
2. Adult-capable sexuality increases perceived completeness without needing to dominate the entire product.
3. B2B adult operators see enough incremental revenue or retention value to pay for white-label companion infrastructure.
4. Persistent identity, memory, preferences, and initiative produce measurable retention improvement.
5. Customers will accept companion disagreement/boundaries when the behavior feels coherent rather than arbitrary.
6. A provider-agnostic relationship runtime is commercially useful enough to justify the added engineering complexity.

## Cheapest first test: customer discovery

Before building a full system, interview legitimate adult creators, agencies, studios, erotic-game teams, and membership platforms.

Do not ask, "Would you use an AI companion?" That invites compliments and fiction.

Ask about current behavior:

- How much time is spent replying to fans/customers?
- Which conversations actually make money?
- What gets delegated today?
- What existing AI/chat tools have they tried?
- What failed?
- Which parts must sound exactly like the creator/character?
- What would they never trust an AI to say or do?
- How valuable is conversation continuity across days/weeks?
- Would they rather own the user relationship or send users to another companion platform?
- What integration would be acceptable?
- What would have to be true before they would pay?

The strongest validation is not enthusiasm. It is willingness to provide data, pilot access, engineering time, a letter of intent, or money.

## Fake-door / concept test

Create a simple landing page and short product demo showing:

- a returning user being remembered;
- a companion initiating from prior context;
- a stable preference or disagreement;
- relationship-state progression;
- optional adult interaction;
- creator controls and boundaries;
- white-label branding.

Measure:

- demo requests;
- qualified operator calls;
- pilot requests;
- willingness to connect an existing audience;
- willingness to pay or sign an LOI.

Do not misrepresent the demo as production software if it is a mockup or concierge prototype.

## Concierge prototype

A high-value test can be partly manual behind the scenes.

Instead of building a multi-tenant production platform immediately, run a single fictional/licensed persona with manually reviewed state updates and hand-tuned boundaries. The user should experience the intended relationship behavior while we learn what must eventually be automated.

This tests the product experience before paying the engineering cost of scaling it.

## A/B behavioral test

Once a prototype exists, compare two conditions using the same underlying model:

### Control

- persona prompt;
- shallow memory;
- user-led conversation;
- highly compliant behavior.

### Attune

- durable identity;
- structured memory;
- relationship state;
- preferences/conation;
- initiative;
- bounded disagreement;
- identical adult-content eligibility.

Compare:

- session return rate;
- conversation length;
- 7/30-day retention;
- user-rated feeling of being known;
- willingness to pay;
- frequency of meaningful callbacks to prior history;
- negative reactions to disagreement/initiative;
- operator conversion/revenue if run through a B2B pilot.

## Suggested validation gates

### Gate 0 — problem evidence

At least 10 serious operator conversations with repeated evidence of the same pain/problem.

### Gate 1 — concept pull

At least 3 credible operators ask to see/test the product or offer concrete pilot access.

### Gate 2 — prototype pull

At least 1 operator or a meaningful set of end users demonstrates willingness to pay, sign an LOI, or commit distribution/audience access.

### Gate 3 — behavioral proof

Attune-style longitudinal features outperform a simpler companion control on retention, perceived continuity, willingness to pay, or operator economics.

The exact numbers are provisional. The principle is not: **do not convert compliments into product-market-fit evidence.**

## Stop / pivot signals

Pause or change direction if:

- operators mostly want cheap automated sexting rather than relationship continuity;
- provider/payment/compliance restrictions make the intended experience unreliable;
- users consistently dislike companion initiative or disagreement;
- continuity features add substantial cost without improving retention or revenue;
- customers demand unlicensed real-person impersonation as a core use case;
- viable distribution requires economics that do not support the infrastructure cost.

## Immediate next research

Before implementation, gather current evidence on:

- existing adult companion products and their pricing/features;
- adult creators' current use of chatters and AI automation;
- model providers that permit lawful adult sexual content;
- payment processors willing to support the target business model;
- age-assurance expectations by target jurisdiction;
- current voice/avatar vendors and pricing;
- privacy obligations for sexual/intimate conversational data.

Keep web research separate from assumptions recorded in this repository and timestamp evidence because provider policies and laws can change quickly.
