## Synopsis and beat map
An old flame, Stacy Corvelli, tells Charlie that her badly behaved son Tommy may be his. Charlie initially wishes he could retroactively have arranged an abortion, then agrees to spend time with Tommy only after seeing an opportunity to impress the Waitress by posing as a caring mentor. Parallel to that, Mac and Dennis turn the abortion debate into a sexual marketplace: Mac adopts anti-abortion rhetoric to pursue Megan while Dennis crosses to the pro-choice side because he expects better prospects there. Their stated principles prove entirely contingent on attraction. Stacy ultimately admits Tommy is Jimmy Doyle's son and that she hoped to recruit Charlie as a replacement father. Charlie, having already leveraged Tommy in his Waitress scheme, still wants to take him to a Big Brother event. Mac's supposed conviction collapses immediately when Megan falsely tells him she is pregnant; his first response is to demand an abortion. The protest itself devolves into performative hostility and an egg fight.

## Character state
- **Charlie:** already converts other people into props in the Waitress pursuit. His brief fatherhood panic is less about parental duty than inconvenience, yet he does form enough attachment to Tommy to continue the outing after learning the truth.
- **Mac:** explicitly religious in argument but admits through behavior that sex, not doctrine, is driving him. The Megan pregnancy test exposes the gap between rhetoric and actual preference.
- **Dennis:** barely bothers pretending to possess conviction; he changes political side openly according to where he thinks women will be easier to meet.
- **Dee:** supplies the clearest critique of the men's hypocrisy, but still participates in helping Charlie stage his image for the Waitress.
- **The Waitress:** again functions as a boundary Charlie refuses to accept directly; he fabricates social virtue rather than becoming more trustworthy.

## Themes / comic mechanism
The episode uses abortion politics as a stress test for the Gang's lack of durable principles. Everyone wants an identity label only while it produces sex, approval, or leverage. The strongest recurring-series mechanism here is **position follows incentive**: ideology is not a belief system for the Gang but another scheme surface.

## Continuity / later relevance
- Expands Charlie's Catholic-school background.
- Reinforces his long-running pattern of manufacturing evidence that he is worthy of the Waitress.
- Establishes Mac's tendency to wrap desire and insecurity in moral certainty.
- Shows Dennis's unusually open nihilism about conviction.
