## Synopsis and beat map
Paddy's unexpectedly fills with teenagers after word spreads that the bar is not carding. The Gang first recognizes the legal problem, then rationalizes continued service as a safer alternative to teenagers drinking in parks; the rationalization immediately becomes a profit plan involving watered-down drinks and inflated prices. The younger crowd also reactivates the Gang's distorted memories of high school. Dee begins seeing popular student Trey, Dennis is pursued by Tammy and initially rejects her because she is in high school, and Mac and Charlie become increasingly invested in being admired by teenagers. Charlie and Mac supply alcohol to a house party after being flattered into it, while Dee and Charlie accept prom invitations and Dennis is blackmailed into attending. The fantasy collapses at Paddy's pre-prom party when Tammy and Trey reunite, the teenagers leave, and the Gang is left exposed as adults who were using teenagers to repair old status wounds.

## Character state
- **Mac:** remembers himself as popular; the others puncture that memory by saying he mainly sold weed and was regarded as an asshole.
- **Charlie:** discovers he can command teenage attention by acting authoritative, and quickly enjoys the status.
- **Dennis:** is more alert than the others to the legal/sexual line with minors, but his restraint is partly self-protective rather than principled.
- **Dee:** reveals how strongly unresolved high-school insecurity can override adult judgment; Trey makes her feel like the popular girl she believes she never got to be.

## Themes / comic mechanism
The Gang converts a genuine safeguarding problem into a self-serving business model and then into a collective high-school regression. The episode begins building one of Sunny's central ideas: adulthood has not replaced their adolescent hierarchy; it has merely given them more dangerous tools.

## Continuity / later relevance
- Introduces Nicky Potnick by name in a remembered drunk-driving story, a name that will recur in reunion-era continuity.
- Establishes conflicting and self-serving memories of the Gang's high-school social positions.
- Early example of Paddy's flourishing only when the Gang is doing something obviously unsustainable or illegal.
