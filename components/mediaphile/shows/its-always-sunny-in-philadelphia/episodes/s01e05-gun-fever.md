## Synopsis and beat map
After Paddy's safe is stolen, Mac and Dennis buy a handgun over Dee's objections. Their caution lasts only moments before shooting it becomes intoxicating; Charlie also develops what they call “gun fever” and takes the weapon home to intimidate landlord Hwang over unpaid rent. Dee's new boyfriend Colin encourages gun ownership and takes her shooting. Mac and Dennis become amateur detectives and decide Colin is the robber because he dates bartenders and learns bar layouts. They bait him by loudly announcing that Paddy's register still contains money, then stake out the dark bar with the gun. The figure they shoot is Charlie, who was trying to steal from Paddy's to cover rent. While the Gang takes Charlie to the hospital, Colin exploits the empty bar and robs it again, validating the suspicion only after the Gang has nearly killed its friend.

## Character state
- **Mac / Dennis:** the weapon rapidly changes their self-image from frightened business owners into fantasy action heroes. Their detective role-play and status dispute matter as much to them as solving the crime.
- **Charlie:** condemns their recklessness before quickly becoming equally intoxicated by the gun's sense of power; his rent crisis pushes him into theft from his own bar.
- **Dee:** starts as the anti-gun dissenter but is drawn into shooting through sexual attraction to Colin.

## Themes / comic mechanism
A security problem becomes an identity drug. The gun does not make anyone safer; it amplifies impulsiveness, paranoia, role-play, and escalation. The ending is characteristically Sunny: the Gang is simultaneously wrong in execution and right about Colin, but the way they pursue being right creates greater harm.

## Continuity / later relevance
- Establishes Hwang as Charlie's landlord and Charlie's chronic rent/financial instability.
- Creates the first “Gun Fever” template, explicitly revisited years later.
- Early evidence of the Gang's fascination with self-appointed policing and investigation.
