## Synopsis / analysis
The legal consequences of “The Gang Goes Jihad” arrive: Mac, Dennis and Dee receive community-service coaching assignments; Charlie must attend AA. The Waitress is also in AA and volunteers as Charlie's sponsor, not to help him but to gain access to Dennis. Coaching becomes another status contest. Mac and Dee fight over head-coach authority; Dennis disdains his players; Frank enters the competition and buys a rival child's absence with a bicycle. Charlie uses sponsorship to force proximity with the Waitress, while she keeps steering conversations toward Dennis. Charlie eventually relapses, referees the game drunk, and the adults encourage a children's basketball match into violence.

The episode is less about rehabilitation than the Gang's ability to colonize corrective institutions. Court-ordered service, AA sponsorship and youth sports are all converted into personal agendas. Charlie and the Waitress mirror each other more than either admits: both instrumentalize the nominal recovery relationship to pursue someone who does not want them in the desired way.
