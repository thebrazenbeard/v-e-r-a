## Synopsis / analysis
Charlie becomes furious when Dennis smokes in Paddy's and, with Dee, starts an anti-smoking protest. Dee treats activism as a performance opportunity and recruits Artemis. In reaction, Mac, Dennis and Frank declare Paddy's an “anything goes” freedom zone. The rule initially serves their fantasies of unrestrained entertainment, then attracts exactly the behavior they do not want: the McPoyles, heroin use, violence, and Frank's Vietnamese gambling associates conducting Russian roulette in the basement. Charlie's own anti-regulation rhetoric collides with his demand to control smoking and bar behavior. The unrestricted experiment ends when one participant shoots himself.

This is a clean Sunny hypocrisy engine: every character defines liberty as permission for their own preferred conduct and restriction for everybody else's. The “anything goes” rule is valuable until other people use it. Dee's protest similarly becomes another stage rather than a durable political commitment.
