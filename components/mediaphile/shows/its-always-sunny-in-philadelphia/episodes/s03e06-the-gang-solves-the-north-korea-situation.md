Paddy's faces competition from a more successful Korean bar during a pub event, and the Gang maps a local business rivalry onto an absurd North Korea geopolitical narrative. Charlie becomes romantically involved with the owner's daughter Sun-Li while the others attempt sabotage and competitive one-upmanship.

As in “The Gang Goes Jihad,” international politics function mainly as vocabulary the Gang barely understands but finds useful for dramatizing personal grievances. Charlie's relationship with Sun-Li is more direct than the Gang's grand strategy, but even that connection is pulled into the bar war. The episode shows how readily Paddy's economic insecurity becomes imagined warfare: losing customers feels to them like a national-security crisis.
