Sources: Springfield `s03e13`; Wikipedia season 3.

The cocaine-debt scheme fractures into three survival strategies: Frank effectively pimps Dennis to wealthy older women; Mac tries to make himself useful to the mob; Charlie and Dee continue attempting to sell the product but consume it themselves. Their economic plan becomes addiction, sexual exploitation and criminal servitude.

The two-parter's point is not that the Gang enters an alien criminal underworld; it is that their normal interpersonal economy scales almost seamlessly into it. Dennis's vanity becomes marketable labor, Mac's desire for status becomes mob apprenticeship, and Charlie/Dee's lack of impulse control destroys inventory. Organized criminals are dangerous, but unlike the Gang they at least understand the transaction.
