After Shelley Kelly dies, Charlie tries to honor his father's burial wishes by carrying the body up a mountain. The physical burden becomes an emotional one as the others repeatedly fail him, and Charlie finally articulates grief and the expectation that a father should have been there to carry him instead. The Gang eventually returns to help. This is one of the series' clearest demonstrations that Charlie's absurdity coexists with serious abandonment pain and that the group's attachment can become action when spectacle drops away.
