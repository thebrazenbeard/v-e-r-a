## Neuroscience / computational neuroscience

### SRC-E-001 — Berridge 2007
- title: *The debate over dopamine's role in reward: the case for incentive salience*
- DOI: `10.1007/s00213-006-0578-x`
- contribution: liking / wanting / learning dissociation; dopamine not treated as a generic pleasure scalar

### SRC-E-002 — Smith, Berridge & Aldridge 2011
- title: *Disentangling pleasure from incentive salience and learning signals in brain reward circuitry*
- DOI: `10.1073/PNAS.1101920108`
- contribution: distinct representations of hedonic impact, incentive salience, and learned prediction

### SRC-E-003 — Berridge 2012
- title: *From prediction error to incentive salience: mesolimbic computation of reward motivation*
- DOI: `10.1111/j.1460-9568.2012.07990.x`
- contribution: current physiological/internal state can recompute wanting without changing prior learned value

### SRC-E-004 — Schultz 2016
- title: *Dopamine reward prediction error coding*
- DOI: `10.31887/DCNS.2016.18.1/WSCHULTZ`
- contribution: prediction error as a learning signal distinct from pleasure

### SRC-E-005 — Keramati & Gutkin 2014
- title: *Homeostatic reinforcement learning for integrating reward collection and physiological stability*
- eLife article 04811
- contribution: explicit integration of internal state and reward-guided action

### SRC-E-006 — Oudeyer, Kaplan & Hafner 2007
- title: *Intrinsic Motivation Systems for Autonomous Mental Development*
- DOI: `10.1109/TEVC.2006.890271`
- contribution: learning progress as an intrinsic motivation/attention-allocation signal

## Transfer rule

External sources may justify:
- distinctions;
- test designs;
- candidate mechanisms;
- implementation hypotheses.

They do **not** automatically justify:
- biological equivalence;
- machine phenomenology;
- conscious experience;
- direct copying of biological parameter values.
