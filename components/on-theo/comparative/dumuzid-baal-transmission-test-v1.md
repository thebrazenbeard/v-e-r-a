## Question

Did the Mesopotamian Dumuzid/Tammuz death-return tradition directly generate or transmit the Ugaritic Baal death-return tradition?

This packet deliberately separates:

1. CHRONOLOGICAL_PRIORITY
2. CONTACT_POSSIBILITY
3. STRUCTURAL_PARALLEL
4. SHARED_MYTHOLOGEM
5. DIRECT_TRANSMISSION
6. DIRECTION_OF_TRANSMISSION

Those are different propositions.

## 1. Chronology permits influence but does not prove it

Sumerian and Old Babylonian Dumuzid traditions are earlier than the surviving Ugaritic Baal Cycle tablets.

The current Dumuzid packet already includes:
- Old Babylonian Sumerian death/capture compositions;
- Inana's Descent half-year alternation;
- Dumuzid and Geshtin-ana substitution;
- Dumuzi's Dream;
- later/current manuscript evidence.

The Baal Cycle survives on six Ugaritic tablets written by Ilimilku/Ilimalku.

Modern scholarship dates the tablets within the Late Bronze Age, with discussion between a traditional mid-fourteenth-century placement and arguments for a later thirteenth-century placement.

Primary scholarly edition:
Mark S. Smith, *The Ugaritic Baal Cycle, Volume I* (1994).
https://brill.com/display/title/1061

Therefore:

DUMUZID_TRADITIONS_PRECEDE_SURVIVING_BAAL_TABLETS = TRUE

but:

EARLIER != SOURCE.

## 2. Baal has stronger explicit revival evidence than most Mesopotamian Dumuzid texts

Noga Ayali-Darshan's 2024 synthesis identifies:
- Baal's death in KTU 1.5 V through 1.6 I/II;
- Baal's revival in KTU 1.6 III-IV and related material.

Her broader argument is that actual revival is sparsely attested in Mesopotamian texts, while the Levantine Baal tradition gives much stronger second-millennium evidence for the dying-and-rising mythologem.

Sources:
- https://www.zaphon.de/dying-and-rising-gods/en
- https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.21154991

This reverses a common assumption.

The data do not support simply saying:

DUMUZI = ORIGINAL FULL MODEL
BAAL = COPY.

## 3. Mari creates a real contact field

Ayali-Darshan identifies three Mari documents relevant to Dumuzi death/return:

- A.1146:
  a letter from the reign of Zimri-Lim, first half of the eighteenth century BCE, mentioning Dumuzi's death and return;

- A.4540:
  an economic/ritual record concerning Dumuzi's burial;

- MARI 5, 1987.14:
  a record associated with Dumuzi's return.

This matters because Mari sits in a West Asian / Amorite environment between southern Mesopotamia and later Levantine traditions.

Disposition:

MARI = PLAUSIBLE_CONTACT_FIELD_AND_INTERMEDIATE_ATTESTATION

But:

MARI_ATTESTATION != DOCUMENTED_MARI_TO_UGARIT_TRANSMISSION_CHAIN.

No currently checked evidence identifies:
- a specific Mari tablet copied at Ugarit;
- a named scribe transmitting Dumuzi material to Ilimilku;
- a direct ritual institution carried from Mari into Baal worship;
- a lexical borrowing that proves narrative dependence.

## 4. Narrative mechanics differ materially

### Dumuzid complex

Major V1 elements:
- Inana must provide a substitute;
- galla act as captors;
- Dumuzid is handed over;
- he attempts escape/transformation;
- Geshtin-ana participates in sibling lament;
- the final Inana's Descent tradition gives half-year alternation.

### Baal complex

The Ugaritic cycle instead centers:
- Baal;
- Mot/Death;
- Baal's death/absence;
- Anat and other divine actors;
- Baal's return to life;
- restored rain/fertility/kingship imagery.

The motifs overlap at a high structural level:
- divine death/disappearance;
- underworld/death power;
- lament or divine response;
- renewed presence/return;
- possible seasonal meaning.

But the causal architecture is different.

Control:

STRUCTURAL_PARALLEL != TEXTUAL_DEPENDENCE.

## 5. Seasonal similarity is not enough

The Baal Cycle has long been read through seasonal/rainfall models.

Mark S. Smith's discussion allows meteorological/seasonal interpretation in Baal's disappearance and return, especially where rain imagery accompanies the recognition that Baal lives.

Dumuzid also admits possible seasonal/pastoral interpretation.

But seasonal fit on both sides does not establish borrowing.

The same ecological problem can generate independently:
- absence/presence patterns;
- drought/rain imagery;
- lament/return motifs.

Control:

SHARED_SEASONAL_STRUCTURE != TRANSMISSION.

## 6. Older scholarship allowed Dumuzi → Baal influence as a possibility

Tryggve Mettinger's *The Riddle of Resurrection* treated Dumuzi-Tammuz as historically early and considered possible influence on West Semitic dying/rising traditions, including Baal.

A contemporary BMCR review accurately presents this as a suggestion/possibility rather than a demonstrated documentary chain.

Source:
https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2002/2002.09.07/

That model belongs in the graph as:

POSSIBLE_INFLUENCE_HYPOTHESIS

not:

ESTABLISHED_TRANSMISSION.

## 7. Current scholarship directly challenges the prototype model

Ayali-Darshan 2024 is a major hostile control because the work explicitly addresses the same transmission assumption.

Its stated conclusion:
- Dumuzi has often been treated as the dominant dying/rising prototype;
- the Mesopotamian revival evidence is much thinner than that model assumes;
- the available evidence does not support the standard Dumuzi-prototype account for Baal and later Adonis;
- the stronger second-millennium dying/rising tradition is Levantine, especially Baal.

Therefore:

CURRENT_SCHOLARLY_STATE = CONTESTED_DIRECTION

with a strong modern challenge to:

MESOPOTAMIA -> UGARIT
as the default route.

## 8. Mari evidence complicates simple directional models

Mari is especially important because it is:
- geographically western relative to southern Mesopotamia;
- culturally Amorite / West Asian;
- earlier than surviving Ugaritic tablets;
- explicitly attesting Dumuzi death and return.

Several historical models remain possible:

A. southern Mesopotamian Dumuzi tradition moved west through Mari;
B. Mari reflects a broader West Asian mythologem already shared across regions;
C. Dumuzi absorbed a western return motif;
D. multiple traditions interacted bidirectionally;
E. similarities developed independently within overlapping ritual/ecological systems.

Current evidence does not close among these.

Disposition:

DIRECTION = UNRESOLVED.

## 9. No demonstrated carrier chain yet

For direct transmission, On-Theo requires a carrier.

Candidate carrier classes:
- bilingual scribes;
- court scholars;
- itinerant ritual specialists;
- merchants and diplomatic networks;
- royal intermarriage;
- temple personnel;
- written school-text transmission.

But V1 has not identified a specific chain:

DUMUZID_TEXT_OR_RITUAL
-> CARRIER
-> INTERMEDIATE
-> UGARITIC_BAAL_TEXT.

Therefore direct lineage remains:

NONE_ESTABLISHED.

## 10. Do not infer borrowing from the label "dying and rising god"

The category itself is historiographically unstable.

If two figures are first compressed into the same modern category and then that shared category is used as evidence of transmission, the reasoning is circular.

Correct order:

1. reconstruct Dumuzid independently;
2. reconstruct Baal independently;
3. compare proposition by proposition;
4. establish chronology/contact;
5. seek carriers and directional evidence;
6. only then evaluate transmission.

## 11. Controlled comparison matrix

### Dumuzid
- captor: galla;
- causal mechanism: substitution/hand-over;
- death-realm term: kur / Arali in different compositions;
- sibling role: Geshtin-ana hides, laments, and in one tradition alternates;
- temporal structure: explicit half-year alternation in Inana's Descent;
- revival status: contested and sparsely attested outside narrow evidence.

### Baal
- antagonist: Mot;
- causal mechanism: divine conflict/death;
- return: strongly represented in Ugaritic cycle;
- seasonal/rain imagery: materially important;
- sister/ally role: Anat, but not the same substitution structure;
- half-year sibling alternation: not the Dumuzid mechanism.

Similarity level:
STRUCTURAL / THEMATIC.

Identity level:
NOT_IDENTICAL.
