# Qur'an / Late Antique Feature Tests V1

Status: `COMPARATIVE HYPOTHESIS MATRIX / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Purpose

This is the first comparison packet permitted after the Qur'an was reconstructed internally in PR #16.

The unit of comparison is a **specific feature**, not a civilization-sized claim such as `Islam came from Judaism`, `Islam copied Christianity`, or `the Qur'an is Zoroastrian`.

Each test asks:

1. Is the candidate antecedent demonstrably earlier?
2. Is the semantic/narrative resemblance specific enough to matter?
3. Was the candidate tradition historically available in the Qur'an's contact environment?
4. Is there a plausible carrier?
5. Are closer competing antecedents available?
6. Does the Qur'an preserve, transform, contest, or merely resemble the earlier feature?
7. What would falsify or weaken the proposed connection?

## Test 1 — Resurrection and final judgment

### Qur'anic target

`CLM-QURAN-BODILY-RESURRECTION`

The Qur'an explicitly argues for restoration of bodily remains (Q 75:3-4) and repeatedly presents resurrection, judgment and differentiated afterlife destinies.

### Earlier comparanda

- Daniel 12:2-3 (`CLM-DANIEL12-RESURRECTION`) gives secure pre-Qur'anic Jewish evidence for awakening from dust into differentiated enduring destinies.
- Jewish and Christian Late Antique traditions develop resurrection/judgment extensively.
- Iranian/Zoroastrian traditions also contain judgment, cosmic restoration and final defeat/separation of evil, although the dating of particular systematic forms matters enormously.

### Result

`SHARED_LATE_ANTIQUE_ESCHATOLOGICAL_FIELD`

Confidence: **high** for historical availability of resurrection/judgment traditions; **unresolved** for any single direct source.

### Why not direct dependence yet?

The motif is too widely distributed across Jewish, Christian and Iranian systems by Late Antiquity. A claim such as `Qur'anic resurrection derives specifically from Zoroastrianism` requires finer lexical/narrative evidence and a carrier model capable of excluding nearer Jewish/Christian routes.

### Falsification pressure

A specific-source model weakens if its allegedly diagnostic features are absent from the Qur'an, postdate the Qur'an, or are equally common in closer Arabian/Jewish/Christian sources.

## Test 2 — Rahman(an) / al-Rahman

### Qur'anic target

Qur'anic divine naming includes `al-Rahman` alongside `Allah`.

### Earlier comparandum

Late Antique South Arabian inscriptions use `Rahman(an)` before Islam. Sigrid Kjaer traces its use across changing South Arabian monotheist contexts, including Jewish/Judaizing and Christian political phases.

Registered concept:
`CON-SOUTH-ARABIAN-RAHMANAN`

### Result

`PRE_QURANIC_LEXICAL_ATTESTATION + CONTACT_ENVIRONMENT_SHARED`

Confidence: **high** that the divine epithet predates the Qur'an in South Arabia; **unresolved** for the exact route by which Qur'anic `al-Rahman` relates to those uses.

### Critical guard

Pre-Islamic occurrence proves historical availability, not one timeless theology and not automatic direct descent.

Possible models include:
- broader Semitic divine epithet continuity;
- South Arabian influence;
- Jewish/Christian biblicalizing language;
- Arabian reuse of a regionally familiar monotheist title;
- combinations of these.

The project does not choose among them yet.

## Test 3 — Scripture, confirmation and prophetic history

### Qur'anic target

`CLM-QURAN-PROPHETIC-CONTINUUM`
`CLM-QURAN-CONFIRMS-CRITERION-PRIOR-SCRIPTURE`

The Qur'an presents itself inside a history of revelation while simultaneously confirming, reframing and adjudicating claims about earlier scripture.

### Earlier environment

By Late Antiquity, Jewish and Christian communities had centuries of scriptural commentary, rewritten narrative, typology, homily, liturgy, apocrypha and polemic.

Angelika Neuwirth argues that the Qur'an's self-presentation as scripture adapts earlier scriptural self-conceptions within a pre-Islamic Arabian conceptual setting, and treats Qur'anic prophetic discourse as an active conversation with earlier traditions rather than a passive reproduction of a book.

### Result

`SCRIPTURAL_DISCOURSE_CONTINUITY / QURANIC_REFRAMING`

Confidence: **high as a broad scholarly model**, but direct literary source remains **passage-specific**.

### Guard

A Qur'anic biblical narrative need not come directly from the canonical Bible. Potential carriers include:
- liturgy;
- homily;
- rewritten scripture;
- oral storytelling;
- Jewish/Christian exegesis;
- apocrypha;
- debate and polemic.

But `oral circulation` is not allowed to become an unfalsifiable placeholder: each strong source claim still needs chronology and carrier evidence.

## Test 4 — Fasting and predecessor-practice framing

### Qur'anic target

`CLM-QURAN-FASTING-PRESCRIBED-WITH-PREDECESSOR-FRAME`

Q 2:183 explicitly says fasting is prescribed for the believers as it was prescribed for those before them.

### Comparative significance

Unlike many modern source hypotheses, the text itself marks fasting as having predecessors.

Jewish and Christian fasting traditions are unquestionably available historically, while Arabian practice also requires investigation.

### Result

`TEXT_ACKNOWLEDGED_PREDECESSOR_PRACTICE / SPECIFIC_LINEAGE_UNKNOWN`

Confidence: **high** that the Qur'an deliberately situates fasting in a predecessor tradition; **unresolved** as to which concrete ritual lineage best explains Ramadan's specific system.

### Next evidence required

- dated Jewish fast practice;
- Syriac/Arabian Christian fasting calendars;
- pre-Islamic Arabian fasting evidence;
- chronology of Q 2:183-187;
- lexical/ritual comparison beyond the generic act of abstention.

## Test 5 — Qibla, sanctuary and Abrahamization

### Qur'anic target

Q 2 contains prayer-direction language and increasingly central Abraham/House/Meccan sanctuary framing.

Neuwirth's communal-formation model treats the Medinan stage as including a renewed focus on Mecca after an earlier orientation toward Jerusalem.

### Earlier environment

Sacred direction, sanctuary, pilgrimage and ancestral-foundation narratives are widespread in Jewish, Christian and Arabian religious systems.

### Result

`COMMUNAL_RITUAL_REORIENTATION / MULTIPLE_POSSIBLE_ANTECEDENTS`

Confidence: **moderate-high** for Qur'anic internal communal-development interpretation; **unresolved** for precise pre-Qur'anic history of each Meccan rite.

### Guard

Later sira reports about pre-Islamic Mecca and the chronology of qibla change are later evidence and must not silently become Qur'anic primary evidence.

The strongest future test is archaeological/epigraphic plus early external textual evidence for the sanctuary and its ritual network.

## Test 6 — Qur'anic law and Late Antique gentile law

### Qur'anic target

Food, sexual, purity and community-boundary regulations, especially Q 4-6.

### Scholarly model

Holger M. Zellentin's *Law Beyond Israel* argues a much more specific continuity than generic `Abrahamic influence`: biblical law for non-Israelites develops through Second Temple, Christian and rabbinic trajectories, and the Qur'an reformulates traditional Arabian religious practice in close continuity with—and sometimes critical distance from—Late Antique Jewish and especially Christian gentile law.

This is a serious, feature-level transmission model because it compares concrete legal structures rather than motifs alone.

### Result

`SCHOLARLY_TRANSMISSION_MODEL / STRONGER_THAN_GENERIC_PARALLEL / NOT_YET_PROJECT-PROMOTED`

Confidence: **substantial scholarly support requiring exact chapter-by-chapter hostile review**.

The project records Zellentin's claim; it does not silently convert the entire book's conclusion into a canonical On-Theo fact.

### High-value cases

- blood/food prohibitions: Genesis 9 / Leviticus 17 -> Acts/gentile law -> Q 5:3 and Q 6:145-146;
- sexual prohibitions: Leviticus 18 trajectories -> Qur'anic `fahisha` / `zina`;
- incest law: Leviticus 18:6-18 -> Q 4:22-23;
- purity/punishment structures.

Each can become a separate adversarial packet.

## Test 7 — Adam, Iblis and jinn

### Qur'anic target

Adam/prostration cycles, Iblis, shayatin and morally diverse jinn.

### Candidate environments

Possible comparison fields include:
- Genesis Adam traditions;
- Enochic rebellious-heavenly-being traditions;
- Jewish/Christian Satan/adversary developments;
- Syriac Adam literature;
- Arabian spirit/jinn traditions.

### Result

`RESEARCH_FRONTIER / NO TRANSMISSION EDGE YET`

The current corpus is not yet source-controlled enough to choose a lineage. In particular:

- Qur'anic Iblis is explicitly `of the jinn` in Q 18:50;
- Enochic Watchers are a different agent class;
- later Christian Satan/Iblis equivalence cannot be assumed;
- Arabian jinn evidence needs its own pre-Qur'anic primary-source packet.

This test is intentionally left unresolved.

## Test 8 — Monotheism and anti-association polemic

### Qur'anic target

Q 112, Q 6:101, Q 42:11 and many other passages insist on divine uniqueness and reject association/offspring claims.

### Earlier environment

By the sixth/seventh century:
- Jewish monotheisms;
- multiple Christian Trinitarian/Christological systems;
- South Arabian Jewish/Christian monotheisms;
- Iranian religious systems;
- surviving Arabian polytheisms
all existed in overlapping contact zones.

### Result

`LATE_ANTIQUE_MONOTHEIST_DEBATE_CONTEXT / QURANIC_POSITION DISTINCT`

The Qur'an clearly participates in monotheist debate, but `monotheism` is far too broad to identify a source tradition.

Its specific polemical targets must be reconstructed per passage rather than treating one council or one Christian denomination as the presumed interlocutor.

## Current relationship matrix

| Qur'anic feature | Earlier evidence | Current relation | Direct dependence? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resurrection/judgment | Danielic + later Jewish/Christian + Iranian | shared eschatological field | not established |
| `al-Rahman` | South Arabian Rahman(an) | pre-Qur'anic lexical availability | route unresolved |
| Scripture/prophets | Jewish/Christian scriptural cultures | historical-discursive continuity | passage-specific |
| Fasting | Jewish/Christian + possible Arabian practice | predecessor explicitly acknowledged | lineage unresolved |
| Qibla/sanctuary | Jerusalem/sanctuary traditions + Arabian sacred geography | ritual-boundary development | unresolved |
| Food/sexual law | biblical -> Jewish/Christian gentile-law traditions | strong scholarly transmission model (Zellentin) | under hostile review |
| Adam/Iblis/jinn | biblical/Enochic/Syriac/Arabian fields | research frontier | none assigned |
| Monotheist polemic | Jewish/Christian/South Arabian/Arabian field | shared debate environment | no single source |

## What this matrix rules out

At V1 the evidence does **not** justify any of these global statements:

- `the Qur'an is copied from the Bible`;
- `Islam came from Zoroastrianism`;
- `Muhammad learned Islam from Syriac Christians`;
- `Qur'anic ritual is simply borrowed Judaism`;
- `Arabian paganism explains the Qur'an without Jewish/Christian contact`;
- `Late Antique parallels are irrelevant because revelation is claimed`.

The historical project can investigate human textual/cultural transmission without deciding theological claims about revelation.
