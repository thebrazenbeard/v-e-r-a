## Purpose

The previous cut established direct Dumuzi-specific scholastic exposure at Ugarit through RS 20.123+ / P332950.

This packet asks whether that artifact belongs to the same physical scribal archive as Ilimilku's Baal Cycle tablets.

It does not.

Core rule:

SAME CITY AND SCRIBAL CULTURE != SAME ARCHIVE OR NAMED-CARRIER ACCESS.

## 1. RS 20.123+ belongs to the Rapanu archive context

Published Ugarit source indexes place:

RS 20.123+ / Ugaritica 5, 137

in:

Quartier Residentiel
House of Rapanu
room 5.

The same source classifies it as a lexical / scribal text.

The House of Rapanu archive contained a large multilingual body of tablets and is interpreted in modern scholarship as functioning in part as an edubba-like scribal-training environment.

Disposition:

P332950_ARCHIVE_CONTEXT =
HOUSE_OF_RAPANU / ROOM_5 / SCRIBAL_TRAINING_ENVIRONMENT.

## 2. The House of Rapanu was a major scribal archive

The House of Rapanu in Ugarit's residential quarter yielded hundreds of tablets.

Modern archaeological synthesis describes Rapanu as:
- a high-ranking scribe;
- an adviser connected to the royal administration;
- associated with diplomatic and sensitive state business.

Functional study of the archive points to:
- Ugaritic and Akkadian use;
- alphabetic scribal exercises;
- Akkadian syllabary/lexical exercises;
- copying and training activity.

This makes RS 20.123+'s Dumuzi row part of a genuine learned scribal environment rather than an isolated imported curiosity.

## 3. Baal Cycle tablets come from a different archive

The Baal Cycle tablets KTU 1.1-1.6 are assigned by current Ugaritic corpus work to:

Ras Shamra Acropolis
House of the High Priest.

KTU 1.6:
- RS 2.[009] + RS 5.155;
- Louvre AO 16636;
- found in the House of the High Priest;
- preserves Ilimilku's full scribal colophon.

Thus:

DUMUZI_LEXICAL_WITNESS_ARCHIVE
!=
BAAL_CYCLE_ARCHIVE.

## 4. Physical distance does not equal intellectual isolation

The two archives belonged to the same city and broader late Ugaritic scribal culture.

The city had:
- multilingual scribes;
- Mesopotamian lexical traditions;
- Akkadian literary texts;
- institutional movement between court, temple, and private learned households.

Therefore cross-archive knowledge circulation is historically plausible.

But:

PLAUSIBLE_SCRIBAL_NETWORK
!=
DOCUMENTED_TABLET_TRANSFER.

V1 has no direct evidence that RS 20.123+:
- was borrowed by Ilimilku;
- was copied in the House of the High Priest;
- was read by Ilimilku;
- supplied any Baal Cycle line.
