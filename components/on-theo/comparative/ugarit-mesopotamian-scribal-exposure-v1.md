## Question

Was the scribal environment at Ugarit exposed to Akkadian and Mesopotamian literary/school traditions strongly enough that cross-cultural transmission into Ugaritic literature was historically possible?

This packet does not ask whether Dumuzid specifically caused the Baal Cycle.

It asks whether the general carrier environment existed.

## 1. Akkadian literary material is physically attested at Ugarit

CDLI currently catalogs multiple Middle Babylonian Akkadian Gilgamesh tablets excavated at Ugarit / Ras Shamra.

Examples include:
- RS 94.2066 / CDLI P500683 — Literary (Gilgamesh MB Ugarit 1);
- RS 94.2082 / P500685 — Literary (Gilgamesh MB Ugarit 3);
- RS 94.2083 / P500686 — Literary (Gilgamesh MB Ugarit 4).

These are physical Akkadian literary witnesses from Ugarit, not merely later claims of cultural influence.

Disposition:

MESOPOTAMIAN_LITERARY_TEXTS_AT_UGARIT = MATERIAL_EVIDENCE_HIGH.

## 2. Scribal schooling used cuneiform lexical traditions

ORACC's Digital Corpus of Cuneiform Lexical Texts includes Middle Babylonian lexical and school materials from Ugarit.

Examples:
- Ras Shamra-Ougarit 7, 052 / CDLI P347890;
- Ras Shamra-Ougarit 7, 062 / P347900;
- other Ugarit lexical/sign exercises.

This is direct evidence that cuneiform scholastic traditions operated locally.

Disposition:

AKKADIAN_CUNEIFORM_SCHOOLING_AT_UGARIT = HIGH.

## 3. Ugarit was multilingual and scribally cosmopolitan

Modern introductions to Ugaritic culture emphasize that Ugarit preserves:
- alphabetic Ugaritic writing;
- Akkadian;
- Hurrian;
- lexical lists;
- school exercises;
- Mesopotamian literary fragments.

Schniedewind and Hunt explicitly characterize Ugarit as a major scribal-training center in the Levant and note the presence of a Gilgamesh fragment and multilingual lexicographic material.

This is a scholarly synthesis supported by the material corpus.

## 4. General Mesopotamian influence is historically plausible

The presence of:
- Akkadian literary tablets;
- cuneiform school texts;
- lexical lists;
- multilingual scribal training

establishes a real mechanism by which Mesopotamian literary forms and concepts could circulate at Ugarit.

Therefore:

MESOPOTAMIAN_TO_UGARIT_LITERARY_CONTACT = PLAUSIBLE_AND_MATERIALLY_GROUNDED.

This is stronger than mere geographic proximity.

## 5. Gilgamesh demonstrates actual literary import, not just administrative Akkadian

The Gilgamesh tablets are especially important because they show that Akkadian at Ugarit was not restricted to:
- diplomacy;
- contracts;
- administrative records.

Mesopotamian literary narrative itself was present.

That raises the prior probability that Ugaritic scribes or scribal institutions could encounter imported literary material.

But:

GILGAMESH_AT_UGARIT
!=
DUMUZID_TEXT_AT_UGARIT.

## 6. No Dumuzid/Tammuz source has yet been identified in the Ugarit corpus

V1 has not identified:
- an Akkadian Dumuzi text excavated at Ugarit;
- a Sumerian Dumuzid text excavated at Ugarit;
- a lexical exercise naming Dumuzi in a relevant transmission context;
- a tablet owned/copied by Ilimilku containing Dumuzi material;
- a colophon linking Ilimilku to Mesopotamian Dumuzi literature.

Therefore:

SPECIFIC_DUMUZID_SOURCE_EXPOSURE_AT_UGARIT = NOT_ESTABLISHED.

## 7. Ilimilku-specific exposure remains unresolved

The general scribal environment was capable of Mesopotamian contact.

But the following stronger chain is not yet demonstrated:

MESOPOTAMIAN_TEXT
-> ILIMILKU_OR_HIS_SCHOOL
-> BAAL_CYCLE.

A named scribe living in a multilingual literary center is a plausible carrier candidate.

He is not thereby a proven carrier.

## 8. Scribal curriculum and literary adaptation

Modern work on ancient scribal education argues that cuneiform school traditions influenced alphabetic scribal training in the Levant.

At Ugarit, alphabetic and cuneiform systems coexisted.

This makes several forms of transmission plausible:
- direct reading;
- oral explanation;
- bilingual teaching;
- adaptation of narrative structures;
- shared professional scribal knowledge.

It does not tell us which mechanism, if any, affected the Baal Cycle.

## 9. Evidence architecture

Strong:
- physical Akkadian Gilgamesh tablets at Ugarit;
- cuneiform lexical/school texts at Ugarit;
- multilingual scribal environment.

Moderate:
- inference that Ugaritic literary scribes could access Mesopotamian literary concepts.

Unresolved:
- Ilimilku personally read a specific Mesopotamian literary tablet;
- Dumuzid/Tammuz material was in his curriculum;
- Baal death/return derives from that material.
