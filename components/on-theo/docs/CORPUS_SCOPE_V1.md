# On-Theo Broad Mythos Corpus V1

Status: `DRAFT / CORPUS EXPANSION / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Purpose

On-Theo is not a Jesus-only project. Jesus, Christian origins, and associated countertraditions form one deep subproject inside a larger comparative-religion corpus.

The broader corpus reconstructs religious systems on their own terms before comparing them. Comparison must never flatten a tradition into a static package or infer borrowing from resemblance alone.

## Governing order

For every tradition or historical phase:

1. reconstruct the tradition internally;
2. establish chronology and source strata;
3. identify textual, material, ritual, and scholarly evidence separately;
4. record internal variation and historical development;
5. only then compare with neighboring traditions;
6. classify each proposed relationship as inheritance, direct dependence, shared environment, polemical reversal, independent convergence, later harmonization, structural parallel, or `NONE_ESTABLISHED`.

## Standard mythos axes

Each mature tradition packet should eventually address, where evidence exists:

- cosmology and cosmogony;
- divine hierarchy / divine council / monotheism / dualism;
- chaos, evil, adversarial beings, and theodicy;
- human origins and anthropology;
- kingship, priesthood, prophecy, revelation, and sacred authority;
- law, ethics, purity, sacrifice, ritual, and cult;
- sacred geography and temples;
- death, underworld, resurrection, judgment, and final restoration;
- messianic, savior, redeemer, or culture-hero figures;
- apocalypse and eschatology;
- angels, demons, spirits, ancestors, and intermediary beings;
- gendered divine roles and divine consorts where applicable;
- textual canon and noncanonical traditions;
- sectarian divergence and reform movements;
- political setting and imperial context;
- transmission history and later reinterpretation.

## Diachronic rule

Names such as `Judaism`, `Christianity`, `Islam`, `Zoroastrianism`, or `Canaanite religion` are research labels, not timeless essences.

Examples of necessary internal slicing include:

- Canaanite / Ugaritic Late Bronze Age systems;
- early Israelite and Judahite religion;
- monarchic Yahwism;
- exilic / post-exilic developments;
- Second Temple Judaisms;
- rabbinic Judaism;
- earliest Jesus movement and Pauline communities;
- second-century Christian plurality;
- patristic and conciliar Christianity;
- Qur'anic religion in Late Antiquity;
- early Islamic exegetical and hadith development;
- Old Avestan / Gathic material;
- Younger Avestan religion;
- Sasanian and Pahlavi Zoroastrian systematization.

A later systematic theology must not be silently projected backward into an earlier source stratum.

## Evidence classes

Every substantive claim retains the project classes:

`PRIMARY_TEXT`
`MATERIAL_EVIDENCE`
`HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION`
`LATER_TRADITION`
`SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`
`PROJECT_INFERENCE`
`SPECULATIVE_MODEL`
`UNKNOWN`

The same document may be primary evidence for what it says and later tradition relative to an earlier event.

## Comparison rules

A similarity alone is not transmission evidence.

Before a direct-influence claim is promoted, test:

- chronological possibility;
- geographic/social contact;
- linguistic or textual pathway;
- intermediary traditions;
- whether the compared feature is actually unusual enough to need borrowing;
- whether the proposed source feature predates the target feature;
- whether internal development explains the target adequately;
- whether the comparison depends on late witnesses for an allegedly early idea.

`STRUCTURAL_PARALLEL` is the default when resemblance is real but lineage is not established.

## V1 corpus priority

The first broad expansion proceeds outward from the ancient Near East and eastern Mediterranean because these traditions directly condition the later Abrahamic materials already present in the repository.

Initial sequence:

1. Canaanite / Ugaritic religion;
2. Mesopotamian religion and Babylonian creation theology;
3. ancient Israelite / Judahite religion;
4. Zoroastrian / Iranian religious development;
5. Second Temple Jewish plurality;
6. Greco-Roman religion and philosophy;
7. earliest Christianity and competing Christianities;
8. rabbinic Judaism;
9. Late Antique Arabian, Jewish, Christian, Iranian, and other contexts for Qur'anic religion;
10. early Islamic development;
11. later comparative expansion beyond the Near Eastern / Mediterranean family where research questions justify it.

## Current boundary

The Panthera / paternity research remains valid and continues as a Jesus subproject, but it is not the organizing center of the corpus.

This document authorizes no merge and makes no tradition-level truth claim by itself.
