# Research Method

## Objective

`on-theo` is not an apologetics repository and not an anti-religion repository. Its job is to preserve competing traditions accurately enough that similarities, contradictions, transmission paths, and speculative reinterpretations can be examined without laundering one category into another.

## Evidence ladder

Every consequential claim should be identifiable as one or more of:

- `PRIMARY_TEXT`: direct content of an identified ancient or medieval source.
- `MATERIAL_EVIDENCE`: archaeology, inscriptions, manuscripts, coins, physical remains.
- `HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION`: inference about historical people/events from available evidence.
- `LATER_TRADITION`: a later community's account of earlier people/events.
- `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`: attributed academic analysis; disagreement must remain visible.
- `PROJECT_INFERENCE`: our reasoning from cited material.
- `SPECULATIVE_MODEL`: counterfactual/metaphysical/systems analogy not asserted as history.
- `UNKNOWN`: not presently recoverable or evidence is insufficient.

## Date discipline

Keep at least three dates separate whenever they differ:

1. `claimed_event_date`: when the story says an event happened.
2. `source_date`: when the surviving source was likely composed/redacted.
3. `witness/manuscript_date`: date of the surviving physical textual witness where relevant.

A seventh-century source discussing a first-century event is not silently presented as a first-century witness. A second-century polemic may preserve earlier rumor, but that possibility is an inference, not automatic provenance.

## Tradition discipline

A tradition may be documented on its own terms without being treated as historically verified.

Examples:

- `Christian tradition: virgin conception` is a valid description of a Christian textual claim.
- `Historical fact: virgin conception` requires evidence of a different kind and is not established merely by canonical status.
- `Jewish/anti-Christian counter-tradition: Pandera/Panthera paternity` records a counter-claim; it does not establish Panthera as the biological father.
- `Qur'anic account: crucifixion only appeared so` describes Qur'an 4:157; it is a seventh-century theological claim about a first-century event, not an independent contemporary execution record.

## Similarity discipline

Classify parallels as:

- `DIRECT_DEPENDENCE_POSSIBLE`: a later source could plausibly know the earlier tradition.
- `SHARED_TRADITION`: both may derive from a common textual/oral environment.
- `STRUCTURAL_PARALLEL`: similar conceptual architecture without an established transmission path.
- `PROJECT_ANALOGY`: modern mapping imposed for exploratory purposes.

Similarity alone does not prove borrowing, common authorship, common supernatural origin, or historical accuracy.

## Systems-language translation rule

The project may translate ancient symbols into modern systems language when useful, but translations must be two-column in spirit:

- **Ancient/source concept**: preserve the source's own semantic range.
- **Modern analogy**: environment, runtime, controller, root authority, agent, permission, information channel, checkpoint, re-instantiation, evaluation, etc.

Never back-project the analogy as ancient technical knowledge. "Word" may be compared with executable command; that does not mean an ancient author meant software.

## Historical Jesus rule

For Yeshua/Jesus research, distinguish:

- high-confidence contextual facts (first-century Jewish Galilee/Judea under Roman power; Jewish setting; execution by Roman crucifixion);
- source-attested claims (teachings/events reported in particular texts);
- historical reconstructions (apocalyptic prophet, healer, teacher, messianic claimant, etc.);
- later doctrinal development;
- project hypotheses (social stigma from disputed paternity, missing-years introspection, substrate skepticism, simulation analogy).

The undocumented years are not evidence for any particular undocumented activity.

## Language rule

Names and translations are indexed, not weaponized. `Yeshua`, `Iesous`, `Jesus`, `Mashiach`, `Christos`, `Messiah`, `Maryam`, `Miriam`, etc. may refer to historically related terms/entities while carrying different linguistic and theological histories. Preserve those histories where they matter.

## Citation preference

Prefer, roughly:

1. primary-text repositories / critical editions;
2. peer-reviewed academic scholarship and university-press reference works;
3. museum / archaeological institutional records;
4. high-quality educational summaries for orientation;
5. popular treatments only when explicitly labeled.

Do not cite a modern summary as though it were the ancient source it summarizes.
