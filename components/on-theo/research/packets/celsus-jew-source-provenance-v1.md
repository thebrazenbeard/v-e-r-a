## Research question

How much provenance can responsibly be assigned to the anti-Christian material that Origen presents as Celsus's Jewish speaker? Specifically, does the evidence establish a single independent Jewish written source behind the Panthera/virgin-birth counter-story, or is that one scholarly reconstruction among competing models?

This matters because the historical weight of the Panthera accusation changes depending on whether it is:

1. Celsus's own literary/polemical construction;
2. a literary persona built partly from Jewish arguments;
3. material drawn from one or more oral Jewish interlocutors/traditions;
4. material copied from a recoverable independent Jewish written source.

Those are different provenance propositions and must not be collapsed.

## 1. Primary-source constraint: Origen preserves Celsus, not the hypothetical Jewish source

The registered preservation chain remains:

`hypothetical Jewish input -> Celsus's lost True Doctrine -> Origen's Contra Celsum -> modern editions/translations`.

Only the latter two stages are directly represented in the current source registry. A distinct mid-second-century Jewish tract is not extant in the current evidence set.

Origen himself repeatedly comments on the literary appropriateness of Celsus's Jewish speaker. At the opening of *Contra Celsum* II, Origen questions Celsus's choices in constructing the Jew's address. Elsewhere, including II.28, Origen complains that Celsus puts an objection into the Jew's mouth that a Jew would not naturally make.

Checked primary access:
- Origen, *Contra Celsum* II.1: https://ccel.org/ccel/origen/against_celsus/anf04.vi.ix.ii.i.html
- Origen, *Contra Celsum* II.28 excerpt/context: https://www.ccjr.us/dialogika-resources/primary-texts-from-the-history-of-the-relationship/origen-celsus

Safe proposition:

`ORIGEN_TREATS_AT_LEAST_SOME_CELSUS_JEW_SPEECH_AS_LITERARILY_INAPPROPRIATE_FOR_A_JEW`.

That does not prove fabrication. It establishes that the extant preserving author himself noticed tension between the persona and his expectations about Jewish argument.

## 2. The scholarship is genuinely divided

### Authentic-Jewish-input side

Lincoln Blumell (2007) argues that Celsus likely preserves authentic second-century Jewish anti-Christian arguments. Importantly, Blumell also surveys the opposing literature rather than presenting authenticity as uncontested.

Maren Niehoff (2013) advances a stronger thesis: the fragments assigned to Celsus's Jew derive from a written Alexandrian Jewish work from the mid-second century.

James Carleton Paget (2017) argues that several features can support authenticity and stresses the value of counter-narrative Jewish-Christian polemic, while discussing the genuineness problem explicitly.

Checked records:
- Blumell 2007: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/000842980703600206
- Niehoff 2013: https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/a-jewish-critique-of-christianity-from-second-century-alexandria-/
- Paget 2017: https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/zac-2017-0015/html

### Skeptical / limiting side

Blumell's literature review explicitly identifies Jeffrey W. Hargis (1999, pp. 36-39) and Joseph W. Trigg (1998, p. 58) as recent scholarship contesting the authenticity of Celsus's Jewish arguments.

Hargis's larger study treats Celsus as a rhetorically strategic anti-Christian polemicist rather than a neutral recorder. Trigg's *Origen* likewise belongs to the skeptical side of the authenticity discussion as reported by Blumell.

Checked bibliographic records:
- Jeffrey W. Hargis, *Against the Christians: The Rise of Early Anti-Christian Polemic* (Peter Lang, 1999): https://www.peterlang.com/document/1054395
- Joseph W. Trigg, *Origen* (Routledge, 1998): https://www.routledge.com/Origen/Trigg/p/book/9780415118354

## 3. The strongest Niehoff thesis is not the same as generic authenticity

Several propositions must stay separate:

- `some arguments attributed to Celsus's Jew may reflect authentic Jewish polemic`;
- `Celsus used Jewish informants or traditions`;
- `Celsus used one identifiable written Jewish source`;
- `that written source was Alexandrian`;
- `that source can be dated to the mid-second century`;
- `the Panthera accusation specifically came from that source`.

Niehoff argues a strong version connecting the fragments to a written Alexandrian Jewish source. The current project evidence does not independently establish each step of that reconstruction.

Therefore the existing claim `CLM-CELSUS-JEW-PRESERVES-AUTHENTIC-JEWISH-SOURCE` should remain `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`, unresolved and contested.

## 4. Celsus's Jew may be a literary persona even if authentic Jewish material lies behind it

The binary question `real Jew or fake Jew?` is too coarse.

A literary persona can incorporate:

- arguments Celsus heard from Jews;
- written Jewish material;
- gospel material known to Celsus;
- Greek literary/mythological comparisons supplied by Celsus;
- rhetorical rearrangement or amplification.

Thus evidence that some arguments are recognizably Jewish does not prove that every line assigned to the speaker comes from an independent Jewish source. Conversely, evidence that the persona contains Greek/pagan rhetorical material does not prove that no Jewish material is present.

The project should therefore model source mixture rather than force a single-source identity prematurely.

## 5. Why the Panthera accusation needs its own provenance claim

Even if an authentic Jewish substrate behind Celsus's Jew is judged plausible, a further proposition remains:

`THE_PANTHERA_PATERNITY_ACCUSATION_SPECIFICALLY_DERIVES_FROM_PREEXISTING_JEWISH_TRADITION`.

The current evidence set does not establish that proposition directly.

Origen reports Celsus's Jewish speaker making the accusation. That is secure textual occurrence at the Origen/Celsus preservation layer. The move from that fact to `therefore a Jewish community already transmitted this specific story independently of Celsus` is a source-provenance inference.

The inference may be plausible under some models, but it is not the same proposition as the textual occurrence.

## 6. Implications for dating

Even if Celsus drew on authentic Jewish material, its date is not automatically first-century.

A mid-second-century Jewish source, an oral tradition circulating near Celsus's time, or a Celsian literary construction all remain chronologically later than Jesus's lifetime. None by itself establishes a first-century Nazareth paternity rumor.

The project must continue to preserve:

`event date != source-input date != Celsus composition date != Origen preservation date != manuscript witness date`.
