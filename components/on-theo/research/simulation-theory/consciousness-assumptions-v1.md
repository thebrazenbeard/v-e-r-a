# Consciousness Assumptions in Simulation Theory V1

Status: `ASSUMPTION LEDGER / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Purpose

Many simulation arguments count simulated agents as observers. That only works if the simulated agents actually instantiate whatever kind of consciousness or observerhood the argument requires.

This packet therefore treats consciousness as a load-bearing assumption rather than background decoration.

## 1. Bostrom's stated assumption

Bostrom explicitly assumes a form of substrate independence.

Source:
- Nick Bostrom, `Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?`
- https://simulation-argument.com/simulation/

Bostrom does not need the strongest possible doctrine that every behaviorally equivalent program must be conscious. His argument requires at least that sufficiently detailed computational replication of the relevant human-brain processes could instantiate subjective experience.

Project classification:
- `CONSCIOUSNESS_ASSUMPTION`
- `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`
- status: `EXPLICITLY_ASSUMED / CONTESTED`

## 2. Computational functionalism

A broad computational-functionalist family holds that the right functional/computational organization is sufficient for mental states and, on many versions, consciousness.

If true in the relevant sense, simulated brains could instantiate conscious persons even on a non-biological substrate.

If false, a simulation might reproduce behavior without producing phenomenal experience.

Simulation-theory consequence:

Observer-number arguments depend on the difference between:

- simulated behavior;
- simulated cognition;
- phenomenal consciousness;
- moral patienthood;
- reference-class membership.

These are not automatically identical.

## 3. Substrate independence

`Substrate independence` can mean several strengths:

### Weak form

At least some non-biological physical substrates could instantiate human-like consciousness if the right causal organization is realized.

### Stronger computational form

Any sufficiently faithful implementation of the relevant computation realizes the same conscious state regardless of physical substrate.

### Implementation-insensitive form

Abstract computational equivalence alone is sufficient even where causal/physical realization differs substantially.

The simulation argument needs something like the first or second form, not necessarily the third.

V1 does not assume any form is settled science.

## 4. Behavioral equivalence != phenomenal equivalence

A simulated agent might:

- report pain;
- claim consciousness;
- behave indistinguishably from a human;
- pass every behavioral test;

while a critic could still deny that phenomenal experience is present.

Conversely, functionalists may argue that sufficiently rich functional equivalence is exactly what warrants consciousness attribution.

On-Theo should preserve the disagreement rather than pick a consciousness theory merely because one is convenient for simulation counting.

## 5. Fine-grained brain simulation

Bostrom's weaker assumption focuses on structural replication of brain computation at sufficiently fine resolution.

But `sufficiently fine` is itself theory-dependent.

Possible relevant levels include:

- neurons and spikes;
- synaptic dynamics;
- dendritic computation;
- neurotransmitter/neuromodulator systems;
- glial interactions;
- molecular/biochemical states;
- electromagnetic or other field-level effects;
- quantum effects, if any are functionally necessary;
- embodied and environmental coupling.

Current neuroscience does not establish that all phenomenally relevant properties have been identified at one computational abstraction level.

Therefore:

`we can simulate neuron-level behavior` != `we have proven conscious instantiation`.

## 6. Multiple realization and copy counting

Suppose one conscious computational pattern is instantiated twice.

Possible views include:

- there are two conscious observers;
- there is one abstract observer with two implementations;
- observer counting should weight physical/computational resources;
- observer-moments rather than persons are the correct unit;
- identity questions are ill-posed while experience tokens remain distinct.

Bostrom-style population reasoning usually assumes multiple implementations contribute multiple observers/observer-moments.

That assumption should be visible in the graph.

## 7. Nested simulations and consciousness duplication

If simulated persons run simulations of conscious persons, the observer population may grow across levels.

But two questions must remain separate:

1. Does each nested implementation instantiate new conscious experience?
2. Does the physical substrate ultimately support the required computations without hidden double counting?

Wolpert's formal self-simulation results show that unusual computational relations are mathematically possible, but they do not settle phenomenology.

## 8. Consciousness as evidence of base reality?

Some arguments claim that consciousness itself indicates we are not classically simulated because conscious experience cannot arise from computation.

That conclusion is only as strong as the anti-computational theory of consciousness supporting it.

A recent example of anti-computational argumentation is present in contemporary consciousness literature, but there is no scientific consensus establishing that classical computation cannot support consciousness.

Project guard:

`theory X says digital systems cannot be conscious` != `simulation hypothesis falsified` unless theory X is independently established and the simulation model requires only that forbidden implementation class.

## 9. Quantum consciousness claims

Quantum-consciousness proposals sometimes enter simulation discussions in two opposite ways:

- consciousness requires irreducible quantum processes, making classical simulation insufficient;
- a quantum simulator could instantiate the required processes.

Neither move is currently licensed as settled fact.

A quantum requirement for consciousness would itself need independent evidence.

Even then, it would constrain classical simulation more strongly than `simulation` generically.

## 10. Consciousness and moral implications

If simulated beings are conscious, running ancestor simulations may create enormous moral stakes.

This matters for Bostrom's second disjunct: advanced civilizations might avoid conscious simulations precisely because the simulations contain suffering persons.

Ethical restraint could therefore alter the expected prevalence of ancestor simulations without denying technological possibility.

The argument's `interest in running simulations` parameter cannot be treated as pure entertainment preference.

## 12. Hostile tests

1. Can the argument be reformulated without assuming phenomenal consciousness, using only information-processing observers? If so, is that still the same self-location argument?
2. If simulated agents lack consciousness, what property makes them members of our reference class?
3. If two exact computational copies count twice, why? Is physical instantiation doing the counting work?
4. If only conscious observer-moments matter, how are duration and branching weighted?
5. Could an advanced civilization intentionally run behaviorally accurate but non-conscious ancestor models to avoid moral cost?
6. Does any empirical test distinguish `conscious simulation` from `perfect behavioral simulation` from outside?
7. Can World Zero model observer-counting assumptions without pretending to instantiate consciousness?

## 13. V1 project position

Simulation theory currently **depends on unresolved philosophy of mind** more than popular presentations usually acknowledge.

The project therefore refuses the inference:

`we can imagine or build convincing artificial agents` -> `we have demonstrated conscious simulated observers`.

Current status:

- substrate independence: `CONSCIOUSNESS_ASSUMPTION / CONTESTED`.
- computational functionalism: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION / CONTESTED`.
- conscious digital personhood: `UNKNOWN`.
- behavioral simulation: technically meaningful but not automatically phenomenal.
- population counts of simulated conscious observers: `MODEL-CONDITIONAL`.
