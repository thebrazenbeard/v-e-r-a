# Simulation Theory Empirical Test Registry V1

Status: `PROPOSED TEST REGISTRY / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Purpose

A simulation hypothesis becomes scientifically interesting only when a **specified implementation model** makes observations more or less likely.

This registry prevents the common mistake:

`observation resembles something computers do` -> `therefore simulation`.

Every test entry must identify the actual model being tested, competing explanations, and what a positive or negative result would mean.

## Test 1: cubic lattice / rotational symmetry artifacts

**Source:** Beane, Davoudi, Savage (2012), https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1847

**Model tested:** universe numerically represented on a cubic space-time lattice with lattice-field-theory-like structure.

**Proposed observable:** cutoff/anisotropy or rotational-symmetry-breaking structure in the highest-energy cosmic rays, plus other lattice-linked consequences.

**Positive result:** evidence for cubic-lattice-like microstructure if ordinary physical alternatives are disfavored.

**Competing explanations:** fundamental discrete spacetime, anisotropic/new high-energy physics, astrophysical source/distribution effects, instrumental/systematic effects.

**Null result:** constrains lattice scale or studied implementation; does not exclude analog, quantum, adaptive, non-cubic, or otherwise different simulations.

**Generic simulation evidence?** `NO`.

## Test 2: compact spatial topology

**Source:** Gordon McCabe (2005), DOI 10.1016/J.SHPSB.2005.04.002.

**Model tested:** selected computer-program universe models with representational/topological constraints.

**Proposed observable:** non-trivial/compact spatial topology detectable cosmologically.

**Positive result:** supports compact topology; additional argument required to link topology to simulation.

**Competing explanations:** base-reality cosmologies with compact topology.

**Null result:** constrains topology model, not simulation generally.

**Generic simulation evidence?** `NO`.

## Test 3: same-physics resource feasibility

**Source:** F. Vazza (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08461

**Model tested:** parent universe obeys sufficiently similar information/energy physics and attempts high-fidelity simulation of our visible universe, Earth, or reduced-resolution Earth.

**Observable/constraint:** required information processing, energy, and power versus physically available resources.

**Positive simulation evidence:** none directly; this is primarily a feasibility constraint.

**Negative result for model:** if required resources exceed physical possibility under same-parent physics, that implementation family is excluded or severely disfavored.

**Escape:** parent universe with different laws, sparse rendering, different fidelity target.

**Generic simulation evidence?** `NO`.

## Test 4: finite resolution / ultraviolet cutoff

**Model tested:** simulations with finite spatial/temporal resolution or maximum represented energy.

**Possible observables:** fundamental cutoff, modified dispersion, Lorentz-symmetry violation, anisotropy, finite state-space effects.

**Competing explanations:** quantum gravity, discrete spacetime, effective field-theory cutoff, new physics unrelated to simulation.

**Positive result:** may favor a discrete/finitely resolved ontology, but external simulation requires separate provenance argument.

**Null result:** constrains implementations whose cutoff lies within the tested regime.

**Generic simulation evidence?** `NO`.

## Test 5: Planck scale as alleged pixel scale

**Popular claim:** Planck length/time are the literal pixel/frame size of simulation.

**Current evidence:** no established physical result identifies the Planck length as a computational pixel spacing.

**Alternative explanation:** Planck units identify dimensional scales built from fundamental constants and a regime where quantum-gravity effects are expected to matter.

**Status:** `POPULAR_ANALOGY / NOT AN ESTABLISHED SIMULATION TEST`.

## Test 6: quantum randomness as pseudorandom generator

**Popular claim:** quantum randomness is simulated pseudorandomness.

**Necessary prediction:** a specified simulator model would need to predict detectable non-random structure, finite state repetition, seed artifacts, or another distribution distinguishable from quantum theory.

**Competing explanation:** intrinsic quantum probabilities or hidden physical mechanisms.

**Current status:** generic analogy supplies no discriminatory prediction.

**Generic simulation evidence?** `NO`.

## Test 7: wavefunction collapse as rendering optimization

**Popular claim:** unobserved reality is not rendered until observation, analogous to game-engine optimization.

**Problem:** quantum measurement does not straightforwardly mean `human looks -> universe renders`; interpretation of measurement is itself contested.

**Needed model:** exact mapping between observation, computation-saving policy, and measurable deviations from ordinary quantum mechanics.

**Current status:** `SPECULATIVE_ANALOGY / NO UNIQUE PREDICTION`.

## Test 8: maximum speed as processor/update limit

**Popular claim:** speed of light is the simulation's processing-speed limit.

**Competing explanation:** Lorentzian spacetime structure in relativity.

**Required evidence:** a simulator-specific reason for the exact relativistic structure that predicts additional observations beyond relativity.

**Current status:** `ANALOGY ONLY`.

## Test 9: information/entropy bounds as memory limits

**Claim family:** black-hole entropy, horizon entropy, Bekenstein-like bounds, or holographic ideas reveal finite simulation memory.

**Problem:** physical information bounds arise inside physical theories and do not themselves identify external hardware.

**Needed bridge:** show that the observed information bound is more likely under an external implementation than under base physical law.

**Current status:** `PHYSICAL_INFORMATION RESULT / EXTERNAL-PROVENANCE INFERENCE NOT ESTABLISHED`.

## Test 10: holography as rendering/simulation evidence

**Claim family:** holographic principle implies universe is a projection or simulation.

**Problem:** gauge/gravity duality or holographic descriptions concern equivalence/encoding of physical theories; `holographic` does not mean a literal projected computer world.

**Needed bridge:** external-substrate/provenance evidence.

**Current status:** `CATEGORY ERROR WHEN PRESENTED AS DIRECT SIMULATION EVIDENCE`.

## Test 11: mathematical simplicity / compressibility

**Claim:** compact physical laws indicate source code or compression.

**Alternative:** discoverable universes may necessarily exhibit stable regularities; mathematical simplicity can arise without a programmer.

**Test possibility:** compare quantitative complexity distributions under explicit generative models.

**Current problem:** simulator priors over code/laws are undefined.

**Status:** `PHILOSOPHICAL_MODEL / UNDERDETERMINED`.

## Test 12: apparent fine-tuning

**Claim:** parameters suitable for life are intentionally chosen simulation settings.

**Alternative hypotheses:** theism, multiverse/selection effects, deeper law, chance under a prior, cosmopsychism/teleology, axiarchism, or other models.

**Simulation-specific problem:** arbitrary simulation goals do not imply life-permitting parameters; likelihood depends on simulator motives.

**Status:** `COMPARATIVE EXPLANATORY HYPOTHESIS`, not unique simulation evidence.

## Test 13: Mandela effect / shared false memories

**Popular claim:** mismatched memories reveal simulation edits or timeline resets.

**Ordinary alternatives:** reconstructive memory, social transmission, source-monitoring errors, schema effects, confabulation, misinformation.

**Needed simulation prediction:** a formal mechanism predicting the exact distribution of memory discrepancies better than psychology/social mechanisms.

**Current status:** `NO ESTABLISHED SIMULATION EVIDENCE`.

## Test 14: déjà vu / subjective glitches

**Popular claim:** déjà vu is a simulation reset/glitch.

**Ordinary alternatives:** neurological/cognitive familiarity processes, temporal-lobe phenomena, memory-processing errors.

**Needed simulation prediction:** independently measurable external anomaly correlated with the experience.

**Current status:** `ANECDOTAL / NON-DISCRIMINATING`.

## Test 15: improbable coincidences

**Popular claim:** unlikely coincidences reveal scripting.

**Problems:** multiple-comparisons effect, selective attention, post hoc probability assignments, base-rate neglect.

**Needed model:** preregistered prediction of event class and probability under simulation versus alternatives.

**Current status:** `NON-DISCRIMINATING WITHOUT A PRIOR MODEL`.

## Test 16: simulator messages

**Hypothesis:** intentional external agents encode an unambiguous message into fundamental physical phenomena.

**Potential evidence strength:** potentially high if the message is repeatable, mathematically non-natural, inaccessible to in-world agents before detection, and causally linked to controlled intervention.

**Authentication problems:** advanced in-world agents, hoax, unknown physics, selection effects, ambiguous encoding.

**Status:** `IN PRINCIPLE HIGH-VALUE PROVENANCE TEST / NO ESTABLISHED INSTANCE`.

## Test 17: controlled law violation

**Hypothesis:** simulator deliberately changes a fundamental law under reproducible requested conditions.

**Potential evidence:** repeated, independently verified, information-rich deviations that cannot be accommodated by a revised endogenous law.

**Problem:** sufficiently regular `violations` become new empirical laws; provenance remains underdetermined unless the interaction identifies an external agent/process.

**Status:** `IN PRINCIPLE STRONG / AUTHENTICATION HARD`.

## Evaluation rules

Every proposed simulation test must answer all seven questions:

1. What exact simulation architecture predicts this?
2. Is the prediction stated before observation or fitted afterward?
3. What base-reality physical theory could also predict it?
4. Is the likelihood of the observation quantitatively higher under the simulation model?
5. Would a null result falsify or constrain the model?
6. Would a positive result demonstrate external provenance or merely implementation-like structure?
7. Can the model evade any result by changing unspecified simulator behavior?

If questions 1 and 5 have no answer, the proposal is not functioning as an empirical test.

## V1 conclusion

The most scientifically productive simulation research currently comes from **narrow models that risk being falsified**.

The more the hypothesis retreats to an unconstrained simulator capable of producing any possible observation, the less empirical content it has.
