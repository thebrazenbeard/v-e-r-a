# Simulation Theory Overview V1

Status: `SOURCE-BASED OVERVIEW / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Scope

`Simulation theory` is not one proposition.

This packet uses the phrase only as an umbrella for several importantly different claims:

1. **Simulation argument** — conditional population/anthropic reasoning about what follows if posthuman civilizations can and do run many conscious ancestor simulations.
2. **External simulation hypothesis** — our observable universe is implemented by a computational process whose physical substrate lies outside our observable universe.
3. **Digital ontology / ontic pancomputationalism** — computation or informational structure is fundamental to physical reality, without requiring an external simulator.
4. **Numerical implementation hypotheses** — our universe is specifically implemented using a finite lattice, grid, discretization, finite-resolution scheme, or other architecture that could leave observable artifacts.
5. **Computational feasibility/constraint models** — physical, energetic, informational, algorithmic, or recursion-theoretic results that constrain what simulations could exist.
6. **Simulation epistemology** — what observers inside a world could know about its implementation or provenance.
7. **Simulation realism / metaphysics** — whether simulated objects, places, and events would count as real rather than illusory.
8. **Theological comparison** — whether creator/created-world relations in simulation models illuminate or differ from theological creator models.

The proposition `our universe actually is simulated` is a separate claim and remains `SPECULATIVE_MODEL / UNRESOLVED` in V1.

## Why the distinction matters

Many popular arguments slide between propositions that do not entail one another.

Examples:

- A universe may be exactly describable by computation without being executed on an external computer.
- A universe may be simulated without using a cubic lattice or classical digital architecture.
- A specific lattice artifact could falsify or support one implementation family without deciding the general simulation hypothesis.
- A simulator could be finite, morally indifferent, created, embodied in another universe, or simulated itself; none of those properties match classical theism automatically.
- An observer may be unable to disprove a sufficiently unconstrained simulation hypothesis without thereby acquiring evidence that the hypothesis is true.

V1 therefore treats every simulation claim as a typed proposition with explicit assumptions.

## Evidence posture

Simulation theory spans philosophy, physics, computer science, cosmology, consciousness studies, and theology. The project therefore uses the existing On-Theo evidence classes rather than creating a special truth category.

Typical mappings:

- Bostrom's paper: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` / `PHILOSOPHICAL_ARGUMENT`.
- Kipping's probability calculation: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` / `BAYESIAN_MODEL`.
- Beane/Davoudi/Savage: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` / `EMPIRICAL_TEST_PROPOSAL` tied to a cubic-lattice implementation.
- Wolpert: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` / `COMPUTATIONAL_LIMIT` and mathematical possibility results.
- Vazza: `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` / `PHYSICAL_CONSTRAINT` under parent-universe physics assumptions.
- Chalmers/Horgan/Peacocke: competing `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION` in metaphysics/epistemology.
- `Our universe is simulated`: `SPECULATIVE_MODEL` unless stronger evidence is independently established.

## V1 anchor sources

### Nick Bostrom, 2003

`Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?`, *The Philosophical Quarterly* 53.211 (2003), 243-255.

Direct public HTML/PDF:
- https://simulation-argument.com/simulation/
- https://simulation-argument.com/simulation.pdf

Bostrom argues that at least one of three disjuncts is true: human civilization becomes extinct before a posthuman stage; posthuman civilizations almost never run many ancestor simulations; or simulated observers vastly outnumber original observers such that we are almost certainly simulated under the argument's reference-class assumptions.

Bostrom explicitly assumes a form of substrate independence: sufficiently fine-grained computational replication of human brain processes could generate subjective experience. That assumption is important rather than incidental.

### David Kipping, 2020

`A Bayesian Approach to the Simulation Argument`.

Public preprint:
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12254

Kipping argues that uncertainty about whether Bostrom-like simulations are possible has to enter the model itself. Under his Bayesian model averaging, the probability of being simulated remains below 50%, approaching 50% as the number of simulations tends to infinity, until evidence that civilizations actually create such simulations changes the evidential situation.

This is a result under Kipping's model and priors, not a measurement of the universe.

### Beane, Davoudi, Savage, 2012

`Constraints on the Universe as a Numerical Simulation`.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1847

They explore observable consequences of a universe represented on a cubic space-time lattice, deriving bounds and discussing possible rotational-symmetry artifacts in the highest-energy cosmic rays.

This is a test of a particular numerical implementation family, not a generic detector for all conceivable simulated universes.

### Gordon McCabe, 2005

`Universe creation on a computer`, *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 36.

- DOI: 10.1016/J.SHPSB.2005.04.002
- http://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/1891/

McCabe treats simulation as an epistemological/metaphysical hypothesis and explores possible empirical consequences, topology, representation, and analog/digital distinctions.

### David H. Wolpert, 2024

`Implications of computer science theory for the simulation hypothesis`.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16050

Wolpert applies computer-science theory to simulation questions. His paper uses recursion-theoretic tools to discuss self-simulation, Rice-theorem limitations, encryption, and nested simulation structures.

Mathematical possibility of self-simulation is not evidence that our universe is physically self-simulating.

### F. Vazza, 2025

`Astrophysical constraints on the simulation hypothesis for this Universe: why it is (nearly) impossible that we live in a simulation`.

- https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08461

Vazza analyzes energy/information requirements under physically realistic scenarios and argues that a parent universe sharing sufficiently similar physics with ours could not feasibly simulate our universe at the relevant resolution. The paper explicitly leaves open parent universes with very different physical properties.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: physical computation

- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computation-physicalsystems/

The SEP distinguishes multiple forms of pancomputationalism and emphasizes that claims such as `everything computes`, `the universe is a computer`, and `our universe is an externally run simulation` are not synonymous.

### David Chalmers and critics

Chalmers's *Reality+* project and the 2024 Oxford debate distinguish metaphysics, epistemology, and value. Chalmers defends simulation realism; Terry Horgan disputes the metaphysical thesis; Christopher Peacocke disputes both parts of the metaphysical/epistemological framework.

## Initial project judgments

### Judgment 1: the generic hypothesis is underconstrained

A simulation hypothesis that permits arbitrarily powerful simulators, arbitrary parent physics, arbitrary implementation, arbitrary interventions, and arbitrary hidden state is difficult or impossible to distinguish empirically from base reality.

That is an epistemic problem, not positive evidence for simulation.

### Judgment 2: narrower simulation models can be scientific

Once a model makes implementation commitments — lattice geometry, resource bounds, finite resolution, topology, update rules, causal limits — it can generate predictions. The cost is that experimental confirmation/falsification usually bears on that narrow architecture, not `simulation` in general.

### Judgment 3: digital physics is not evidence of a simulator by itself

Even if future physics found a fundamental computational/informational description, an additional argument would still be required to infer an external implementation substrate or intentional simulator.

### Judgment 4: consciousness is a load-bearing assumption

Population arguments about simulated observers require the simulated entities to count as observers in the relevant sense. If conscious experience is not substrate independent, or if computational duplication does not instantiate additional phenomenal observers, the population argument changes substantially.

### Judgment 5: theology comparison is useful but dangerous

Simulation models offer unusually concrete examples of created worlds and creator-like agents. That makes them philosophically useful comparanda for theology. It does not make a programmer equivalent to God, nor does it show that historical religions were describing computation.

## Research questions

- Which parts of Bostrom's argument survive if substrate independence is rejected?
- How sensitive are observer-counting conclusions to reference-class choice?
- Can nested simulations increase observer counts without impossible resource multiplication?
- Which proposed empirical tests actually discriminate simulation from non-simulation physics?
- Can an embedded observer distinguish a simulator artifact from a fundamental law?
- Does a positive computational/discrete signature establish external provenance?
- What parent-universe assumptions are required by energy/information objections?
- What does simulation realism require for reference, causation, and spatial relations?
- How does the simulator concept differ from classical theism, demiurgy, cosmopsychism, or mathematical ontology?
- Can World Zero simulations help formalize the limits of substrate inference without being mistaken for evidence about our own world?
