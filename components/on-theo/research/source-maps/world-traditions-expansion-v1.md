# World Traditions Expansion — Initial Source Map V1

Status: SOURCE_MAP / RESEARCH_ACTIVE

## Shinto

- Kokugakuin University, Encyclopedia of Shinto, 'Introduction: The History of Shinto' — institutional reference; distinguishes shrine, sectarian, imperial, folk and scholastic/philosophical Shinto and explicitly traces interaction with Buddhism, Confucianism, Onmyodo, Shugendo and Christianity.
- Helen Hardacre, *Shinto: A History* — major modern diachronic synthesis.
- Mark Teeuwen and John Breen — major scholarship challenging timeless/unchanged-Shinto models and reconstructing institutional histories such as Ise.

Initial inference: the project must model 'Shinto' as a historically assembled and changing field rather than one archaic animist essence.

## Daoism

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 'Religious Daoism' — strong historiographic control against the simplistic philosophical-Daoism/religious-Daoism binary.
- Russell Kirkland, *Taoism: The Enduring Tradition* and textual-research surveys.
- Kristofer Schipper and Franciscus Verellen, *The Taoist Canon*.
- Fabrizio Pregadio, *The Encyclopedia of Taoism*.

Initial inference: Daodejing/Zhuangzi textual history, later institutional Daoism, ritual systems and alchemical/immortality traditions need separate but interacting lanes.

## Hindu traditions

- Existing On-Theo Vedic/Brahmanical/Upanishadic foundation remains the chronological base.
- Expansion needs scholarship by period and lineage rather than one 'Hindu beliefs' summary.
- Priority source families: Mahabharata, Bhagavad Gita, Ramayana, major Puranas, Agamas/Tantras, bhakti corpora and sect-specific commentarial traditions.

Initial inference: 'Hinduism' will be represented as a historical family of interacting traditions, with Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta, Smarta, Vedanta, bhakti, temple and regional systems kept distinguishable.

## Ancient Norse religion and modern Ásatrú/Heathenry

- Poetic Edda, skaldic poetry, runic/archaeological material and later Snorri preservation layers for ancient religion.
- The Troth, 'The History of Ásatrú, Heathenry and Norse Paganism' — useful modern self-description explicitly identifying contemporary Heathenry as reconstructed religion rather than proven uninterrupted survival.
- Modern movement scholarship must be separated from ancient religious reconstruction.

Initial inference: one corpus branch should reconstruct pre-Christian Norse/Germanic evidence; a separate branch should study twentieth/twenty-first-century Ásatrú/Heathenry.

## Lakota traditions

- Prioritize Lakota and tribal institutional/public educational sources.
- U.S. National Park Service sacred-site pages are useful secondary/public-history access surfaces for Bear Lodge/Devils Tower, Wind Cave/Oniya Ohloka, Black Hills sacred geography and continuing ceremony, but must not outrank community self-description.
- Ethnographic classics require colonial-context/source-bias review before promotion.

Initial inference: sacred geography is not peripheral; place, emergence, buffalo relations, ceremony, kinship and sky/land relationships form a major structural layer.

Restriction rule: do not republish non-public ceremonial detail merely because historical ethnographers recorded it.

## Satanism

- Joseph P. Laycock, *Satanism* (Cambridge Elements in New Religious Movements, 2023/2024) — high-value overview of accusation history, black-mass legend, Romantic Satanism, occult currents and modern Satanic religions.
- Jesper Aagaard Petersen — key scholarship on modern Satanism as a family of differing religious/philosophical movements.
- Group primary sources will be used for self-description but not treated as neutral history.

Initial inference: modern Satanisms cannot be collapsed into literal devil worship. Church of Satan, The Satanic Temple, explicitly theistic Satanists, Setians and extremist/esoteric groups require separate nodes.

## 'Devil worship'

Research default: historical accusation category until self-identification is demonstrated.

Key research periods:
- medieval and early-modern demonological accusation;
- witchcraft and heresy prosecution;
- anti-minority and anti-new-religion labeling;
- nineteenth/twentieth-century occult fears;
- 1980s–1990s Satanic Panic;
- contemporary conspiracy culture;
- actual self-identified theistic Satanic practice.

## Sumerian religion

- Oxford Electronic Text Corpus of Sumerian Literature (ETCSL) — primary-text access surface for compositions such as *Enki and the World Order*, *Inana's Descent to the Netherworld*, Dumuzi material and other Sumerian literary texts.
- Critical editions must supersede ETCSL where newer manuscript collation materially changes a reading.
- Hannes D. Galter on Enki/Ea — useful diachronic bridge between Sumerian and later Mesopotamian traditions.
- New critical work on *Enki and the World Order* should be used for witness-level refinement.

Initial inference: city cult, divine office/function, kingship and temple economy are likely at least as important to the Sumerian religious system as modern retellings of 'myths.'

## Cross-corpus guard

Every one of these traditions enters On-Theo first as an internally reconstructed historical system. Comparative edges come later.

Resemblance alone remains insufficient for borrowing, common origin, or metaphysical equivalence.
