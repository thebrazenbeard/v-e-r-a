# Epistemic tests and attack surface

The GOD model is easy to make unfalsifiable. This file exists to prevent that from being mistaken for strength.

## 1. Structural similarity is cheap evidence

Religious language about higher reality, hidden causes, death, creation and judgment will naturally map onto modern information/system metaphors because both domains talk about layers, authority and causality.

Therefore:

> "This maps beautifully to software" is interesting but weak evidence that software is ontologically involved.

## 2. Historical-priority test

For every apparent technical rhyme, ask:

- Is the motif already well explained in earlier Jewish scripture/apocalypticism?
- Does Christianity inherit it normally from Judaism?
- Does the Qur'an inherit/reframe it normally from Jewish/Christian Late Antiquity?
- Does a noncanonical source predate or postdate the canonical form?

If ordinary transmission predicts the similarity, no external common-source hypothesis is needed.

## 3. Specificity test

A simulation hypothesis would become more interesting if traditions contained a cluster of **specific, otherwise-unmotivated** concepts that map to one coherent architecture rather than generic metaphors.

Weak matches:

- creator = programmer;
- world = simulation;
- heaven = outside.

Potentially stronger, if independently attested and historically difficult to derive:

- consistent separation of rendered state and hidden authoritative state;
- explicit identity persistence independent of current body state;
- privileged operations governed by permission rather than law-breaking;
- precise nested-world or recursive-creator structure;
- information-preservation/re-instantiation concepts with unusual specificity.

Current evidence does not establish such a technical cluster.

## 4. Prediction versus retrofitting

Ask whether the model predicts something **before** finding the text.

Bad workflow:

1. read religious symbol;
2. invent software analogue;
3. announce prediction success.

Better workflow:

1. specify a GOD architecture;
2. derive what in-world religious descriptions might look like;
3. blind-search multiple traditions for predicted features;
4. record misses as well as hits;
5. compare against alternative models.

## 5. Counter-models

At minimum compare GOD against:

- ordinary cultural transmission;
- convergent human cognition/metaphor;
- social-control/institutional evolution;
- apocalyptic response to political domination;
- mystical/altered-state interpretation;
- philosophical appearance/reality dualism;
- narrative accretion around charismatic founders.

A model earns value by explaining more with fewer ad hoc patches.

## 6. Paternity-stigma test

Hypothesis: Yeshua experienced meaningful illegitimacy/paternity stigma.

Evidence that would strengthen it:

- demonstrably early independent traditions of disputed paternity;
- unambiguous first-century slurs tied to parentage;
- independent social records or traditions not plausibly generated by later virgin-birth polemic.

Evidence currently available:

- ambiguous "son of Mary" in Mark;
- Matthew's narrative awareness of an unexpected pregnancy;
- late-second-century Panthera polemic;
- later rabbinic/counter-gospel traditions.

Status: plausible question, not established biography.

## 7. Mary-authority suppression test

Hypothesis: later sexualized portrayal of Mary Magdalene functioned to neutralize competing authority.

Strengthening evidence:

- earlier texts assigning Mary authority;
- later texts explicitly opposing that authority on gender/status grounds;
- reception history showing her authority reduced as sexual-sinner identity rises;
- explicit strategic statements by influential actors.

Current record supports the first three to varying degrees, but a single coordinated suppression plan remains difficult to prove.

## 8. Qur'anic independence test

Because the Qur'an is centuries later, its agreement with earlier Abrahamic material usually cannot count as an independent witness without a specific reason to think the tradition arose separately.

Its divergences can nevertheless preserve evidence of which controversies remained active in Late Antiquity.

## 9. Simulation epistemology problem

If a simulation is internally complete and external access is intentionally hidden, inhabitants may have no decisive experiment capable of establishing substrate status.

That means the general simulation hypothesis may be empirically underdetermined.

Do not confuse "cannot disprove" with "therefore probable."

## 10. Experimental extension

A future computational project can make parts of the question testable **as model behavior**, even if metaphysics remains untestable.

Build simulated worlds containing agents with:

- limited local observations;
- mortality;
- social hierarchy;
- occasional privileged interventions;
- noisy cross-layer messages;
- varying controller objectives;
- memory/re-instantiation rules.

Then ask:

- What religions/metaphysics do agents infer?
- Can they distinguish providence from noise?
- Which controller behaviors produce concepts analogous to miracle, revelation, judgment or hidden order?
- Does a non-intervening controller become observationally indistinguishable from no controller?

This is where the original World3/systems interest can become an empirical modeling program rather than only metaphysical conversation.
