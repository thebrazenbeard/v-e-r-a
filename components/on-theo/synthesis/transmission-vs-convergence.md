# Transmission, inheritance, and convergence

When two traditions share an image or conceptual structure, at least five explanations should be considered before invoking a supernatural or simulation-level common source.

## 1. Direct literary dependence

A later author/community has access to a written form of the earlier tradition.

Evidence can include unusually specific wording, sequence, rare motifs, demonstrable textual availability, and historical contact.

## 2. Oral / liturgical / homiletic transmission

Stories and concepts circulate without direct manuscript copying. This is especially important in Late Antiquity, where communities could know narratives through preaching, prayer, storytelling, debate, translation and retelling.

## 3. Shared ancestral tradition

Judaism, Christianity and Islam are genealogically related religious traditions. Similar creation, prophecy, judgment and resurrection structures are therefore often expected inheritance, not independent confirmations.

## 4. Polemical dependence

A later tradition can preserve an earlier claim specifically by **rejecting or correcting it**.

Examples include:

- Panthera counter-tradition as an anti-virgin-birth response;
- Qur'anic rejection/reconfiguration of Christian divine-sonship/Trinitarian claims;
- Christian rereading of Jewish scripture as messianic fulfillment.

Opposition is itself a transmission mechanism.

## 5. Independent structural convergence

Different minds can generate similar metaphors because they confront similar problems:

- why anything exists;
- why suffering exists;
- why humans behave badly;
- whether death is final;
- whether perception exhausts reality;
- how local authority relates to moral truth;
- how hidden causes can be inferred from visible effects.

"Appearance versus reality" is philosophically recurrent enough that similarity alone cannot prove contact.

## Additional model: external common source

The GOD hypothesis adds a sixth explanation:

> multiple traditions may imperfectly encode interactions with or intuitions about the same higher-order reality.

This model is logically possible but currently evidentially expensive. It should be considered **after**, not instead of, historically ordinary transmission models.

Each claimed parallel should receive one or more provisional tags:

- `DIRECT_DEPENDENCE_POSSIBLE`

## Example: Jesus as Word

- Jewish wisdom/word traditions predate Christianity -> shared conceptual substrate.
- Johannine Logos -> Christian development inside Jewish/Hellenistic environment.
- Qur'anic Jesus as a word from God appears centuries later -> direct/indirect Christian interaction is historically plausible.
- software "executable expression" -> project analogy.

It would be bad methodology to skip the first three steps and claim the software analogy proves common knowledge of simulation architecture.

## Example: world not ultimate

- Jewish apocalypticism: present oppressive order will be overturned by divine rule.
- Christian age-to-come / Kingdom / resurrection: inherits and transforms that structure.
- Qur'anic dunya/akhira distinction: emerges in a later Abrahamic environment.
- Gnostic and philosophical systems can intensify appearance/reality dualism.
- simulation metaphor maps naturally onto all of them.

Again: historical inheritance explains much of the overlap. The remaining philosophical rhyme is still interesting without being mysterious.
