# Shinto — Master Research Lane V1

Status: FOUNDATION / TEXT-RITUAL-HISTORY SEPARATION

## Scope

Shinto is not treated as a single unchanged system extending transparently into prehistory.

Kokugakuin University's Encyclopedia of Shinto explicitly notes that Shinto's origins cannot be assigned a simple founding point and that the systematization of kami rites developed historically, especially around the ritsuryō state.

Core guard:

LATER_SHINTO_SYSTEM != DIRECT_WINDOW_INTO_PREHISTORIC_JAPANESE_RELIGION.

## Core textual sources

Priority classical source set:
- Kojiki;
- Nihon shoki;
- Fudoki;
- Kogo shūi;
- Man'yōshū;
- Engishiki norito.

Institutional source:
https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/

The Encyclopedia of Shinto treats Kojiki and Nihon shoki as central classical sources for kami traditions while also emphasizing their historical compilation context.

## Kojiki / Nihon shoki control

Kojiki and Nihon shoki must not be treated as interchangeable witnesses.

Nihon shoki preserves multiple alternative accounts in its kami-age material, demonstrating that the compilers worked with variant traditions.

Research rule:

KIKI_PARALLEL != SINGLE_ORIGINAL_MYTH.

Track:
- shared motifs;
- variant genealogies;
- divergent deity order;
- political/editorial framing;
- continental influence.

## Kami

Kami is not normalized to:
"god" in a single Abrahamic sense.

Claims must identify:
- source;
- ritual context;
- shrine/cult association;
- function;
- genealogy;
- whether the concept is classical, medieval, early-modern, or modern.

## Historical lanes

### Pre-ritsuryō / protohistoric
Evidence is largely archaeological and retrospectively interpreted.
Avoid using Kojiki/Nihon shoki as unfiltered prehistoric reportage.

### Ritsuryō system
Track state organization of kami rites and institutions.

### Medieval
Buddhist-Shinto interaction is central.
Do not model Buddhism and Shinto as sealed systems throughout the medieval period.

### Early modern
Track Kokugaku and other reinterpretive projects.

### Modern / State Shinto
Separate Meiji-state institutions and ideology from earlier shrine traditions.

### Contemporary
Distinguish current shrine practice, local matsuri, sectarian Shinto, and individual belief.

## Comparative controls

Do not flatten:
- kami into universal creator gods;
- Yomi into Christian hell;
- kegare into moral sin;
- harae into forgiveness;
- Amaterasu into every solar-deity model;
- Izanagi/Izanami into generic primordial-couple templates without lineage evidence.

## Source bias controls

Classical myth compilations are also state texts.

Therefore:
MYTHIC_ACCOUNT
+
POLITICAL_GENEALOGY
+
RITUAL_MEMORY
may coexist in one passage.

A political function does not make a myth "fake"; a mythic function does not make it literal political history.
