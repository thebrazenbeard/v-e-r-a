## 1. Inana's Descent to the Netherworld — c.1.4.1

Primary:
https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/cgi-bin/etcsl.cgi?text=c.1.4.1

### kur

kur is the dominant realm/topographic term in the composition.

Representative direct occurrences include:
- lines 4-13: Inana repeatedly descends/goes to kur after abandoning heaven, earth, offices, and cult centers;
- line 26: Inana goes toward kur;
- lines 32-33: Ninshubur's instructions describe Inana going toward kur;
- lines 43-64: pleas to Enlil, Nanna, and Enki repeatedly use kur in the death-realm context;
- lines 74-75, 78, 90, 94, 99-100, 117, 119, 123, 125: doors, gates, and Neti are constructed with kur;
- lines 132-163: the repeated formula me kur-ra / jarza kur-ra accompanies passage through the seven gates;
- later rescue passages again use kur for the realm from which Inana must be restored.

ETCSL's glossing is methodologically revealing:
- line 94 glosses kur as "(mountain) land" while the English paragraph translates Neti as "chief doorman of the underworld";
- lines 99-100 likewise lemmatize kur in "door/gate of kur" while the English translation says "door/gate of the underworld."

Therefore:

INANA_DESCENT_KUR = LOCAL_DEATH_REALM_USE

but:

KUR_LEXEME != UNDERWORLD_IN_ALL_SUMERIAN_CONTEXTS

### ganzer

Ganzer is not used as a transparent synonym for every kur occurrence.

Verified occurrences include:
- line 73: Inana approaches the palace Ganzer;
- line 98: repeated report that Inana has approached the palace Ganzer;
- line 120: each door of the palace Ganzer is to be opened separately;
- line 126: repetition of the same palace-Ganzer instruction.

ETCSL glosses ganzer as a GN/place-name in these lines.

The composition therefore distinguishes:
- kur = the broader death-realm/topographic domain;
- Ganzer = a named palace/gateway locus within that domain.

Control:

GANZER_IN_C141 != EVERY_OCCURRENCE_OF_KUR

### gidim

No gidim occurrence was found in the ETCSL transliteration search for c.1.4.1.

This matters because the composition concerns divine death/descent without making gidim the governing category.

### arali

No lexical a-ra-li / arali occurrence was identified in c.1.4.1 during this pass.

The substring "a-ra" in verbal forms is not arali and must not be false-matched.

### kurnugi

No kurnugi occurrence was found in the ETCSL transliteration search for c.1.4.1.

## 2. Gilgamesh, Enkidu and the Netherworld — c.1.8.1.4

Primary:
https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/cgi-bin/etcsl.cgi?text=c.1.8.1.4

This composition is especially valuable because kur, Ganzer, and gidim occur within the same textual system.

### kur and ganzer paired

Verified paired sequences include:
- line 166: abula ganzer igi kur-ra-ka — the gate of Ganzer at/before kur;
- line 175: Enkidu's associated object falls to Ganzer in one manuscript;
- lines 178-179: Gilgamesh promises to bring items up from kur / Ganzer;
- lines 181 onward: descent toward kur;
- lines 224 and 232: parallel laments explicitly pair one object falling to kur and another to Ganzer;
- lines 225-236: Enkidu is repeatedly said to have been seized/held by kur;
- lines 239-242: Utu opens a chink in kur so Enkidu's shade can emerge.

This is direct internal evidence that:

KUR_AND_GANZER_CO-OCCUR_AS_DISTINCT_TERMS

They participate in one death-realm scene but are not merely orthographic variants.

### gidim

gidim appears repeatedly in the final survey of postmortem conditions.

Verified examples:
- line 213: a gidim occurs in the netherworld episode;
- lines 303 and parallels: a person's gidim may fail to have a place under certain conditions;
- lines 340-343 and parallel manuscript lines: gidim is explicitly attached to deceased individuals;
- line 292 and parallel: gidim introduces the neglected-dead/funerary-offering case.

This is the human-dead person-category, not the place-name.

Control:

GIDIM = DEAD_PERSON/SPIRIT_CATEGORY

while:

KUR/GANZER = REALM/PLACE_CATEGORY

## 3. The Death of Gilgamesh — c.1.8.1.3

Primary:
https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/cgi-bin/etcsl.cgi?text=c.1.8.1.3

### gidim

This composition explicitly promotes Gilgamesh into a distinguished postmortem gidim role.

Verified occurrences:
- the Nibru tradition includes an ezen gidim, a festival of ghosts;
- lines 38-39 in a major sequence: Gilgamesh as gidim among the dead becomes governor of kur and pre-eminent among the gidim;
- parallel lines 131-132 repeat the same formulation;
- a fragmentary sequence also addresses "your gidim."

This makes a useful semantic structure:

GILGAMESH = HUMAN_DEAD -> GIDIM

with exceptional royal office:

GIDIM_GILGAMESH -> SAGINA/GOVERNOR_OF_KUR

The text does not make all gidim royal governors.

### kur

kur is the governing place/domain term in the royal-afterlife passages.

Examples:
- kur-ra ki ku10-ku10, the dark place of kur;
- shagina kur-ra, governor of kur;
- prohibitions/advice about going to kur;
- funerary/judicial authority exercised within kur.

The English translation regularly renders this as "nether world."

### arali

A direct a-ra-li occurrence appears in the later Me-Turan/Nibru sequence:
- line 166: iri-gal a-ra-li in the passage ETCSL translates with "the Great City Arali."

This shows that the same composition can use:
- kur as broad death-realm terminology;
- Arali as a named/marked death-realm location expression.

Control:

ARALI_IN_C1813 != AUTOMATIC_SYNONYM_FOR_ALL_KUR

## 4. The Death of Ur-Namma — c.2.4.1.1

Primary:
https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/cgi-bin/etcsl.cgi?text=c.2.4.1.1

### arali

Arali is explicitly named.

Verified examples:
- line 62: a-ra-li ki saj-ki kalam-ma-she3 — Ur-Namma is brought to Arali;
- line 127: Ninazimua is described in relation to a-ra-li;
- a parallel manuscript sequence preserves another a-ra-li reference for Ninazimua.

The English translation renders Arali as a named place in the death realm.

### kur

kur is much more frequent and carries the practical vocabulary of the journey and underworld order.

Verified uses include:
- line 65: a foreign/unknown land comparison, showing the lexeme's semantic flexibility even inside the same composition;
- line 73: the journey/road of kur;
- line 76: seven great gatekeepers/doorkeepers of kur;
- lines 79-86: cries, food/water, rites, and offerings of kur;
- line 90: Nergal as Enlil of kur;
- line 95: Gilgamesh as king of kur;
- lines 132-144: offerings of kur, great dais/throne of kur, dwelling in kur, and judgments of kur.

This is a major result because c.2.4.1.1 itself contains both:
- a non-underworld geographical/foreign-land use of kur;
- multiple underworld-context uses of kur.

Therefore the semantic guard is demonstrated inside one composition:

SAME_LEXEME_KUR + DIFFERENT_LOCAL_CONTEXTS -> DIFFERENT_ENGLISH_SENSES

## 6. Translation-loss findings

### Finding A — English "underworld" collapses source distinctions

ETCSL's readable English necessarily translates several different Sumerian expressions into "underworld/nether world."

For On-Theo, the translation must not become the semantic registry.

Required storage model:

translation_gloss: underworld
source_lexeme: kur | ganzer | arali | other
composition_id: exact composition
line_ref: exact line
local_function: realm | gate | palace | named city/place | office-domain | ambiguous

### Finding B — kur can shift sense inside one composition

The Death of Ur-Namma uses kur both in a comparison to a foreign/unknown land and repeatedly for the death realm.

This is stronger evidence against naive global glossing than dictionary statistics alone.

### Finding C — person terms and place terms must remain type-separated

gidim answers "what kind of postmortem entity/person is involved?"

kur / arali / ganzer answer spatial, institutional, or topographic questions.

A graph that lets gidim and kur compete as synonyms has already lost the source semantics.

### Finding D — absence is composition-specific evidence

The absence of kurnugi from all four core ETCSL transliterations searched here matters.

It does NOT prove the lexeme was unknown to all Sumerian speakers or traditions.

It DOES block the project from retrofitting kurnugi into these four texts merely because English summaries call their setting "the underworld."

Strong conclusions:
- kur is the broadest recurring death-realm lexeme in the four core compositions, but it remains context-sensitive;
- Ganzer is compositionally distinct from kur where both co-occur;
- gidim is a person/ghost category and must not be modeled as a place term;
- Arali appears as a named/marked place in Death of Gilgamesh and Death of Ur-Namma;
- kurnugi is absent from the four searched core ETCSL transliterations and must not be inserted into them by English-gloss harmonization.
