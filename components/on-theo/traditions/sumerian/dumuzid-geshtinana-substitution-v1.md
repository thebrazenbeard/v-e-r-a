## 2. Galla and kur are jointly present from the opening

P346096 directly preserves:
- line 1: junior and senior galla;
- line 3: galla enter Uruk and seize Inana;
- lines 4-10: repeated kur-sze3 e11-de3 descent formula.

This gives one physical witness containing both:
- captor-entity vocabulary: galla;
- death-realm direction vocabulary: kur.

They are not lexical synonyms.

## 3. Inana hands Dumuzid over

Line 13:
Inana herself hands Dumuzid over.

P346096 preserves the line in the aligned score.

This is direct primary-text support for the transfer/substitution transition after Inana's release.

It should not be paraphrased as:
"Inana kills Dumuzid."

The text instead depicts transfer into the captors' power.

## 4. Restraint and threatened execution

Lines 14-21 describe:
- feet restrained;
- neckstock/restraint;
- weapons raised;
- axes sharpened;
- rope/binding;
- an executioner;
- covering his face.

This is capture/execution preparation imagery, not a generic underworld geography passage.

Semantic type:
CAPTURE_AND_EXECUTION_PREPARATION.

## 5. Explicit kur substitution formula

The strongest sequence is lines 24-30.

P346096 preserves:
- line 25: she descends to kur;
- line 26: because she descends to kur;
- line 27: ga2-e ki-gar-ra-bi-sze3 kur-sze3 ba-ab-szum2-mu-de3.

CDLI translates line 27:
"I, as a substitute, am to be given to the netherworld."

This provides direct composition-level support for:
- Inana descent;
- Dumuzid in-her-place transfer;
- kur as destination/domain.

Project formulation:

DUMUZID_SUBSTITUTION_TO_KUR = DIRECT_TEXTUAL_MODEL

not:

UNIVERSAL_SUBSTITUTION_LAW_OF_SUMERIAN_AFTERLIFE.

## 6. Escape attempt does not erase substitution

Lines 29-37:
- Dumuzid asks Utu to transform him;
- he wants to escape his galla;
- his form is changed;
- he flees toward Geshtin-ana.

This shows the narrative treats substitution and capture as contestable events within the story, not a simple instantaneous metaphysical transfer.

## 7. Geshtin-ana's lament

After Dumuzid reaches Geshtin-ana:
- she sees him;
- scratches face/nose;
- tears/splits her garment;
- intones lament for her brother.

P346096 preserves this sequence in the aligned score.

This creates a direct bridge between:
- substitution/capture narrative;
- sibling lament;
- the broader Dumuzid lament corpus.

## 8. Galla remain the pursuing agents

Later lines again describe:
- galla searching for Dumuzid;
- junior/senior galla;
- lack of compassion/kin ties in their characterization.

This reinforces the earlier semantic control:

galla = pursuing/captor entity class

not:

gidim = dead person's ghost.

## 9. Relationship to Inana's Descent

This composition repeats several structural elements known from Inana's Descent:
- kur descent formula;
- Inana's release/return problem;
- transfer of Dumuzid;
- galla involvement.

But the texts are separate compositions with separate witnesses and textual histories.

Therefore:

STRUCTURAL_OVERLAP != ONE_IDENTICAL_TEXTUAL_WITNESS.

The project should model cross-composition relation as:
RELATED_DUMUZID_INANA_TRADITION / TEXTUALLY_DISTINCT.

## 10. Relationship to Dumuzid's Dream

Dumuzid's Dream and Dumuzid and Geshtin-ana share:
- galla pursuit;
- Dumuzid escape attempts;
- Geshtin-ana refuge/lament;
- death/capture framing.

But Q000347 and Q000344 have very different witness structures.

This strengthens a Dumuzid death-cycle family while preserving composition identity.
