## 19. Unbiased Analysis of “My Good Soul”

Core read:

> The song is sincere, but not clean.

It does not sound fake. It sounds like an 18-year-old writing from genuine emotional dependence, longing, gratitude, and fear of darkness.

But it does not sound like a song that knows exactly who it is talking to.

The “you” is almost too perfect:

- light
- hope
- smile
- oxygen
- passion/warmth
- endurance
- someone who holds on through darkness/shadows
- someone who does not cave during disagreements
- someone who still loves her through frustration

The “good soul” figure reads less like a specific boyfriend and more like an emotional role:

> The good soul who keeps me from falling apart.

Since Amber said it was about both Patrick and Brent, the song likely fuses two emotional functions into one impossible addressee.

Possible split:

- Brent may have been the public tether/continuity/familiar boyfriend in her Florida life.
- Patrick may have been the light/hope/desire/rescue/intensity figure who saw danger and tried to pull her back.

But do not overclaim. The song is not precise enough to assign every line cleanly.

Most important thematic note:

The song does not primarily say, “I choose you.”

It says something closer to:

> You keep me alive. You hold me. You don’t let go. You still love me.

That is emotionally real, but it puts the burden outward.

Key analysis conclusion:

> The song proves Amber felt something real. It does not prove she loved cleanly, chose cleanly, or saw Patrick separately enough from Brent.

Or:

> Some of it was real, but she was not honest enough or whole enough to make the real parts safe.

Do not use “none of it was real” unless Patrick says it himself. The better reading is:

> Not fake. Not faithful. Not clean. Not nothing.
