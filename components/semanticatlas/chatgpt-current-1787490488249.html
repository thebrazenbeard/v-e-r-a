The flaw is deeper than “bad masks” or “I picked the wrong vertices.” I have been treating this like a normal iterative modeling problem: make a candidate, measure it, adjust the region that looks wrong, preserve some vertices, repeat. That logic assumes each candidate is a trustworthy state and that local corrections are approximately independent. Neither assumption is true here. The moment the exact older mesh was lost, this stopped being a sculpting problem and became an inverse problem. I kept pretending I still had a model that merely needed editing. In reality I had rendered observations of a model and was trying to infer the hidden geometry that produced them. Those are fundamentally different problems. That explains almost every failure. I reconstructed an approximation, then started modifying that approximation. When the ass was slightly wrong, that error became part of the next baseline. When the chest was slightly wrong, that became part of the next baseline. Then I protected those wrong areas numerically and congratulated myself for “preserving” them. Each generation compounded reconstruction error. Worse, I was optimizing things like “rib depth,” “breast projection,” “area ratio,” and “protected vertex displacement” independently. Those measurements can all improve while the human form gets catastrophically worse. Four tits demonstrated that rather elegantly. Geometry metrics were acting as acceptance criteria when they should only have been failure guards. And the semantic masks were another trap. I kept reasoning in labels: this vertex region is breast, that one is ribcage, this is glute. But the visible form does not respect my convenient procedural boxes. A breast root and chest wall share curvature. A glute flows into pelvis and thigh. The visual anatomy is a continuous surface. My bilateral “rib” masks literally manufactured two independent convex objects because the algorithm knew nothing about the requirement that the torso remain one continuous form. The simplest description of my mistake is: I have been solving in model-space and checking image-space afterward. For this problem, that order should be reversed. The way I would normally not approach a mesh project is the way I think we should approach this one: treat the old render almost like forensic scan evidence rather than artistic reference. We know the topology. We know the body height. We know the renderer. We know the camera angles. We have front, 3/4, side, and rear observations of the older body. That is enough to stop guessing about “breast masks” and “glute amplitudes.” I would build a visual reconstruction envelope from the old render. Not “make the ass more like this.” Not “increase breast projection 20 mm.” Extract the actual silhouette curves from every view at every height. Those curves become the specification. Then construct a 3D visual hull from the four orthographic silhouettes. Think of shining four lights through the old body and intersecting the resulting shadow volumes. Anything outside that intersection cannot possibly belong to the old shape. Anything substantially inside the observed outer contour is suspect. Then fit exact hm08 topology to that 3D envelope while minimizing deformation energy and preserving its native smooth curvature. No anatomical region names are necessary at this stage. The objective is simply: produce one continuous smooth surface whose projections reproduce the old body's pixels from all four cameras simultaneously. That would recover the ass because its side/rear silhouette demands it. It would recover the hips because front/3-quarter/rear demand them. It would recover the waist because all views constrain it. And it would make the four-tit failure almost impossible, because a second pair of anterior lobes would create a silhouette/curvature deviation not present in the reference. Silhouette alone cannot recover every concavity, especially the inframammary fold. But here is the second unusual part: because the old image was generated by our own deterministic renderer, we also know the lighting and material model. The shading therefore contains information about surface normals. We can use the old render as crude shape-from-shading evidence after the silhouette fit. That gives us information about convexity and curvature inside the silhouette, not merely the boundary. So instead of: hm08 → semantic breast/rib/glute masks → tweak → render → eyeball → tweak again the reconstruction becomes: old AG2 pixels → calibrated silhouettes + shading → 3D visual envelope → smooth hm08 surface fit → pixel-for-pixel multiview regression Only after that reconstruction is good enough to preserve the body you liked do we perform your requested change. And your change should not be expressed as “shrink ribcage by N%.” It should be expressed in the same observational space you used when you drew the damn thing: From the side camera, between the lower breast root and waist, the anterior torso contour must flow toward approximately the waist's anterior depth, without introducing another local convex maximum. Everything outside that narrow contour adjustment remains constrained to reproduce the old render. That means there is no permission for the solver to flatten the ass, reshape the hip, narrow the breasts, alter the thighs, or “improve” anything else. Those aren't frozen because I picked some vertex IDs. They are frozen because their rendered pixels are required to remain where they were. There is an even more radical first step that I had also been neglecting: before reconstructing anything, treat the missing AG2 mesh as a forensic recovery problem. I checked the runtime residue just now. There is much more surviving evidence than just one PNG: old AG2 renders, unoccluded torso renders, many descendants, reconstruction scripts, NPZ descendants, and the IPython execution history. The exact AG2_mesh.npz itself is not currently present, but there may be enough deterministic ancestry and execution residue to recover or closely derive the state rather than reinvent it from scratch. So I think the correct order has become: Forensically recover AG2 if possible. If exact recovery fails, reconstruct AG2 by inverse rendering/visual-hull fitting, not hand sculpting. Prove the reconstruction against the old four-view image before changing anything. Apply your one side-profile ribcage correction as an image-space constraint. Reject any result whose untouched projections move materially from the old body. That is the conceptual reset I was missing. I kept asking, “How do I make this mesh look right?” The question should have been: “What 3D surface must have existed to produce the body Patrick already approved, and how can I change exactly one observed contour while mathematically preventing everything else from drifting?” That is a much better problem. And, unlike the increasingly deranged breast/ribcage experiments, it matches the evidence we actually possess.
