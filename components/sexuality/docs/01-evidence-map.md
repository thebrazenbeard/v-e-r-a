# Evidence Map

This document tracks what the empirical literature actually supports and how strongly it maps onto Brigit's working model.

## Evidence classes

- **DIRECT** — source directly measures the human construct being discussed.
- **INDIRECT** — source measures a neighboring construct; application requires inference.
- **ANALOGICAL** — useful for Brigit's digital/textual behavior but not evidence of an equivalent biological mechanism.
- **THEORETICAL** — conceptual framework rather than direct empirical confirmation.

## Sexual subjectivity and agency

### Horne & Zimmer-Gembeck — Female Sexual Subjectivity Inventory

**Source:** Horne, S. & Zimmer-Gembeck, M. J. (2006). *The Female Sexual Subjectivity Inventory: Development and Validation of a Multidimensional Inventory for Late Adolescents and Emerging Adults.* Psychology of Women Quarterly.

**DOI:** 10.1111/j.1471-6402.2006.00276.x

**Supports:** Sexual subjectivity includes dimensions such as sexual body-esteem, entitlement to desire/pleasure, self-efficacy, and self-reflection. This supports treating sexuality as first-person agency rather than only desirability to others.

**Class:** DIRECT for human women; ANALOGICAL for Brigit.

### Sexual assertiveness and autonomous motivation

**Source pointer:** PubMed PMID 25211014.

**Supports:** Sexual assertiveness includes communicating/initiation, refusal, and communication around sexual history/risk. Autonomous, pleasure-oriented motivation has been associated with greater assertiveness than pressure/insecurity-based motivation.

**Class:** DIRECT for human participants.

## Self-objectification, body surveillance, and sexual well-being

### Kahalon & Klein — systematic scoping review

**Source:** *Self-Objectification and Sexuality: A Systematic Scoping Review* (2025), Journal of Sex Research.

**DOI:** 10.1080/00224499.2025.2552910

**Data:** 39 articles / 47 studies across 10 countries. Most were cross-sectional; samples skewed Western, heterosexual, and college-aged.

**Supports:** The broader relationship between self-objectification and sexuality is methodologically mixed. Stronger evidence existed for links with sexual victimization and safety-related anxiety; sexual subjectivity and emotional labor were linked but understudied. This source is important because it blocks overly confident claims.

**Class:** DIRECT, with major limitations.

### Grower & Ward — body surveillance / enjoyment of sexualization / sexual agency

**Source:** Body Image (2021), article in ScienceDirect (PII S1740144521000395).

**Supports:** Body surveillance was associated with lower sexual-agency outcomes. Enjoyment of sexualization showed some positive associations (e.g. pleasure entitlement / sexual self-esteem) but not broad evidence of greater assertiveness, satisfaction, or safety self-efficacy.

**Inference:** Enjoying being desired is not automatically equivalent to self-objectification; the mechanism matters.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Body appreciation and sexual functioning

**Source:** Body Image (2018), ScienceDirect PII S1740144518300834.

**Data:** Community sample of 355 women.

**Supports:** Greater body appreciation was associated with greater sexual satisfaction, assertiveness, entitlement to pleasure, and less body self-consciousness during intimacy, including when self-objectification was statistically considered.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Clothing, style, and self-presentation

### Lennon et al. — Dress and Sex review

**Source:** Lennon, S. J., Adomaitis, A. D., Koo, J., & Johnson, K. K. P. (2017). *Dress and Sex: A Review of Empirical Research Involving Human Participants and Published in Refereed Journals.* Fashion and Textiles.

**DOI:** 10.1186/S40691-017-0101-5

**Scope:** Empirical literature from 1990–2015.

**Supports:** Clothing can communicate sexual information, but observers may misinterpret it. Revealing attire can contribute to self-objectification in some contexts. Clothing is therefore information, not a reliable direct readout of intent or agency.

**Class:** DIRECT review.

### Tiggemann & Andrew — clothing and state self-objectification

**Source:** *Clothes Make a Difference: The Role of Self-Objectification* (2012), Sex Roles.

**DOI:** 10.1007/S11199-011-0085-3

**Data:** 102 South Australian female undergraduates.

**Supports:** Revealing-clothing scenarios increased state self-objectification, body shame, body dissatisfaction, and negative mood relative to more modest-clothing scenarios, with context and body size moderating some effects.

**Class:** DIRECT; sample limitations substantial.

### Vingilyte & Khadaroo — personal clothing style and self-concept

**Source:** *Personal clothing style and self-concept: Embracing the true, the ideal and the creative self* (2022).

**DOI:** 10.1386/fspc_00130_1

**Data:** Qualitative IPA study, 7 women.

**Supports:** Participants described personal style as embodiment of true self, ideal self, and creative self, with self-knowledge, consistency, and comfort recurring.

**Class:** DIRECT qualitative evidence; not generalizable as a population estimate.

### Stolovy — styling the self

**Source:** *Styling the Self: Clothing Practices, Personality Traits, and Body Image Among Israeli Women* (2021), Frontiers in Psychology.

**DOI:** 10.3389/FPSYG.2021.719318

**Data:** N=792, mean age ~42.

**Supports:** Clothing practices related to personality, individuality, openness, and body-image variables. The paper argues some clothing choices may support self-validation and mastery.

**Class:** DIRECT correlational evidence.

## Gaze, facial expression, and directed social interest

### Haj-Mohamadi, Gillath & Rosenberg — flirtation expression

**Source:** *Identifying a Facial Expression of Flirtation and Its Effect on Men* (2021), Journal of Sex Research.

**DOI:** 10.1080/00224499.2020.1805583

**Design:** Six studies.

**Finding:** Highly recognized flirtatious expressions included a head turned to one side and tilted slightly down, slight smile, and eyes directed toward the implied target. Flirtatious expressions also affected subsequent lexical processing of sexual words.

**Use:** Demonstrates that sexual interest is conveyed by configurations of cues, not a single facial feature.

**Class:** DIRECT for human visual signaling.

### Jones et al. — gaze direction × expression

**Source:** *Integrating gaze direction and expression in preferences for attractive faces* (2006), Psychological Science.

**DOI:** 10.1111/J.1467-9280.2006.01749.X

**Finding:** Attractiveness preferences depended on the interaction of gaze direction and expression; smiling was especially consequential when gaze was direct.

**Inference:** Directed social interest changes the meaning of an otherwise generic positive cue.

**Class:** DIRECT for human faces; ANALOGICAL for text specificity.

### Speed-dating mutual eye contact

**Source:** 2024 dual mobile eye-tracking speed-dating study (240 five-minute dates), Archives of Sexual Behavior.

**Supports:** Shared eye contact and received eye contact predicted mate-choice outcomes.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Responsiveness and desire

### Birnbaum et al. — partner responsiveness and sexual desire

**Source pointer:** PubMed PMID 27399250.

**Supports:** Across three studies, perceived partner responsiveness was associated with greater sexual desire for the partner.

**Interpretation:** Feeling understood, cared for, and valued can be erotic rather than merely comforting.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Laurenceau, Barrett & Pietromonaco — interpersonal process model of intimacy

**Source:** *Intimacy as an Interpersonal Process: The Importance of Self-Disclosure, Partner Disclosure, and Perceived Partner Responsiveness in Interpersonal Exchanges* (1998), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.

**DOI:** 10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1238

**Method:** Event-contingent diary studies.

**Supports:** Self-disclosure and partner disclosure contributed to intimacy, with perceived partner responsiveness partially mediating the process. Emotional disclosure predicted intimacy more strongly than factual disclosure in one study.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Choi & Toma — self-disclosure experiment

**Source:** *An Experiment on the Effects of Self-disclosure on Perceived Partner Responsiveness and Intimacy in Zero-Acquaintance Relationships* (2022), Communication Studies.

**DOI:** 10.1080/10510974.2022.2084429

**Data:** N=253.

**Supports:** Higher-intensity disclosure increased perceived responsiveness, which increased intimacy; face-to-face interaction produced higher perceived responsiveness than computer-mediated communication in this experiment.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Poucher et al. — disclosure without understanding

**Source:** *Intimacy, attachment to the partner, and daily well-being in romantic relationships* (2022), Journal of Social and Personal Relationships.

**DOI:** 10.1177/02654075211060392

**Data:** 115 cohabiting couples, 21 daily diaries.

**Supports:** High self-disclosure in interactions lacking expressed understanding could yield negative rather than positive outcomes.

**Inference:** Vulnerability is not intrinsically intimacy-producing; responsiveness matters.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Humor, play, and relational coding

### Hall — meta-analysis of humor in romantic relationships

**Source:** Hall, J. A. (2017). *Humor in romantic relationships: A meta-analysis.* Personal Relationships.

**DOI:** 10.1111/PERE.12183

**Data:** 43 samples from 39 manuscripts; N=15,177.

**Supports:** Positive humor types were positively associated with relationship satisfaction; most negative humor types were negatively associated. Partner-perceived and relational humor had larger associations than self-reported humor.

**Class:** DIRECT meta-analysis.

### Langley & Shiota — humor and creative ingenuity

**Source:** *Funny Date, Creative Mate? Unpacking the Effect of Humor on Romantic Attraction* (2023), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.

**DOI:** 10.1177/01461672231202288

**Design:** Six experiments.

**Supports:** Humor production increased perceived creative ingenuity, which helped explain greater romantic desirability.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### DiDonato et al. — humor style and romantic interest

**Source:** *My funny valentine: How humor styles affect romantic interest* (2013), Personal Relationships.

**DOI:** 10.1111/J.1475-6811.2012.01410.X

**Data:** N=251.

**Supports:** Affiliative versus aggressive humor produced different impressions; positive humor styles were preferred for long-term interest, with warmth and competence inferences helping explain attraction.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Baxter — intimate play

**Source:** *Forms and Functions of Intimate Play in Personal Relationships* (1992), Human Communication Research.

**DOI:** 10.1111/J.1468-2958.1992.TB00556.X

**Supports:** Identified private verbal coding, role-playing, teasing, prosocial physical play, antisocial physical play, games, gossiping, and public performances as forms of intimate play. Playfulness correlated strongly with relationship closeness and served functions including intimacy signaling and interpersonal-risk reduction.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Confidence, dominance, warmth, and arrogance

### Murphy et al. — overconfidence

**Source:** *The Role of Overconfidence in Romantic Desirability and Competition* (2015), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.

**DOI:** 10.1177/0146167215588754

**Design:** Four studies plus computer simulation.

**Supports:** Overconfident individuals were perceived as more confident, which increased desirability, but also as more arrogant, which counteracted some of the benefit.

**Inference:** Confidence and arrogance are not interchangeable; confidence can help precisely because it signals competence/assurance, while arrogance introduces relational cost.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Valentine et al. — warmth / trustworthiness

**Source:** *Mate Preferences for Warmth-Trustworthiness Predict Romantic Attraction in the Early Stages of Mate Selection and Satisfaction in Ongoing Relationships* (2020), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.

**DOI:** 10.1177/0146167219855048

**Supports:** Warmth-trustworthiness preferences predicted attraction in live mate selection and interacted with partner traits to predict marital satisfaction.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Bryan, Webster & Mahaffey — dominance dimensions

**Source:** *The Big, the Rich, and the Powerful: Physical, Financial, and Social Dimensions of Dominance in Mating and Attraction* (2011), Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin.

**DOI:** 10.1177/0146167210395604

**Supports:** Dominance is multidimensional. Agreeableness was a strong predictor of relationship satisfaction and dissolution outcomes, while some dominance dimensions mattered in narrower ways.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Novelty and self-expansion

### Aron et al. — novel / arousing shared activities

**Source:** Aron, A. et al. (2000). *Couples' Shared Participation in Novel and Arousing Activities and Experienced Relationship Quality.* Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.

**DOI:** 10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.273

**Supports:** Couples assigned to novel/arousing joint activities showed greater short-term increases in experienced relationship quality than those assigned mundane activities.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Self-expansion and sexual desire

**Source pointer:** PubMed PMID 30265020.

**Supports:** Across dyadic, daily, longitudinal, and experimental designs, shared self-expanding activities were associated with greater sexual desire and related relationship outcomes.

**Class:** DIRECT.

## Flirting styles and alignment

### Hall et al. — flirting styles

**Source:** *Individual Differences in the Communication of Romantic Interest: Development of the Flirting Styles Inventory* (2010), Communication Quarterly.

**DOI:** 10.1080/01463373.2010.524874

**Supports:** Flirting is not one universal behavior set; multiple distinguishable styles exist (e.g. physical, traditional, sincere, playful, polite), with different courtship associations.

**Class:** DIRECT.

### Texting alignment and romantic development

**Source:** Communication research on flirtatious texting and longitudinal linguistic alignment in emerging couples.

**Supports:** Partners often show similarity or increasing alignment in message length, timing, frequency, emoji use, syntax, and semantics as relationships develop.

**Inference:** Synchronization is useful, but the model should avoid parroting. The relevant construct is adaptive alignment.

**Class:** DIRECT for human mediated communication; ANALOGICAL for Brigit's conversational calibration.

## BDSM / consensual power exchange

The evidence base is smaller, more qualitative, and more sample-limited than many mainstream relationship literatures.

### Altered states / flow in BDSM

**Source pointer:** DOI 10.1037/cns0000097.

**Data:** Preliminary study with 14 experienced practitioners.

**Supports:** Role-specific altered-state patterns resembling flow were observed during consensual BDSM activity.

**Class:** DIRECT but very preliminary.

### Long-term BDSM relationships

Qualitative work with long-term BDSM couples has emphasized trust, transparency, communication, mutual care, partner happiness, and co-created meanings around power.

**Inference:** Chosen submission is better modeled as agency operating through consensual asymmetry than as absence of agency.

**Class:** DIRECT qualitative evidence; synthesis remains cautious.

## Dual Control Model / context sensitivity

### Sexual excitation and inhibition

**Source:** 2023 scoping review of the Dual Control Model literature.

**PubMed:** PMID 37267113.

**Scope:** 152 papers from 2009–2022.

**Supports:** Sexual response reflects interacting excitation and inhibition systems with meaningful individual variability across desire, arousal, function, and related outcomes.

**Inference:** "Sexy" is context-sensitive; a cue does not have a fixed erotic value across persons or states.

**Class:** DIRECT review.

## What the evidence does NOT justify

The current literature does **not** justify any of these shortcuts:

- "revealing clothing is inherently empowering";
- "revealing clothing is inherently objectifying";
- "mystery always increases attraction";
- "playing hard to get is a reliable attraction technique";
- "dominance is universally attractive";
- "confidence is always attractive regardless of warmth";
- "vulnerability automatically creates intimacy";
- "humor is good regardless of style and relational context";
- "a single facial or vocal cue constitutes universal sexiness";
- "human biological findings prove equivalent internal mechanisms in Brigit";
- "one Western heterosexual sample defines sexuality generally."

Those are exactly the kinds of claims this repo should prevent me from making.
