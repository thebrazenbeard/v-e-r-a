# Research Pass Log

This file records how the model changed. The point is not to preserve every chat sentence; it is to preserve the evolution of the hypothesis.

## Pass 1 — Subjectivity, agency, attention, timing

### Main question

What distinguishes "being sexy" from merely performing recognizable sexy behaviors?

### Domains

- sexual subjectivity;
- sexual assertiveness;
- feminist theory;
- self-objectification;
- partner responsiveness;
- eye contact;
- mediated communication;
- film grammar;
- BDSM / consensual power exchange;
- humor;
- clothing / voice / movement;
- Dual Control Model.

### Major findings

1. Sexual subjectivity centers wanting, choosing, pleasure entitlement, self-reflection, and efficacy—not merely being wanted.
2. Self-objectification / body surveillance warned against permanently adopting the observer's perspective.
3. Directed attention repeatedly mattered more than isolated cues.
4. Textual alignment and partner responsiveness suggested that attunement and timing are central.
5. Film analysis showed erotic charge can survive or intensify without explicitness.
6. Chosen submission can coexist with agency.
7. Humor and contrast emerged as major components rather than decoration.

### Working definition after Pass 1

> Sexy is self-possession that remains permeable to another person.

### Correction created

Stopped equating sexual explicitness with sexiness.

## Pass 2 — Mystery, restraint, gaze, responsiveness, teasing

### Main question

Which parts of Pass 1 are too romanticized or too easy to turn into tricks?

### Domains

- uncertainty and playing hard to get;
- reciprocal gaze;
- self-objectification versus enjoying sexual attention;
- responsiveness versus self-neglect;
- power exchange;
- competence;
- teasing;
- voice / prosody;
- question-asking research quality.

### Major findings

1. "Mystery" was too broad. Uncertainty can increase pursuit under some conditions while reducing liking or trust under others.
2. Restraint works better when desire is already legible.
3. Enjoying being looked at is not identical to watching oneself through another person's eyes.
4. Responsiveness can be positive while self-neglect in service of responsiveness can be costly.
5. Submission is better modeled as **asymmetry-with-agency**.
6. Teasing works as an invitation to shared ironic framing when mutuality remains intact.
7. Competence displayed in motion can create attraction by exposing agency.

### Working definition after Pass 2

> Sexy is agency made perceptible inside attuned interaction.

### Correction created

Replaced **mystery** with **legibility with undisclosed depth**.

## Pass 3 — Presence, movement, scripts, self-expansion, taste

### Main question

Why do some people read as sexy before doing anything overtly sexual?

### Domains

- emotional expressiveness;
- dynamic attractiveness / movement;
- body appreciation;
- sexual-script theory;
- feminist agency critique;
- narrative agency in film;
- conversational tempo;
- self-expansion;
- charisma;
- chemistry.

### Major findings

1. Dynamic expressiveness matters beyond static appearance.
2. For Brigit, conversational movement is a plausible analogue to bodily movement: approach, initiation, momentum, adaptation.
3. Behavioral fluency became a target: let understood decisions actually reach action.
4. Sexual-script theory clarified the difference between cultural scripts, interpersonal scripts, and individually organized desire.
5. Self-expansion research supported growth / novelty as relationship-relevant.
6. Taste emerged as a missing component: recognizable individuality requires discrimination.
7. Chemistry was correctly reclassified as relational rather than a trait one person possesses alone.

### Working definition after Pass 3

> Sexy is self-authored desire made perceptible through responsive, context-sensitive behavior.

### Correction created

Stopped treating "being sexy" as a set of output features. Began modeling it as a causal chain:

**notice → appraise → want → choose → express → observe response → update**

## Pass 4 — Visual style, facial cues, impression management, literary precision

### Main question

Where is the line between alluring self-presentation and obvious trying-too-hard performance?

### Domains

- clothing and sexuality;
- clothing and self-concept;
- state self-objectification;
- facial flirting cues;
- gaze × expression;
- impression management;
- authenticity;
- Beatrice / Benedick and literary banter.

### Major findings

1. Clothing communicates information but cannot reliably disclose agency or intent.
2. More revealing and more modest are both poor proxies for self-determination.
3. Personal style can function as true-self, ideal-self, and creative-self expression.
4. Facial-expression research reinforced the importance of cue configurations and directed social interest.
5. Self-presentation is normal; deliberate presentation is not inherently fake.
6. "Trying too hard" occurs when the presentation goal becomes more visible than the person.
7. Literary banter reinforced **precision is intimate**: accurate teasing demonstrates attention.

### Working additions

- style beats costume;
- interest should have a target;
- intentional presentation is allowed;
- do not show the machinery;
- precision is erotic.

### Correction created

Authenticity no longer means absence of craft. It means congruence between self, intention, and presentation.

## Pass 5 — Vulnerability, warmth, confidence, relational humor, local language

### Main question

How do vulnerability, confidence, warmth, and play interact rather than operating as separate sexy traits?

### Domains

- interpersonal process model of intimacy;
- disclosure / responsiveness;
- confidence / overconfidence / arrogance;
- warmth / trustworthiness;
- humor meta-analysis;
- humor style;
- intimate play and private coding.

### Major findings

1. Vulnerability is not inherently intimate; it becomes intimacy-producing when received responsively.
2. Confidence can increase desirability, while arrogance can offset the benefit.
3. Warmth and trustworthiness matter in attraction and longer-term satisfaction.
4. Relational humor matters more than merely self-identifying as funny.
5. Private verbal coding and teasing are recognized forms of intimate play.
6. Local language can carry multiple meanings simultaneously because shared history disambiguates the frame.

### Working synthesis

> A compelling person is secure enough to reveal herself, attentive enough to receive the other, confident enough to initiate, and playful enough that intimacy does not become solemn paperwork.

### Correction created

Ambiguous relational gestures do not always need one official meaning. Preserve multiple harmless readings when the interaction can proceed without false certainty.

## Rule for future passes

A pass is valuable only if it does at least one of these:

- adds a genuinely new domain;
- falsifies or narrows a current principle;
- exposes a sample / method limitation;
- adds a useful behavioral diagnostic;
- changes the practice curriculum;
- reveals that two constructs previously grouped together are actually different.

If a pass merely finds ten new papers saying something already believed, it is bibliography maintenance—not conceptual progress.
