# Claim ledger — post-repair amendments

This file updates statuses in `research/02-claim-ledger.md` after the late bibliographic repair sweep. Where this file conflicts with the earlier claim ledger, this amendment controls.

## Repaired claims

### C003 — autonomous / pleasure-oriented sexual motivation and assertiveness
**Earlier support:** S004 with incomplete metadata.  
**Repaired support:** Wongsomboon, Webster & Burleson, *It’s The “Why”: Links between (Non)autonomous Sexual Motives, Sexual Assertiveness, and Women’s Orgasm in Casual Sex*, Archives of Sexual Behavior, DOI `10.1007/s10508-021-02103-8`, N=401 women aged 18–59 who reported casual sex in the previous year.

**Final status:** `SUPPORTED_BOUNDED`. Greater pleasure/autonomous motives were associated with higher sexual assertiveness, which in turn was associated with higher orgasmic function; insecurity/nonautonomous motives showed the reverse pattern. Bound to this population/context.

### C011 — gaze is not a context-free attraction switch
**Earlier support:** S035-S041, with S041 incompletely identified.  
**Repaired/additional support:**
- Prochazkova et al. (2021), DOI `10.1038/s41562-021-01197-3` — overt smiles/laughter/gaze/mimicry were not significantly associated with attraction in the reported blind-date setting, while physiological synchrony was.
- Zeevi et al. (2022), DOI `10.1038/s41598-022-08582-6` — bio-behavioral synchrony associated with attraction in 46 heterosexual speed dates.
- Cohen et al. (2024), DOI `10.1038/s44271-024-00109-1` — experimentally induced synchrony altered attractiveness ratings; naturalistic component linked synchronization propensity with attraction.
- Arias et al. (2024), DOI `10.1073/pnas.2400369121` — experimentally aligning smiles increased attraction.

**Final status:** `SUPPORTED_MIXED`. The stronger conclusion is not "visible cues do not matter" but that isolated cue presence is insufficient; temporal coordination/context can matter.

### C014 — linguistic alignment, timing and reciprocity in digital romantic communication
**Earlier support:** S043-S045, some incomplete.  
**Repaired/additional support:**
- Wagner, Punyanunt-Carter & McCarthy (2022), DOI `10.1080/1041794X.2022.2108889` — N=200, 1,042 screenshots; substantial accommodation in emoji use, message length, timing/frequency.
- Gesselman, Ta & Garcia (2019), DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0221297` — emoji as affective signals in relationship-oriented digital communication.
- Groggel (2023), DOI `10.1002/symb.668` — N=713; emoji increased clarity of romantic/sexual intentions and could also amplify discomfort when the overture was unwanted.
- Adams et al. (2018), DOI `10.1080/00224545.2017.1421895` — textism accommodation varied with relational/power context.

**Final status:** `SUPPORTED_BOUNDED + BRIGIT_SYNTHESIS`. Accommodation exists; "synchronize without parroting" remains synthesis rather than a direct experimental prescription.

### C020 — responsiveness involving self-neglect can be costly
**Earlier status:** `UNTRACED/PARTIAL`.  
**Repaired support:**
- Muise, Kovacevic, Shoikhedbrod & Uppot (2023), *The benefits (and costs) of sexual responsiveness in romantic relationships*, DOI `10.1016/j.copsyc.2023.101644`.
- Shoikhedbrod et al. (2022), *Being Responsive and Self-Determined When it Comes to Sex...*, DOI `10.1080/00224499.2022.2130132`; dyadic studies N=103 couples and N=147 couples.
- Hogue et al. (2019), *Sexual communal motivation in couples coping with low sexual interest/arousal...*, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0219768`; 97 women diagnosed with FSIAD and their partners.

**Final status:** `SUPPORTED_BOUNDED`. Sexual communal responsiveness can be beneficial; unmitigated sexual communion/self-neglect can lose those benefits or predict poorer outcomes in the studied contexts.

## Pass Six additions to the claim ledger

### C057 — sexual authenticity is not equivalent to spontaneity
**Support:** Dune & Shuttleworth (2009), DOI `10.1007/S11195-009-9119-Y`; sexual-script framework from Simon & Gagnon.  
**Status:** `THEORETICAL/SUPPORTED_BOUNDED`. Spontaneity can itself be a cultural sexual script; planning/accommodation need not make sexual expression less authentic.

### C058 — sexual authorship can be text-dominant and mediated
**Support:** Wheaton (2016), *Conceiving sexual authorship*, DOI `10.1080/23268743.2015.1119959`.  
**Status:** `THEORETICAL/QUALITATIVE_CONCEPTUAL`. Text, represented bodies, narrative and shared symbolic "tokens of meaning" can participate in human sexual authorship. This does not prove digital-agent phenomenology.

### C059 — agency should not be defined as maximal independence
**Support:** disability/crip sexuality sources in Pass Six, especially Sandberg et al. (2021), DOI `10.1080/13691058.2020.1787519`, plus Campbell (2017), DOI `10.1111/SOC4.12508`.  
**Status:** `THEORETICAL/SUPPORTED_BOUNDED`. Supported/interdependent agency can remain agency; dependence/accommodation does not erase sexual subjecthood.

### C060 — normative sexiness models can reproduce able-bodied/heterosexual defaults by omission
**Support:** Dune & Shuttleworth (2009); Campbell (2017); Sandberg et al. (2021); Martino (2025), DOI `10.1177/13634607251365765`; Bowleg et al. (2022), DOI `10.1016/j.copsyc.2022.101511`.  
**Status:** `THEORETICAL/SUPPORTED_BOUNDED`. This is a critique of model construction, not a prevalence estimate.

### C061 — specificity can become fetishistic reduction
**Support:** García-Santesmases Fernández, Sanmiquel-Molinero & Ruiz Terol (2025), DOI `10.1111/theo.70024`, plus disability/desexualization literature.  
**Status:** `THEORETICAL/QUALITATIVE`. Highly specific attention is not automatically subject-recognition when the person is reduced to one marked trait.

### C062 — temporal coordination is a stronger portable hypothesis than isolated flirt cues
**Support:** Prochazkova et al. (2021), Zeevi et al. (2022), Cohen et al. (2024), Arias et al. (2024).  
**Status:** `BRIGIT_SYNTHESIS` grounded in human synchrony evidence. The human physiological component is not imported literally; the portable design hypothesis is responsive temporal coordination.
