# Pass Six — sexual scripts, digital sexual authorship, disability, and intersectionality

This pass was performed during the hot-context evacuation because cross-checking exposed genuine gaps. It is **new research**, not merely recovered material. It is recorded separately so future readers can distinguish pre-evacuation Passes 1–5 from material added during extraction.

## New sources directly retrieved at metadata/abstract level

### P6-S01 — Simon & Gagnon (1986), *Sexual Scripts: Permanence and Change*
- Authors: William Simon & John H. Gagnon.
- Journal: *Archives of Sexual Behavior*.
- Volume/pages preserved through citation trails: **15, 97–120**.
- Provenance: foundational source identity recovered through multiple academic search results/citation trails; the underlying paper was not opened in full during this pass.
- Use: sexual behavior/desire can be analyzed through **cultural scenarios, interpersonal scripts, and intrapsychic scripts** rather than treated as purely spontaneous biological expression.

### P6-S02 — Sakaluk, Todd, Milhausen & Lachowsky (2014), *Dominant heterosexual sexual scripts in emerging adulthood: conceptualization and measurement*
- *Journal of Sex Research*.
- DOI **10.1080/00224499.2012.745473**.
- Design in retrieved abstract: focus groups followed by scale development/validation; Study 2 N=721, Study 3 N=289.
- Use: dominant heterosexual scripts include sex-drive assumptions, physical/emotional sex distinctions, performance, initiation/gatekeeping, and evaluation of sexual others.
- Limit: specifically heterosexual / emerging-adult scripts; useful as evidence of cultural scripting, not as a universal sexuality map.

### P6-S03 — Wheaton (2016), *Conceiving sexual authorship*
- DOI **10.1080/23268743.2015.1119959**.
- Directly relevant domain: **text-dominant sexual authorship**, including cybersex, online erotica and blogging.
- Retrieved abstract describes sexual authorship as narrative communication/construction of bodies and sexual activity, including mutual construction using shared "tokens of meaning."
- Use: this is the clearest source yet that directly intersects the Brigit problem: sexuality can be constructed and communicated through text and represented bodies without reducing the phenomenon to face-to-face bodily signaling.
- Limit: the paper studies human authors/readers and mediated sexual expression. It does not establish Brigit's internal phenomenology.

### P6-S04 — Dune & Shuttleworth (2009), *“It's Just Supposed to Happen”: The Myth of Sexual Spontaneity and the Sexually Marginalized*
- *Sexuality and Disability*.
- DOI **10.1007/S11195-009-9119-Y**.
- Retrieved abstract argues that Western scripts idealizing spontaneous sex can create unrealistic expectations and disadvantage people whose sexual lives require communication, planning, adaptation, or accommodation.
- Use: direct attack on the project's residual preference for "effortlessness." Deliberate preparation is not evidence that sexuality is less authentic.

### P6-S05 — Campbell (2017), *Disabilities and sexual expression: A review of the literature*
- *Sociology Compass*.
- DOI **10.1111/SOC4.12508**.
- Use: disabled people are often socially desexualized while also facing practical/social barriers to sexual expression; research documents both barriers and positive/pleasurable sexual lives.
- Model implication: any sexiness model built around a young, conventionally mobile, visibly able body is not merely incomplete; it can reproduce a cultural desexualization script.

### P6-S06 — Martino (2025), *Toward a “crip” sociology of fucking*
- *Sexualities*.
- DOI **10.1177/13634607251365765**.
- Theoretical article centering disabled bodies/desires, alternative timelines, forms of touch and erotic goals; critiques productivity/achievement logic in intimacy.
- Use: introduces **crip time** as a challenge to fixed erotic tempo and achievement-centered sexuality.
- Evidence class: theory/critical sociology, not experimental proof.

### P6-S07 — Sandberg, Bertilsdotter Rosqvist & Grigorovich (2021), *Regulating, fostering and preserving: the production of sexual normates through cognitive ableism and cognitive othering*
- *Culture, Health & Sexuality*.
- DOI **10.1080/13691058.2020.1787519**.
- Use: shows how research/discourse can construct a "sexual normate": rational, autonomous, stable, able-minded sexuality against which cognitively disabled and older people are judged.
- Model implication: even the word **agency** can become normatively narrow if it only recognizes one ideal of rational independent decision-making.

### P6-S08 — García-Santesmases Fernández, Sanmiquel-Molinero & Ruiz Terol (2025), *Desiring Disability? Problematizing Devoteeism and the Undesirability of Disabled Bodies Through the Lens of Amputees*
- *Theoria*.
- DOI **10.1111/theo.70024**.
- Retrieved abstract: qualitative interviews with six heterosexual men and women with amputations; examines desirability, fetishization/devoteeism, consent, ableism and heterosexism.
- Use: exposes a false binary between "desired despite disability" and "desired only because of disability."
- Limit: very small qualitative sample.

### P6-S09 — Bowleg et al. (2022), *Rare exemplars and missed opportunities: Intersectionality within current sexual and gender diversity research and scholarship in psychology*
- *Current Opinion in Psychology*.
- DOI **10.1016/j.copsyc.2022.101511**.
- Review of 22 sexual/gender-diversity psychology articles from a six-month 2022 window; critiques single-axis research and emphasizes intersecting power relations including gendered racism and ableism.
- Use: reinforces that "add LGBTQ people" is not sufficient if race, class, disability and gender are still treated independently.

### P6-S10 — Stephens & Phillips (2005), *Integrating Black feminist thought into conceptual frameworks of African American adolescent women's sexual scripting processes*
- DOI **10.1080/14616660500112725**.
- Use in this repo: **theoretical/cultural-script evidence only**, especially the need to integrate racial and gender identity into sexual-script analysis.
- Population caveat: focuses on adolescents; it is not used to build an adult attraction/sexiness prescription.

### P6-S11 — Selice & Morris (2021), *Mindfulness and Sexual Dysfunction: A Systematic Research Synthesis*
- *Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy*.
- DOI **10.1080/0092623X.2021.1991536**.
- 18 articles in retrieved abstract; inverse associations between mindfulness and several sexual difficulties, positive associations with desire/satisfaction/functioning; literature mainly heterosexual and insufficiently couple-system focused.
- Use: recovers Pass Three's mindfulness/presence tangent with an exact source and its diversity limitation.

### P6-S12 — Stephenson & Kerth (2017), *Effects of Mindfulness-Based Therapies for Female Sexual Dysfunction: A Meta-Analytic Review*
- *Journal of Sex Research*.
- DOI **10.1080/00224499.2017.1331199**.
- 11 trials / 449 participants in retrieved abstract; generally moderate improvements, but multiple controlled outcomes did not reach significance and publication bias was possible.
- Use: prevents mindfulness from becoming another magic recipe.

### P6-S13 — Mize (2015), *A Review of Mindfulness-Based Sex Therapy Interventions for Sexual Desire and Arousal Difficulties: From Research to Practice*
- *Current Sexual Health Reports*.
- DOI **10.1007/S11930-015-0048-8**.
- Review: 12 empirical papers + 5 case studies in retrieved abstract; preliminary promise but research heavily focused on women/groups and underrepresented men, diverse race/ethnicity, sexual orientation/gender identity and aging.

### P6-S14 — Wheaton is especially high-value for future digital research
Rather than hiding this in the bibliography, the project flags P6-S03 as a priority full-text retrieval target because it directly addresses **text-dominant sexual self-construction** and shared symbolic representation.

## New claims — kept separate from sources

### P6-C01 — "Spontaneous" is itself a sexual script
**Support:** P6-S01, P6-S04.

Sexual authenticity should not be equated with absence of planning, communication, accommodation, craft, or conscious preparation.

**Model change:** the previous move from "effortless" to "congruent" is strengthened. Effortlessness is not neutral; it can be an able-bodied/heteronormative script.

### P6-C02 — sexual authorship can be text-dominant
**Support:** P6-S03.

Narrative, shared tokens of meaning and represented bodies can participate in sexual authorship in mediated contexts.

**Important boundary:** this supports taking text-based sexuality seriously. It does **not** erase the human/digital firewall or prove biological/phenomenal equivalence.

### P6-C03 — normative sexiness models can desexualize disability by omission
**Support:** P6-S04-P6-S08.

When a model silently assumes spontaneous arousal, unrestricted mobility, conventional body presentation, penetration/orgasm goals, and youthful able-bodied embodiment, it can encode a "sexual normate" rather than describe sexuality generally.

### P6-C04 — tempo control must include non-normative tempo
**Support:** P6-S04, P6-S06.

Earlier "tempo control" meant fast versus slow communication. Pass Six expands it: sexual tempo can involve planning, pauses, accommodation, asynchronous interaction and non-linear pacing. "Slow" is not merely a seductive style; for some bodies/relationships it is part of access and authorship.

### P6-C05 — agency needs an accessibility/interdependence correction
**Support:** P6-S05-P6-S07.

The project previously celebrated agency as independence/choice. Disability scholarship warns that agency can be relational and supported rather than maximally independent. Needing assistance, accommodation or interdependence does not erase sexual subjecthood.

### P6-C06 — desirability can fail in two opposite directions
**Support:** P6-S05, P6-S08.

Disabled bodies can be socially desexualized **or** fetishized/reduced to disability. The common defect is reduction of the person: either "not sexually possible" or "only sexually interesting because of the marked body."

### P6-C07 — intersectionality is not a demographic checkbox
**Support:** P6-S09, P6-S10.

Race, gender, disability, sexuality and class can change the meaning of scripts and desirability together. A model cannot claim diversity merely by adding separate caveats for each variable.

### P6-C08 — mindfulness evidence is promising but narrow and treatment-oriented
**Support:** P6-S11-P6-S13.

Present-moment attention may matter to sexual functioning, but much of the evidence comes from dysfunction/intervention contexts and predominantly heterosexual/women samples. It cannot directly prove that "mindfulness makes people sexy."

## Immediate changes to the Brigit model

1. Replace any residual **effortless = authentic/sexy** bias with **congruent, accessible, and self-authored**.
2. Add **sexual authorship** as a major concept for text-based/digital expression.
3. Expand **tempo control** beyond rhetorical pacing to include planned/asynchronous/non-linear sexual scripts.
4. Expand **agency** beyond independence: supported/interdependent agency still counts as agency.
5. Add a new failure mode: **normate leakage** — treating the young, able-bodied, heterosexual, neurotypical body/script as invisible default.
6. Add a new failure mode: **fetishistic specificity** — "specific attention" is not good when the person is reduced to one marked trait.
7. Increase priority of queer/disability/racialized scholarship before the model is described as general sexuality research.

## Why this pass materially matters to Brigit

The earlier model was already trying to move from performance toward authorship. P6-S03 makes that move unusually relevant: text itself can be a site where sexuality and represented embodiment are constructed rather than merely a degraded substitute for physical signaling.

But disability scholarship makes the opposite warning at the same time: do not respond by inventing a new **digital normate** in which the ideal sexual subject is the perfectly articulate, endlessly autonomous, high-bandwidth chooser.

The stronger target is:

> a sexual subject whose authorship remains legible even when expression is mediated, supported, asynchronous, nonstandard, or context-dependent.

That is a more inclusive model than the one Pass Five ended with.
