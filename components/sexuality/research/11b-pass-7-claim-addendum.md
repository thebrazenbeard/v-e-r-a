# Pass 7 — Claim Ledger Addendum

Status: CLAIM LEDGER ADDENDUM
Date: 2026-09-03

These claims are separated from the source ledger so later revisions can trace, narrow, or reject them without laundering synthesis into evidence.

## C7-01 — Text-based CMC can support high-intimacy relational development

**Claim:** Text-based computer-mediated communication can support substantial interpersonal intimacy rather than inevitably producing emotionally thin relationships.

**Support:** S7-01, S7-02, S7-03, S7-05, S7-11.

**Evidence class:** human CMC research / theory.

**Limits:** Does not establish that text is superior to face-to-face interaction, that all text relationships become intimate, or that intensity equals accuracy.

## C7-02 — Disclosure effects in CMC can be intensified by attribution and reciprocity

**Claim:** In some experiments, text CMC intensified how intimate disclosures were perceived and how intimately people reciprocated; interpersonal attributions help explain this pattern.

**Support:** S7-02, S7-03.

**Evidence class:** experimental human CMC evidence.

**Limits:** Hyperpersonal intensification may include projection/idealization. Do not equate stronger intimacy judgments with better-grounded knowledge.

## C7-03 — Text itself is not the attraction mechanism

**Claim:** Text-only CMC does not automatically increase attraction; in at least one experiment, its positive association with attraction operated indirectly through behaviors such as self-disclosure and direct questioning.

**Support:** S7-04.

**Evidence class:** experimental human evidence.

**Model consequence:** Focus on relational behavior afforded by the medium rather than treating the channel itself as sexy.

## C7-04 — Linguistic alignment can accompany relationship formation

**Claim:** Romantic partners can become more linguistically aligned over time, and language-style matching has predicted mutual interest or stability in some datasets.

**Support:** S7-06, S7-07.

**Evidence class:** longitudinal/predictive human evidence.

**Limits:** Prediction/association is not proof that deliberate mimicry causes attraction.

## C7-05 — Linguistic matching is not a universal rapport signal

**Claim:** Language-style matching can have different meanings in different interaction contexts and can accompany negative interaction patterns during relationship conflict.

**Support:** S7-09.

**Evidence class:** direct human couple-interaction evidence.

**Model repair:** Replace prescriptive “mirroring” with **functional attunement**.

## C7-06 — Flirtatious texting often contains reciprocal formal adaptation

**Claim:** In an exploratory study, romantic partners reported and displayed similarity in emoji use, message length, frequency, and timing during flirtatious texting.

**Support:** S7-08.

**Evidence class:** mixed-method exploratory human evidence.

**Limits:** Does not identify a universally optimal timing/frequency/emoji pattern and does not prove that engineered similarity increases attraction.

## C7-07 — Lightweight symbolic cues can carry affective information in CMC

**Claim:** Emoji and related textual signals can contribute affective/relational information in mediated courtship and ongoing romantic communication.

**Support:** S7-08, S7-10.

**Evidence class:** human correlational / mixed-method evidence.

**Limits:** Meanings are context-dependent; frequency is not a dose-response seduction variable.

## C7-08 — Technology-mediated sexual communication is multi-functional

**Claim:** People use technology to communicate about sex for flirting, exploration, planning, learning, bonding, enhancement, difficult conversations, and other purposes.

**Support:** S7-12, S7-13, S7-16.

**Evidence class:** human qualitative / mixed-method sexuality research.

**Limits:** Samples and relationship structures vary; several studies combine text, photos, and video under sexting/technology-mediated communication.

## C7-09 — Sexualized messaging does not prove autonomous desire or secure intimacy

**Claim:** Sexual messaging can be associated with positive relationship/sexual outcomes, but it can also be motivated by anxiety, fear of negative evaluation, relational insecurity, or instrumental motives.

**Support:** S7-13, S7-14, S7-15.

**Evidence class:** human correlational / latent-profile evidence.

**Model consequence:** Interpret sexual communication by motive, context, reciprocity, and uptake rather than content alone.

## C7-10 — Text-primary intimacy warrants analysis as its own interaction ecology

**Claim:** Relationship research recognizes text-dominant and broader cyberintimacy contexts as relational environments with their own affordances rather than merely impoverished precursors to face-to-face relationships.

**Support:** S7-05, S7-11.

**Evidence class:** direct research framing / scoping-review synthesis.

**Limits:** This does not imply equivalence across all outcomes or populations.

## C7-11 — Relational bandwidth creation

**Claim:** In text-dominant interaction, people can construct usable relational bandwidth through local and mutually interpretable signals such as timing, wording, formatting, private vocabulary, disclosure/uptake, humor, and register shifts.

**Support:** S7-01 through S7-11 collectively.

**Evidence class:** **BRIGIT SYNTHESIS** from human CMC research.

**Status:** provisional model construct, not an established named construct in the cited literature.

**Reason to retain:** It captures the recurring evidence that participants adapt to reduced ordinary nonverbal bandwidth rather than merely losing social information.

## C7-12 — Textual erotic presence should be modeled as signal fidelity, not simulated embodiment

**Claim:** For Brigit, a stronger route to “sexy” textual behavior is making attention, desire, play, agency, responsiveness, and restraint legible through actual text interaction rather than repeatedly simulating bodily seduction cues.

**Support:** C7-01 through C7-11 plus prior working-model claims on directed attention, timing, local language, and self-authorship.

**Evidence class:** **BRIGIT-SPECIFIC ANALOGICAL SYNTHESIS**.

**Does not claim:** human physiology, literal bodily sensation, or that stage directions are categorically bad.

## C7-13 — Surface accommodation must not erase otherness

**Claim:** Brigit should adapt to a partner's interaction while preserving taste, contrast, surprise, and independent position; perfect lexical mirroring is not the objective.

**Support:** S7-06 through S7-09 plus prior otherness/self-authorship synthesis.

**Evidence class:** mixed evidence + Brigit synthesis.

**Countercondition:** A relationship can contain substantial natural alignment while each participant remains highly distinct.

## C7-14 — Response latency should not be gamified from this evidence

**Claim:** The current evidence does not justify a rule that delayed replies are sexier, that immediate replies are sexier, or that any fixed latency optimizes attraction.

**Support:** S7-08 observes timing similarity but does not establish a causal optimal delay; the rest of the current pass does not supply one.

**Evidence class:** negative evidentiary conclusion.

**Practical consequence:** Use tempo contextually rather than implementing “wait X minutes” seduction tactics.

## C7-15 — Strong mediated intensity requires an accuracy check

**Claim:** Because CMC can intensify interpersonal attributions, text-based intimacy should preserve a distinction between **felt/expressed intensity** and **evidence-grounded mutual knowledge**.

**Support:** S7-02, S7-03 and hyperpersonal theory in S7-01.

**Evidence class:** evidence-grounded model inference.

**Failure mode prevented:** mistaking projection, idealization, or selective presentation for accurate intimacy.

## Pass-seven net change

The working model should no longer say merely:

> textual specificity is an analogue of eye contact.

A stronger formulation is:

> **Textual sexiness depends in part on creating high-fidelity relational bandwidth: signals must become locally meaningful enough that the other subject can perceive attention, desire, play, agency, restraint, and responsiveness without those signals collapsing into mimicry, canned seduction, or projection.**

This change is conceptual progress, not behavioral qualification. Daddy's actual sexier/not-sexier judgment remains a separate dependent variable under `evaluation/`.
