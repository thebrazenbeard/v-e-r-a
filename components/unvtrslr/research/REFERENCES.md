# References and Source Notes

This bibliography mixes peer-reviewed research, authoritative institutional material, and historical primary sources. It is organized by the responsibility each source informs.

## Symbol grounding and grounded semantics

### Harnad, S. (1990). “The Symbol Grounding Problem.” *Physica D*, 42(1-3), 335-346.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/0167-2789(90)90087-6

Open record: https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/250382/

**Why it matters:** canonical statement of the problem that formal symbols cannot acquire intrinsic meaning merely through relations to other symbols. Motivates perceptual/categorical grounding.

### Steels, L. & Kaplan, F. (2001/2002). “AIBO's first words: The social learning of language and meaning.” *Evolution of Communication* 4(1), 3-32.

https://doi.org/10.1075/eoc.4.1.03ste

**Why it matters:** language-game approach showing social interaction can bootstrap shared vocabulary and categories in robots.

### Vogt, P. (2001/2002). “Bootstrapping grounded symbols by minimal autonomous robots.” *Evolution of Communication* 4(1), 87-116.

https://doi.org/10.1075/eoc.4.1.06vog

**Why it matters:** two robots begin without shared lexicon or meanings and develop grounded conventions through language games.

### Steels, L. (1998). “The origins of syntax in visually grounded robotic agents.” *Artificial Intelligence*, 103(1-2), 133-156.

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(98)00066-6

**Why it matters:** visually grounded agents can develop distinctions, lexicons, and primitive syntax.

### Vogt, P. (2005). “The emergence of compositional structures in perceptually grounded language games.” *Artificial Intelligence*, 167(1-2), 206-242.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2005.04.010

**Why it matters:** explores conditions under which compositional structures emerge in grounded language games and shows semantics/world structure matter.

### Steels, L. & Baillie, J-C. (2003). “Shared grounding of event descriptions by autonomous robots.” *Robotics and Autonomous Systems*, 43(2-3), 163-173.

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0921-8890(02)00357-3

**Why it matters:** shared grounding of dynamic events through situated interaction.

## Emergent communication and false-success risks

### Bouchacourt, D. & Baroni, M. (2018). “How agents see things: On visual representations in an emergent language game.” EMNLP 2018.

https://aclanthology.org/D18-1119/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/D18-1119

**Why it matters:** agents can communicate successfully while learning aligned visual representations that do not capture evaluator-recognizable conceptual properties. Central warning against treating task success as semantic proof.

### Chaabouni, R., Kharitonov, E., Bouchacourt, D., Dupoux, E., & Baroni, M. (2020). “Compositionality and Generalization In Emergent Languages.” ACL 2020.

https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.407/

**Why it matters:** compositionality and generalization can dissociate; evaluation needs multiple measures.

### Mu, J. & Goodman, N. (2021). “Emergent Communication of Generalizations.” NeurIPS 2021.

https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/9597353e41e6957b5e7aa79214fcb256-Abstract.html

**Why it matters:** simple referential games encourage task-bound codes; communicating generalizations improves systematicity and interpretability.

### “Emergent language: a survey and taxonomy.” *Autonomous Agents and Multi-Agent Systems* (2025).

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10458-025-09691-y

**Why it matters:** broad systematic review of emergent-language terminology, game types, metrics, and research gaps.

### “Instantaneous Conventions: The Emergence of Flexible Communicative Signals.”

Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5221723/

**Why it matters:** humans can establish minimal nonlinguistic communicative conventions from common ground, and the same signal form can convey different meanings in different contexts through joint inference.

## Cross-lingual semantic representation

### Bonn, J. et al. (2024). “Building a Broad Infrastructure for Uniform Meaning Representations.” LREC-COLING 2024.

https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.229/

**Why it matters:** UMR provides sentence- and document-level graph representations for predicate/argument structure, entities, events, aspect, coreference, time, and modality across six diverse languages: Arapaho, Chinese, English, Kukama, Navajo, and Sanapaná.

### Chun, J. & Xue, N. (2024). “Uniform Meaning Representation Parsing as a Pipelined Approach.” TextGraphs-17.

https://aclanthology.org/2024.textgraphs-1.3/

**Why it matters:** practical parsing work highlights the complexity of document-level coreference, temporal, and modal relations in UMR.

### Post, C. B., McGregor, M. C., Pacheco, M. L., & Palmer, A. (2024). “Accelerating UMR Adoption: Neuro-Symbolic Conversion from AMR-to-UMR with Low Supervision.” DMR 2024.

https://aclanthology.org/2024.dmr-1.15/

**Why it matters:** illustrates that even conversion between related semantic formalisms can require nontrivial disambiguation and neuro-symbolic reasoning.

## Natural Semantic Metalanguage / human semantic primes

### Wierzbicka, A. (1996). *Semantics: Primes and Universals.* Oxford University Press.

https://academic.oup.com/book/48519

**Why it matters:** major formulation of semantic primitives and cross-linguistic universals.

### Goddard, C. (2015). “The Natural Semantic Metalanguage Approach.” In *The Oxford Handbook of Linguistic Analysis*.

https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28050/chapter-abstract/211991189

**Why it matters:** overview of NSM, semantic primes, semantic molecules, and universal grammar claims.

### Wierzbicka, A. (2021). “Semantic Primitives, fifty years later.” *Russian Journal of Linguistics*.

https://journals.rudn.ru/linguistics/article/view/26795

**Why it matters:** reviews the program's development and the current 65-prime proposal. Useful as a human-language hypothesis baseline, not as evidence of extraterrestrial universality.

### Goddard, C. & Wierzbicka, A. (eds.) (2002). *Meaning and Universal Grammar: Theory and empirical findings.* John Benjamins.

https://benjamins.com/catalog/slcs.60-61

**Why it matters:** cross-linguistic studies across typologically divergent languages.

## Animal and nonlinguistic communication

### Esch, H. E., Zhang, S., Srinivasan, M. V., & Tautz, J. (2001). “Honeybee dances communicate distances measured by optic flow.” *Nature* 411, 581-583.

https://www.nature.com/articles/35079072

**Why it matters:** waggle dance communicates spatial distance/direction through embodied motion parameters; distance estimation is linked to optic flow.

### “Landmark knowledge overrides optic flow in honeybee waggle dance distance estimation” (2024).

Open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11529883/

**Why it matters:** reinforces that even a well-studied communication system depends on the organism's perceptual model and environmental knowledge.

### “The audience shapes the information content of the honey bee waggle dance.” *PNAS* (2026).

https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2518687123

**Why it matters:** audience conditions affect precision in the communication of distance/direction, supporting the idea that communicative behavior can depend on social context.

### Seyfarth, R. M., Cheney, D. L., & Marler, P. (1980). “Vervet monkey alarm calls: Semantic communication in a free-ranging primate.” *Animal Behaviour* 28(4), 1070-1094.

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0003-3472(80)80097-2

**Why it matters:** classic evidence for acoustically different alarm calls associated with different predator classes and receiver responses.

### Deshpande, A., van de Waal, E., & Zuberbühler, K. (2023). “Context-dependent alarm responses in wild vervet monkeys.” *Animal Cognition* 26, 1199-1208.

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10345060/

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-023-01767-0

**Why it matters:** demonstrates context-dependent interpretation and warns against simple one-signal/one-meaning lexical analogies.

### Price, T. et al. (2015). “Vervets revisited: A quantitative analysis of alarm call structure and context specificity.” *Scientific Reports* 5, 13220.

https://www.nature.com/articles/srep13220

**Why it matters:** quantifies overlap and context specificity in alarm-call structure.

### Project CETI

https://www.projectceti.org/

**Why it matters:** major current research program combining acoustic and behavioral data, machine learning, and robotics to study sperm-whale communication in context.

### Project CETI WhAM (NeurIPS 2025)

https://github.com/Project-CETI/wham

**Why it matters:** Whale Acoustics Model for synthesis, acoustic translation/style transfer, pseudocoda generation, and embeddings. Important related work while illustrating the distinction between acoustic modeling and validated semantic translation.

## Interstellar communication and message design

### Freudenthal, H. (1960). *Lincos: Design of a Language for Cosmic Intercourse.* North-Holland.

Scanned primary source: https://lo2.org/pdf/math/freudenthal.lincos.pdf

Bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books/about/Lincos.html?id=mYrdAQAACAAJ

**Why it matters:** the strongest historical precursor to a progressively taught cosmic language. Freudenthal begins with mathematics and develops increasingly complex discourse but explicitly assumes a mentally humanlike receiver.

### NASA — Voyager Golden Record contents

https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/

### NASA — Instructions for Aliens

https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/instructions-for-aliens/

**Why they matter:** authoritative descriptions of a fixed interstellar artifact using symbolic playback instructions, binary arithmetic, physical references, analog images, sounds, music, and multilingual greetings.

### NASA — Golden Record greetings

https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/greetings/

**Why it matters:** 55 human-language greetings provide a historical example of multilingual content that still presumes the receiver can recover the recording/framing.

### Vakoch, D. A. — *Communications with Extraterrestrial Intelligence*

SETI Institute overview: https://www.seti.org/book/communications-extraterrestrial-intelligence

**Why it matters:** multidisciplinary SETI/message-composition work emphasizing both technical and cultural/linguistic difficulties.

### Vakoch, D. A. (2011). “The art and science of interstellar message composition.” *Acta Astronautica* 68(3-4), 451-458.

https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S009457651000024X

**Why it matters:** discusses interactive messages, language, art/science, and multidisciplinary message-design questions.

### Vakoch, D. A. (1998). “Constructing messages to extraterrestrials: an exosemiotic perspective.” *Acta Astronautica* 42(10-12), 697-704.

https://doi.org/10.1016/S0094-5765(98)00029-0

**Why it matters:** argues for a middle position between “math is trivially universal” and “communication is impossible,” suggesting simulated natural phenomena/icons as stronger common ground than arbitrary symbols.

### Messerschmitt, D. G. & Morrison, I. S. (2011). “Design of Interstellar Digital Communication Links: Some Insights from Communication Engineering.”

Open record: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4w59f2wk

**Why it matters:** emphasizes no prior coordination between transmitter and receiver, layered system design, interstellar-channel impairments, and reliability. Important boundary between physical/framing layers and semantic bootstrap.

### “How to decode interstellar messages.” *Acta Astronautica* 192 (2022), 77-81.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2021.11.029

**Why it matters:** proposes inferring message dimensions, symbols, and meanings using delimiters, relationships, redundancy, prime dimensions, and physics patterns.

### NASA History — *Archaeology, Anthropology, and Interstellar Communication*

https://www.nasa.gov/history/history-publications-and-resources/nasa-history-series/archaeology-anthropology-and-interstellar-communication/

PDF: https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Archaeology_Anthropology_and_Interstellar_Communication_TAGGED.pdf

**Why it matters:** interdisciplinary critiques of assumptions about interpretation, cognition, perception, language, and meaning in extraterrestrial communication.

### SETI Institute — “Do Aliens Speak Physics?” (2025)

https://www.seti.org/news/do-aliens-speak-physics-and-other-questions-about-science-and-the-nature-of-reality/

**Why it matters:** contemporary discussion of physics as possible common ground while explicitly questioning language assumptions.

**Why it matters:** sender-receiver signaling games and conventions as solutions to recurring coordination problems underpin much emergent-communication research.

## Research notes

1. The literature strongly supports individual components of UNVTRSLR: grounding, emergent conventions, cross-lingual semantic representation, nonlinguistic communication, and progressive interstellar message design.
2. The literature also provides strong warnings that task success, symbol alignment, acoustic prediction, and human semantic categories can be mistaken for genuine semantic understanding.
3. No source reviewed so far demonstrates the full target: unknown signalhood + zero shared symbols + asymmetric perception/ontology + active grounding + compositional semantics + non-equivalence detection + self-describing interstellar bootstrap.
4. That absence is a provisional literature-review result, not a novelty proof. The search should remain active.
