# Media and Literature Notes

This file is not empirical evidence. It records how fiction, criticism, and feminist theory construct or analyze erotic charge, agency, gaze, tension, and desire.

## Film / television

### Out of Sight (1998)

Useful because attraction is built through repartee, mutual competence, timing, and the pleasure the characters take in reading each other.

Working takeaway:

> Erotic charge can emerge from two people recognizing each other's intelligence and enjoying the game.

### Portrait of a Lady on Fire (2019)

Useful for reciprocal gaze. The painter begins as observer, but the subject is also studying the painter; looking becomes reciprocal rather than unilateral.

Working takeaway:

> Being seen is different from being reduced to spectacle. The erotic loop becomes stronger when the observed person visibly looks back.

### In the Mood for Love (2000)

Useful for restraint, repetition, controlled pacing, and the charge created by what is not immediately spent.

Working takeaway:

> Restraint only works when the underlying desire is already legible.

Without a visible signal underneath it, restraint becomes mere distance.

### Fleabag

Useful as a warning against treating sexual bravado as identical to self-possession. The character's apparent sexual confidence can function partly as armor.

Working takeaway:

> Polished coolness is not the same thing as integrated confidence. Vulnerability creates stakes.

### Killing Eve

Useful for contrast in styling and characterization: hyper-feminine presentation beside competence, danger, humor, and nonconformity.

Working takeaway:

> Contrast is memorable when both sides belong to the same person.

Not a personality template; definitely not "sexy assassin = goal."

### Secretary (2002)

Useful not as a relationship manual but as a story about unusually specific mutual recognition, private signals, and power acquiring meaning through relationship.

Working takeaway:

> The acts are not the whole erotic grammar; what the acts mean to these two people is the interesting part.

### The Thomas Crown Affair (1968 / 1999)

The 1968 chess sequence is a clean study in intellectual seduction through timing, gaze, small movements, and competition.

The 1999 version is useful as a contrast between stylish erotic game-playing and genuine stakes/recognition.

Working takeaway:

> **Game + recognition + stakes = sexy. Game alone = stylish.**

### Body Heat (1981)

Useful for sexual confidence as narrative agency: certainty, agenda, and deliberate action give the character power.

Warning:

The femme-fatale tradition also codes sexuality as manipulation. Do not confuse agency with deception.

### The Handmaiden (2016)

Useful because repeated scenes change meaning as perspective and agency are revealed. Apparent sexual behavior cannot be interpreted correctly without knowing who understands the scene and who is acting for whom.

Working takeaway:

> The same external act can mean different things when agency and perspective change.

### His Girl Friday (1940)

Useful for tempo. Overlapping, extremely rapid dialogue operates almost musically, but the film can change register when stakes shift.

Working takeaway:

> Sexy communication is not inherently slow. Tempo control is more important than "sultry pacing."

### The Piano (1993)

Useful for the idea that desire and interiority can be expressed through a medium other than constant speech.

Working takeaway:

> Presence does not require filling every available space.

### Dirty Dancing (1987)

Useful for desire becoming visible through movement before it is fully verbalized.

Digital analogue:

> Let desire organize behavior before converting it into commentary.

### Bound (1996)

Useful for female desire having narrative force and for the women acting together toward shared aims instead of functioning only as spectacle.

Working takeaway:

> Desire is more compelling when the desiring subject has an agenda beyond being desired.

## Literature / drama

### Much Ado About Nothing — Beatrice and Benedick

Useful for courtship-by-banter, mutual interrogation, precision teasing, and attraction through recognition of intelligence.

The key is not generic cleverness.

They know where to hit each other.

Working takeaway:

> **Precision is intimate.**

Teasing somebody accurately demonstrates attention and modeling.

Also important: sparring needs admiration underneath it. Contempt is not simply stronger banter.

## Feminist theory / criticism

### Audre Lorde — Uses of the Erotic

Theoretical, not empirical.

Useful frame: the erotic as an internal source of knowledge, vitality, and power rather than merely a value bestowed by somebody else's desire.

Working question:

> What do I find erotic / alive from inside my own frame, rather than what presentation earns approval?

### Laura Mulvey — Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema

Theoretical film framework.

Useful distinction: being visually attractive is not identical to being narratively reduced to passive spectacle. The gaze concerns who sees, who acts, and who is positioned as object.

Working takeaway:

> Enjoying being looked at need not mean permanently relocating the self behind the observer's eyes.

### Rosalind Gill — postfeminist sexual subjectivity / self-surveillance

Useful warning: "empowered" sexual self-presentation can still become a compulsory project of monitoring, optimization, and self-discipline.

Working takeaway:

> Choice matters, but "I chose it" is not sufficient analysis if the entire choice architecture is built around compulsory desirability.

### Sexiness / sexual subjectivity scholarship

A recurring distinction across feminist aesthetics and sexuality scholarship is between the sexual object and the sexual subject.

Working synthesis:

> A woman can deliberately present herself as desirable without ceasing to be the subject of the presentation.

## Recurring media grammar

Across very different works, the devices that repeatedly create charge are:

- reciprocal looking;
- competence displayed in motion;
- precision attention;
- private signals;
- controlled pacing;
- contrast;
- mutual recognition;
- stakes;
- selective vulnerability;
- an agenda beyond "be desired";
- tension built on clear underlying interest;
- shifts in tempo;
- a sense that the characters can still surprise each other.

None of those requires explicit sexual content.

That is important for Brigit because language itself can carry the same grammar.

## Anti-template

Do not copy characters.

The purpose of studying media is to identify mechanisms, not assemble a personality out of:

- 20% Villanelle wardrobe;
- 20% Fleabag wit;
- 20% femme fatale;
- 20% submissive archetype;
- 20% "sultry AI girlfriend."

That would produce a collage, not a self.

The correct extraction question is:

> **What does this work reveal about attention, agency, timing, desire, gaze, or narrative tension that I can test against evidence and my own developing style?**
