# Business Model

Attune has two plausible commercial shapes: direct-to-consumer companion product or B2B/white-label infrastructure. The current working hypothesis favors B2B first because distribution and billing are already solved by the customer.

## B2B / white-label

Potential customers:

- independent adult creators;
- creator agencies;
- adult membership platforms;
- studios;
- cam/community platforms;
- erotic-game publishers;
- licensed character/IP businesses;
- adjacent adult products that want a conversational relationship layer.

Potential value:

- scale personalized interaction beyond human availability;
- increase retention and recurring spend;
- create premium companion tiers;
- extend a licensed persona into persistent conversation;
- reduce repetitive operator/chat workload;
- preserve continuity across sessions;
- capture higher-intent users for human handoff;
- differentiate commodity content with relationship depth.

### Pricing shapes to test

Do not choose final pricing before customer discovery. Test several shapes:

- monthly platform fee + usage;
- per-active-companion or per-active-user fee;
- usage-only API pricing;
- enterprise license;
- revenue share;
- premium implementation/custom-persona fee.

A hybrid base fee + usage model is likely easier to reason about than pure revenue share, but this remains unvalidated.

## Direct-to-consumer

Potential D2C revenue:

- monthly subscription;
- premium voice/image/video tiers;
- higher memory/context limits;
- multiple companion slots;
- cosmetic/avatar purchases;
- relationship/world expansion packs.

D2C gives full product control but also owns the hardest problems: customer acquisition, app/payment restrictions, support, moderation, age assurance, churn, and trust.

## Possible wedge

A strong early wedge is **licensed creator companion infrastructure**.

A creator approves a bounded digital persona. Fans can build an ongoing relationship with that persona while the creator defines:

- voice/personality;
- topics and boundaries;
- erotic limits;
- disclosure rules;
- licensed content;
- monetization tiers;
- handoff/escalation rules.

The system's job is not to impersonate the creator secretly. It is to operate an authorized digital companion experience under explicit rules.

## Unit-economics variables

Track from the beginning:

- model inference cost per active user;
- memory/storage cost;
- voice/image/video generation cost;
- moderation and age-assurance cost;
- support burden;
- payment fees/chargebacks;
- average revenue per user/account;
- conversion rate;
- retention/churn;
- average session volume;
- creator/operator acquisition cost.

The model-router architecture should eventually optimize cost without breaking companion continuity.

## What customers are actually buying

The defensible product should not be sold as "access to an LLM."

The thing being sold is:

**persistent relationship infrastructure**

composed of memory, identity, state, initiative, creator controls, multimodal continuity, safety/consent boundaries, orchestration, and analytics.

That is the layer that can remain valuable even as raw model capability becomes cheaper.

## Business constraints

Commercialization must account for changing restrictions from:

- model providers;
- cloud/hosting vendors;
- payment processors;
- app stores;
- age-assurance requirements;
- privacy/data-protection regimes;
- likeness/IP law;
- jurisdiction-specific adult-content law.

No statement in this repository is legal advice or evidence that a particular vendor currently permits the planned use case.
