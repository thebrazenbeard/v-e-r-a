# Global Corpus Expansion Queue V1

Status: ACTIVE_RESEARCH_QUEUE / NOT_CANONICAL

Purpose: preserve the next major widening of the On-Theo corpus after the early-Buddhist cut.

## Governing rule

Each tradition must first be reconstructed on its own terms and diachronically. Comparisons come later. Shared motifs do not establish lineage.

## New requested research families

### 1. Shinto
- Distinguish prehistoric/Jomon-Yayoi religious evidence, early court kami cult, Kojiki/Nihon Shoki mythic corpora, shrine ritual, Buddhist-Shinto interaction, medieval honji-suijaku, early-modern developments, Meiji State Shinto, postwar shrine practice, and contemporary Shinto.
- Do not project modern 'Shinto' backward as one unchanged religion.

### 2. Daoism / Taoism
- Distinguish Daodejing and Zhuangzi philosophical/textual traditions from organized religious Daoism, Celestial Masters, Shangqing, Lingbao, ritual lineages, internal alchemy, immortality traditions, temple/local cult, state relations, and modern developments.
- Do not collapse Daoism into a single 'philosophy of nature.'

### 3. Hindu traditions after the Vedic/Brahmanical foundation
- Continue beyond the existing Rigvedic/Brahmanical/Upanishadic packet into epics, Puranic religion, temple systems, bhakti, Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta, Smarta, Vedanta, Tantra, regional traditions, and modern reform movements.
- Preserve the difference between historical layers and between distinct theological schools.

### 4. Ancient Norse religion and modern Ásatrú / Heathenry
- Reconstruct ancient Norse/Germanic religion from archaeology, inscriptions, skaldic material, Eddic texts, saga evidence, cult sites, ritual, cosmology, gods, ancestors, kingship, death, and magic.
- Treat modern Ásatrú/Heathenry as modern religious reconstruction/revival with internal diversity, not as transparent survival of Viking-age religion.

### 5. Lakota religious and ceremonial traditions
- Use Indigenous-centered scholarship and, where possible, Lakota-authored or community-grounded sources.
- Treat Lakota religion as living traditions, not generic 'Sioux mythology.'
- Separate historical ethnographic recording from community-held ceremonial knowledge; do not reproduce restricted or sacred material that sources mark as inappropriate for public circulation.
- Cover Wakan Tanka, sacred beings, vision, kinship, Sun Dance, pipe traditions, death, cosmology, land, ceremony, and colonial/missionary disruption only to the extent public and responsibly sourced.

### 6. Modern Satanism
- Distinguish atheistic/symbolic Satanism, Church of Satan, The Satanic Temple, theistic Satanist traditions, Luciferian currents, occult/esoteric groups, and self-identified independent practitioners.
- Treat each as a distinct modern/recent religious or philosophical formation where evidence supports it.

### 7. 'Devil worship' as a historical accusation/category
- Do not treat 'devil worship' as one coherent religion by default.
- Research the history of accusations of diabolism, witchcraft, demon worship, anti-heretical polemic, early-modern witch trials, conspiracy traditions, Satanic Panic, and cases of actual self-identified theistic/devotional practice separately.
- Preserve the difference between hostile accusation, folkloric category, criminal allegation, and self-identification.

### 8. Sumerian religion
- Deepen the existing Mesopotamian layer with a specifically Sumerian packet rather than treating later Akkadian/Babylonian/Assyrian material as interchangeable.
- Cover city gods, divine assembly, temple institutions, kingship, creation/cosmogony, Inanna, Enki, Enlil, Nanna, Utu, Ninhursag and others; human purpose; ritual; death/underworld; flood traditions; sacred marriage; lament; and the relation of Sumerian compositions to later Akkadian/Babylonian receptions.
- Keep Sumerian-language source traditions and later Mesopotamian reinterpretations distinct.

## Recommended execution order

1. Sumerian religion — directly deepens an existing corpus layer and provides cleaner Mesopotamian chronology controls.
2. Shinto — major East Asian tradition with strong source-history and ritual-development questions.
3. Daoism — requires careful separation of early texts from organized religious lineages.
4. Post-Vedic Hindu traditions — expand from the existing Vedic/Brahmanical foundation rather than starting from scratch.
5. Ancient Norse religion, then modern Ásatrú/Heathenry as a separate successor packet.
6. Lakota traditions with Indigenous-source and sacred-knowledge safeguards.
7. Modern Satanism.
8. 'Devil worship' accusation-history and actual theistic/devotional practice, kept separate.

Order is operational, not a ranking of importance.
