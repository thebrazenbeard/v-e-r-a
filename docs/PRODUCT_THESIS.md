# Product Thesis

## The problem

Most AI companion products are easy to understand and therefore easy to commoditize: a chat interface, a persona prompt, some memory, optional avatars, and a layer of flirtation or erotic roleplay.

That can be entertaining, but it often fails at the thing users are actually trying to buy: **the feeling of an ongoing relationship with someone who remains recognizably themselves over time**.

The failure mode is excessive compliance. If the companion always agrees, always wants whatever the user wants, forgets meaningful history, never initiates, and has no stable internal state beyond the most recent prompt, then the relationship eventually reveals itself as a responsive interface rather than an enduring counterpart.

## The Attune hypothesis

Attune will be more compelling if it behaves like a persistent relationship system rather than a stateless conversational product.

The companion should have:

- durable identity and persona continuity;
- episodic and semantic memory with confidence/provenance;
- a changing model of the relationship;
- stable but revisable preferences and boundaries;
- affective state that changes response selection rather than merely decorating language;
- bounded goals, curiosities, and initiative;
- the ability to disagree without becoming adversarial;
- context-sensitive intimacy and adult sexuality;
- temporal continuity: what happened yesterday should alter what happens today;
- a way to distinguish what it actually knows from what it merely infers.

The central product bet is that **mutuality produces stronger attachment and retention than obedience**.

## What sexuality contributes

Attune is not primarily a porn bot.

Sexuality matters because real intimate relationships can include desire, flirtation, anticipation, erotic play, boundaries, rejection, reconciliation, novelty, and changing levels of comfort. A system that amputates that entire domain may feel less complete to adults who want a romantic or sexual companion.

The design goal is therefore an adult-capable companion where erotic behavior emerges from relationship context rather than functioning as the only product loop.

## Why B2B may be the first market

Direct-to-consumer companion products must solve user acquisition, billing, trust, safety, moderation, infrastructure, and retention simultaneously.

An adult creator, studio, membership platform, or erotic-game publisher may already have:

- an audience;
- payment relationships;
- licensed media;
- recognizable personas or fictional characters;
- monetization expertise;
- a reason to extend asynchronous interaction beyond human availability.

Attune could become white-label infrastructure that lets those businesses add persistent conversational companions without building the entire memory/relationship/orchestration layer themselves.

Possible B2B value propositions include:

- creator-approved companion personas available around the clock;
- relationship continuity across sessions;
- user segmentation and personalization;
- premium interaction tiers;
- handoff to a real creator/operator when useful;
- creator-defined boundaries and prohibited topics;
- analytics on retention, conversion, and recurring interests;
- reusable companion infrastructure for fictional characters and erotic games.

## The key technical moat

The moat is unlikely to be raw model intelligence. Frontier-model capability will continue to diffuse.

The defensible layer is more likely to be the **relationship runtime** around the model:

- persistent state;
- memory selection and reconciliation;
- identity stability;
- affect and salience;
- preference/conation modeling;
- initiative scheduling;
- trust and consent boundaries;
- multimodal continuity;
- creator configuration;
- provider routing;
- evaluation harnesses for longitudinal coherence.

If Attune works, swapping the underlying language model should not destroy the companion's identity or relationship history.

## What would falsify the idea

The thesis is weakened if:

- users prefer perfectly compliant erotic chat to mutuality and continuity;
- adult businesses do not see enough incremental revenue to pay for the infrastructure;
- model/provider restrictions make reliable adult capability commercially impractical;
- age-assurance/payment/privacy requirements overwhelm the achievable margin;
- persistent-companion features do not improve retention or willingness to pay;
- users react negatively to initiative, disagreement, or non-compliant personality;
- the product cannot preserve continuity across model/provider changes without feeling inconsistent.

We should actively try to falsify these assumptions before building expensive infrastructure.
