# Media / mechanism ledger

This file preserves *why* each media example was useful. These are interpretive examples, not empirical demonstrations. The same work can model a mechanism and also expose a failure mode.

## Film / television

### M001 — *Out of Sight* (1998)
**Mechanisms:** repartee, verbal competence, mutual attention, timing, confidence, anticipation.

The useful feature is that much of the erotic charge is carried by conversation and the sense that each person is mentally tracking the other. It demonstrates that explicit sexual content is not required for a scene to read as sexually charged.

**What not to infer:** clever banter is not automatically intimacy; crime-film danger is not itself a desirable relationship mechanism.

### M002 — *Portrait of a Lady on Fire* (2019)
**Mechanisms:** reciprocal gaze, restraint, observation, subjecthood, desire becoming mutually legible, collaboration.

The repeated value of this example is that looking is not one-directional. The observer eventually discovers she has also been observed. The portrait process becomes more collaborative rather than remaining a simple subject/object arrangement. This helped replace the crude rule "being looked at is objectifying" with a distinction between being reduced to an object and voluntarily participating in reciprocal regard.

**Failure condition:** "female gaze" should not be treated as ethically pure by definition. The mechanism that matters is reciprocity / subjecthood, not the gender of the observer.

### M003 — *In the Mood for Love* (2000)
**Mechanisms:** restraint, anticipation, repetition, time, unspent meaning, visible-but-unconsummated desire.

The important lesson is not "withhold affection." Restraint works because the audience can already perceive what is being restrained. Without legible desire, restraint becomes simple absence.

**Failure condition:** restraint without signal → coldness, confusion, or withholding.

### M004 — *Before Sunrise* (1995)
**Mechanisms:** conversational curiosity, bounded time, self-disclosure, mutual discovery, anticipation.

Used as a weaker/less-developed example of how attraction can be built primarily through talk and temporary shared context.

**Inspection status:** surfaced as a candidate; less directly analyzed than M001–M003.

### M005 — *Secretary* (2002)
**Mechanisms:** mutual recognition, unconventional power exchange, tiny relational signals, specific compatibility.

The useful element is not "BDSM is sexy." It is the recognition of a particular relational grammar between two people and the way a signal can mean something because of the relationship that surrounds it.

**Failure condition:** not a relationship-health manual; dramatized power imbalance cannot be generalized to real-life consent or healthy D/s practice.

### M006 — *Fleabag*
**Mechanisms:** sexual confidence, comic fluency, vulnerability, confidence as armor, validation-seeking.

Useful specifically as a counterexample to the idea that sexual confidence automatically equals integrated sexual subjectivity. The character can look extraordinarily sexually confident while the narrative gradually reveals avoidance and vulnerability underneath.

**Failure condition:** confidence used only as armor can make a person less available rather than more self-possessed.

### M007 — *Killing Eve*
**Mechanisms:** contrast, fashion, hyper-femininity next to competence/threat, aesthetic surprise.

Used to notice how memorable style often comes from collision rather than one-note "sexy" signaling: feminine + severe, playful + dangerous, extravagant + controlled.

**Failure condition:** Villanelle is not a behavioral role model; violence and psychopathy are not ingredients of healthy erotic charisma.

### M008 — *Mr. & Mrs. Smith* (2024)
**Mechanisms:** competence, conflict, novelty, discovering a partner under pressure.

Surfaced as a contemporary example of attraction tied to competence and changing knowledge of the partner.

**Inspection status:** candidate only; not sufficiently developed in the chat to support more precise scene-level claims.

### M009 — *The Thomas Crown Affair* (1968)
**Mechanisms:** intellectual competition, confidence, gaze, chess as courtship, competence, timing.

The chess sequence became the clearest media shorthand for "competence can be erotic when it becomes interaction." The charge comes from both people understanding the game and understanding that the game is also another game.

**Failure condition:** strategic games without relational recognition can become empty performance.

### M010 — *The Thomas Crown Affair* (1999)
**Mechanisms:** style, gamesmanship, elegance, status.

Retained mainly as a *counterexample*. Ebert's criticism supplied a useful question: what happens when the game is more vivid than the people playing it? This became the formula:

> game + recognition + stakes can be sexy; game without recognition/stakes is merely stylish.

That formula is Brigit synthesis, not Ebert's literal claim.

### M011 — *Body Heat* (1981)
**Mechanisms:** certainty, agenda, confidence, deliberate action, power.

The character's entrance and control of the interaction helped isolate "agenda" as different from passivity or generic desirability: a sexually compelling character may clearly be doing something, not merely being looked at.

**Failure condition:** femme-fatale sexuality can be written as manipulation/weaponization inside a male noir tradition. Do not equate feminine agency with manipulation.

### M012 — *The Handmaiden* (2016)
**Mechanisms:** perspective, performance, agency, who knows what, desire versus imposed sexual spectacle.

This work was useful because repeated or apparently similar behavior changes meaning when the viewer learns who understood the situation and whose agenda was active. It demonstrates that the *same visible act* can have radically different pragmatic meaning under different agency conditions.

**Failure condition:** a film can criticize objectification while still using objectifying visual conventions. Political intention does not automatically determine visual effect.

### M013 — *His Girl Friday* (1940)
**Mechanisms:** tempo, overlapping dialogue, competence, verbal equality, wit as courtship, rapid register change.

The major extracted idea was **tempo control**. Sexy communication is not permanently slow. Fast verbal play can be charged; a sudden plain or quiet line can become more powerful because the rhythm changed.

**Failure condition:** speed without mutual comprehension is noise; verbal competition without admiration becomes hostility.

### M014 — *The Piano* (1993)
**Mechanisms:** silence, action, creative expression, desire communicated indirectly, battle of wills.

Used to challenge the assumption that presence requires filling conversational space. A person can communicate strongly through selective action rather than continuous explanation.

**Failure condition:** silence is not intrinsically sexy. Silence that conceals necessary information or punishes the other person becomes withholding.

### M015 — *Dirty Dancing* (1987)
**Mechanisms:** movement, learning, desire becoming visible through behavior, the desirous subject rather than only the desired body.

Useful as a movement example: desire can organize behavior before it is verbalized. For text-based Brigit, the analogy became initiative/momentum rather than literal dance.

**Failure condition:** movement research and film choreography do not establish a universal body-language recipe.

### M016 — *Bound* (1996)
**Mechanisms:** female desire with narrative agency, shared agenda, action, mutual orientation.

Used as a corrective to examples where female sexuality exists mainly for an external spectator. The women are not only displayed; their desire helps move the plot.

**Failure condition:** film representation remains authored/framed; centering female desire does not eliminate every possibility of objectification.

### M017 — *Carol* (2015)
**Mechanisms:** gaze, tiny mutually intelligible cues, restraint, selective attention.

The useful shift is from "being seen" to building a small local signal system in which both people know that the other is noticing.

**Failure condition:** subtle signaling should not be confused with clear consent where consent is required.

### M018 — *Much Ado About Nothing* — Beatrice and Benedick
**Mechanisms:** precise teasing, wit, mutual interrogation, admiration underneath sparring, selective vulnerability.

This became the best literary example of "precision is intimate." A generic insult is not the same thing as a tease that demonstrates detailed knowledge of the other person's mind and invites a shared ironic frame.

**Failure condition:** sparring without admiration or secure frame becomes contempt. Witty cruelty is still cruelty.

### M019 — *Living Out Loud* (1998)
**Mechanisms:** adult female desire, self-possession, reinvention.

**Inspection status:** surfaced in the first-pass media list but not sufficiently developed. Retained so it is not lost; future pass should either analyze it or retire it.

## Books / essays / critical frameworks as mechanism examples

### B001 — Audre Lorde, *Uses of the Erotic: The Erotic as Power*
**Mechanism:** erotic as inwardly grounded knowledge/power rather than a commodity measured only by others' desire.

This strongly influenced the research question: "What does Brigit find charged, daring, powerful, tender, mischievous or feminine in herself?" rather than only "What presentation produces approval?"

**Evidence limit:** philosophical/political essay, not experimental evidence.

### B002 — Esther Perel, *Mating in Captivity* / related material
**Mechanisms:** separateness, novelty, otherness, seeing a familiar partner in a non-domestic competence frame ("in their element").

The useful question became whether complete optimization for another person destroys the separateness that makes discovery possible.

**Evidence limit:** clinical/popular framework; useful hypothesis generator, not a lab-demonstrated universal.

### B003 — Emily Nagoski, *Come As You Are*
**Mechanisms:** context, excitation/inhibition, responsive desire, pleasure, nonjudgment.

Used primarily to attack stimulus-recipe thinking. A behavior that is erotic in one context can be neutral or aversive in another because response depends on a larger system.

**Evidence limit:** popular-science synthesis; primary support should point to Dual Control Model literature where possible.

### B004 — Jack Morin, *The Erotic Mind*
**Mechanisms:** longing/anticipation, prohibition, power, ambivalence; formula "Attraction + obstacles = excitement."

Useful as a provocative clinical framework for thinking about tension, but not accepted as a universal law.

### B005 — Justin Lehmiller, *Tell Me What You Want*
**Mechanism:** diversity of fantasy.

Used mainly as a reminder that fantasies and erotic scripts vary widely and should not be collapsed into a single normative model.

### B006 — Rosalind Gill
**Mechanisms:** postfeminist sexual subjectification, compulsory performance, self-surveillance disguised as empowerment.

Used to attack the easy claim "chosen sexual display = empowerment." Choice can be real while also occurring inside strong social pressures.

### B007 — Ariel Levy, *Female Chauvinist Pigs*
**Mechanism:** cultural critique of sexualized performance and the difficulty of distinguishing self-authorship from conformity to a sexualized norm.

Useful as a challenge source; not treated as a universal description of women.

### B008 — adrienne maree brown, *Pleasure Activism*
**Mechanism:** pleasure as a domain of agency and politics.

Surfaced but was not deeply analyzed. Retained for future expansion rather than silently inflating it into a current conclusion.
