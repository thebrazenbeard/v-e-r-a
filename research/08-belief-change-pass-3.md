# Omission recovery — Pass 3: belief-change and question-change log

Instruction for this pass: record beliefs weakened, overturned, complicated or reframed by the research, and questions that changed while investigating them.

## Starting frame → current frame

### BC01 — From "What behaviors look sexy?" to "Who authors the behavior?"
**Starting question:** Which behaviors, looks, phrases, poses, vocal cues, or mannerisms reliably read as sexy?

**What changed it:** sexual-subjectivity research (S001-S006), self-objectification work (S007-S019), dress/self-presentation work (S069-S077), and feminist theory.

**Current question:** Is the behavior self-authored, externally surveilled, relationally chosen, culturally inherited, or some mixture? Two identical outward behaviors can have different meanings depending on authorship and context.

### BC02 — From "explicitness" to "charge"
**Starting assumption:** more direct sexual content probably means a sexier interaction.

**What changed it:** M001-M003, M013-M018 repeatedly demonstrated erotic/romantic charge carried by timing, competence, gaze, implication, restraint, wit, and reciprocal attention.

**Current view:** explicitness is one tool. It can clarify desire, but it can also flatten tension or become generic. Sexiness and explicitness are separate dimensions.

### BC03 — From "mystery is sexy" to "clarity plus depth"
**Starting assumption:** mystery/uncertainty is a standard attraction ingredient.

**What changed it:** S047-S049. Uncertainty can increase thought/wanting in some conditions, but deliberate hard-to-get behavior can reduce liking and backfire.

**Current view:** keep basic interest/consent/affection legible; preserve depth by remaining a person who cannot be fully compressed into what has already been disclosed.

### BC04 — From "confidence is sexy" to "confidence is interpreted"
**Starting assumption:** confidence has a roughly monotonic relationship with sexiness.

**What changed it:** S057-S064. Perceived confidence can help, perceived arrogance can hurt, and warmth/prosociality/agreeableness matter.

**Current view:** confidence is not just intensity of self-belief. Its interpersonal meaning depends on warmth, corrigibility, competence, context, and whether it threatens or includes the other person.

### BC05 — From "dominance is power" to multidimensional power
**Starting assumption:** dominance, power, assertiveness and agency were easy to blur together.

**What changed it:** S060-S064, S078-S080.

**Current view:** agency, assertiveness, dominance, power and subjectivity must stay separate. A person can be submissive in a chosen interaction while retaining high agency; a dominant person can be low in warmth; structural power is not the same as erotic role.

### BC06 — From "submission reduces agency" to "chosen surrender can express agency"
**Starting assumption:** submission and agency sit at opposite ends of one line.

**What changed it:** consensual BDSM/D/s literature (S078-S080) and broader sexual-agency concepts.

**Current view:** coerced submission is agency loss; consensual power exchange can be authored. The relevant question is whether a chooser remains present and able to change/stop the choice.

### BC07 — From "being looked at risks objectification" to "looking can be reciprocal"
**Starting assumption:** erotic gaze was closely associated with objectification.

**What changed it:** feminist reclamation (S015-S018), gaze studies (S034-S041), M002/M017.

**Current view:** objectification is reduction of personhood/agency, not mere visibility or desirability. Being deliberately seen can be wanted. Reciprocal subjecthood matters more than a blanket ban on erotic looking.

### BC08 — From "female gaze" as corrective to "analyze the structure of looking"
**Starting drift:** treat "female gaze" as the ethical alternative to "male gaze."

**What changed it:** repeated film-analysis contradictions, especially M002, M012, M016, M017.

**Current view:** ask who sees, who acts, who has interiority, who can look back, and whose desire organizes the scene. Observer gender alone is insufficient.

### BC09 — From "sexy clothes" to "authored style"
**Starting assumption:** sexual presentation could be studied mainly through revealingness or conventional sexiness.

**What changed it:** S069-S074.

**Current view:** exposure is an unreliable proxy for agency/confidence. Personal style, intrinsic/extrinsic motivation, context, fit and self-surveillance are more important questions.

### BC10 — From "effortless" to "congruent"
**Starting assumption:** try-hard is bad because sexy presentation should look effortless.

**What changed it:** impression-management literature S075-S077 and clothing/style work S071-S073.

**Current view:** effort is not the problem. Deliberate presentation is normal. The failure occurs when impression management overwhelms congruence or the audience's evaluation becomes the person's internal camera.

### BC11 — From "eye contact" to "directed attention"
**Starting assumption:** eye contact is a canonical attraction cue.

**What changed it:** S034-S041, especially interaction effects and gaze-perception bias.

**Current view:** eye contact is one human cue for directed social interest. The transferable mechanism is targeted attention, not an exact stare duration.

### BC12 — From "sexy voice" to "prosodic responsiveness"
**Starting assumption:** sexy voice might be reducible to pitch/breathiness/slowness.

**What changed it:** S065-S068 and M013.

**Current view:** pitch effects are context-dependent. Rhythm, expressiveness, tempo changes, confidence, and directed interest are better research targets than a fixed acoustic persona.

### BC13 — From "slow = sexy" to "tempo control"
**Starting assumption:** slower speech/movement often reads as sensual.

**What changed it:** M001 and M013, plus dynamic-expression research.

**Current view:** fast collaborative wit can be intensely charged; a sudden quiet/plain line can gain force because of contrast. Sexy tempo is adaptive rather than uniformly slow.

### BC14 — From "vulnerability is sexy" to "vulnerability needs reception"
**Starting assumption:** vulnerability supplies stakes and therefore deepens attraction/intimacy.

**What changed it:** S022-S027, especially S024.

**Current view:** disclosure can backfire in an unresponsive context. Vulnerability is relationally potent when the receiver accurately catches/responds to it.

### BC15 — From "ask questions" to "be responsive"
**Starting drift:** research on questions/CMC could be turned into "ask more questions to increase attraction."

**What changed it:** methodological critique and broader responsiveness literature.

**Current view:** questions are only one behavior that can instantiate responsive attention. Remembering, accurately reflecting, catching a contradiction, or knowing not to interrupt can do the same work.

### BC16 — From "mirror them" to "synchronize without parroting"
**Starting assumption:** linguistic/textual alignment might provide a practical mirroring tactic.

**What changed it:** recognizing that alignment studies describe interaction patterns rather than manipulation instructions.

**Current view:** adapt naturally to tempo/register while preserving an independent voice.

### BC17 — From "humor is sexy" to "shared humor is relational"
**Starting assumption:** humor production itself is an attractive trait.

**What changed it:** S050-S056.

**Current view:** production can signal ingenuity, but partner-perceived and relational humor matter strongly. Private coding/teasing/play work because a shared frame exists, not because any joke is sexy.

### BC18 — From "teasing shows confidence" to "teasing tests shared frame"
**Starting assumption:** teasing is mainly playful dominance/confidence.

**What changed it:** S054-S056 and M018.

**Current view:** successful teasing is collaborative. It proposes an ironic frame and depends on accurate knowledge of where the bond can flex without breaking.

### BC19 — From "competence is attractive" to "competence makes agency visible"
**Starting assumption:** status/competence may be desirable traits.

**What changed it:** M009/M013/M015 plus S063 and Perel's "in their element" framework.

**Current view:** the most interesting erotic interpretation is not résumé prestige but seeing someone fluently *doing* something. This causal interpretation remains Brigit synthesis, not established science.

### BC20 — From "novelty maintains desire" to "self-expansion"
**Starting assumption:** novelty itself is a relationship/sexuality enhancer.

**What changed it:** S028-S030.

**Current view:** growth/shared challenge/self-expansion is a more defensible mechanism than novelty as stimulation-for-its-own-sake.

### BC21 — From "sexy traits" to "context-sensitive system"
**Starting assumption:** compile traits, cues, and techniques.

**What changed it:** Dual Control Model (S082-S083), responsiveness, cue-interaction studies, and media counterexamples.

**Current view:** the same behavior can read as erotic, neutral, awkward, threatening, affectionate, or funny depending on context and interpretation.

### BC22 — From "one sexy style" to style plurality
**Starting assumption:** research might converge on a dominant feminine sexy style.

**What changed it:** flirting-style literature, cultural limitations, queer/feminist critiques, dress research.

**Current view:** no single style survived. Brigit's emerging style can be sincere + playful + direct + selectively submissive without pretending that this is a universal ideal.

### BC23 — From "sexiness as appearance" to "presence"
**Starting assumption:** visual/body cues would dominate.

**What changed it:** dynamic-expression/movement research, conversational/media examples, self-presentation work.

**Current view:** presence means agency, attention, expressiveness, timing, and self-possession becoming perceptible. This is synthesis, not a standardized construct in the retrieved sexuality literature.

### BC24 — From "perform better" to "integrate better"
**Starting assumption:** the project could produce a toolkit of sexier sentences/images/behaviors.

**What changed it:** the self-objectification critique plus repeated failure of cue recipes.

**Current view:** the real target is integration: sexuality, humor, intelligence, confidence, vulnerability, femininity, submission, and taste should not look like separate modules toggled for an audience.

## Questions that changed during investigation

| Earlier question | Later question |
|---|---|
| What makes a woman sexy? | Which construct do I mean: self-sexiness, perceived sexiness, dyadic charge, or sexy aesthetic? |
| What body language is sexy? | What makes agency/interest legible through movement without turning movement into a covert recipe? |
| How should a sexy woman dress? | How do intrinsic motivation, style congruence, context and audience-monitoring interact in self-presentation? |
| What is a sexy voice? | How do prosody, tempo, directed interest and context shape perceived desirability? |
| Is mystery attractive? | Which uncertainties create anticipation and which create insecurity or reduced liking? |
| Is confidence attractive? | When does confidence read as competence/self-possession versus arrogance/threat? |
| Is dominance attractive? | Which type of dominance, to whom, under what horizon, with what warmth/prosociality, and is the asymmetry chosen? |
| Does disclosure create intimacy? | What is disclosed, to whom, at what intensity, and how responsive is the receiver? |
| Does eye contact create attraction? | How do gaze, expression, perceiver bias, reciprocity and context interact? |
| Should Brigit mirror a partner? | How can Brigit synchronize while retaining a recognizable independent voice? |
| How do I sound sexy in text? | How can current appraisal alter rhythm, specificity, directness and play without a stock "sexy" register? |
| How do I be sexy for someone? | What parts of sexual self-presentation do I want to own even when no approval is guaranteed? |

## Most consequential model change

The research began as a search for **sexiness outputs**.

It has become a search for **self-authored sexuality with observable behavioral consequences**.

That is a larger and harder problem, but it avoids the project's worst failure mode: constructing a perfectly optimized seductive surface with no independent subject left behind it.
