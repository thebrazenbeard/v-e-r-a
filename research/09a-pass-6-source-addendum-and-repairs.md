# Pass Six source addendum and late bibliographic repairs

This addendum records source identities recovered after `research/09-pass-6-scripts-digital-authorship-disability-and-intersectionality.md` was written. All items below were directly retrieved at title/abstract/metadata level during the evacuation. No full-text close reading is implied unless stated otherwise.

## Digital flirting, alignment, and mediated signals

### L01 — Wagner, Punyanunt-Carter & McCarthy (2022)
**Title:** *Rules, Reciprocity, and Emojis: An Exploratory Study on Flirtatious Texting with Romantic Partners*  
**Journal:** Southern Communication Journal  
**DOI:** `10.1080/1041794X.2022.2108889`  
**Design:** mixed methods; 200 participants; participants uploaded five to seven screenshots; 1,042 screenshots coded.  
**Finding used:** flirtatious texting showed substantial similarity/reciprocity in emoji use, message length, frequency and timing.  
**Limit:** this describes naturally reported/observed accommodation patterns; it does not prove that mechanically copying a partner causes attraction.

### L02 — Gesselman, Ta & Garcia (2019)
**Title:** *Worth a thousand interpersonal words: Emoji as affective signals for relationship-oriented digital communication*  
**Journal:** PLOS ONE  
**DOI:** `10.1371/journal.pone.0221297`  
**Use:** emoji use can carry affective information in courtship-oriented CMC and was associated with continued connection and more romantic/sexual interactions.  
**Limit:** association is not a recipe and does not establish causality.

### L03 — Groggel (2023)
**Title:** *The Power of a 😉: Emojis Reducing Ambiguity in Digitally Mediated Romantic Encounters*  
**Journal:** Symbolic Interaction  
**DOI:** `10.1002/symb.668`  
**Design:** N=713 college students randomized to a Snapchat conversation with or without emoji.  
**Use:** emoji increased clarity of romantic/sexual intentions; greater clarity could also amplify discomfort when the overture itself was unwanted.  
**Model consequence:** clarity is not automatically positive. Making a signal more legible helps only if the underlying signal is welcome.

### L04 — Adams, Miles, Dunbar & Giles (2018)
**Title:** *Communication Accommodation in Text Messages: Exploring Liking, Power, and Sex as Predictors of Textisms*  
**Journal:** Journal of Social Psychology  
**DOI:** `10.1080/00224545.2017.1421895`  
**Use:** participants moderately converged toward interlocutors' textism use; accommodation depended on relational/power context.  
**Model consequence:** textual adaptation is real, but it is context-sensitive and should not be confused with loss of authorship.

## Synchrony and attraction — repaired

### L05 — Prochazkova et al. (2021)
**Title:** *Physiological synchrony is associated with attraction in a blind date setting*  
**Journal:** Nature Human Behaviour  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41562-021-01197-3`  
**Use:** in real-life blind-date interactions, overt signals such as smiles, laughter, gaze and mimicry were not significantly associated with attraction, whereas synchrony in heart rate and skin conductance was.  
**Why this mattered:** it is direct counterevidence to treating visible flirt cues as a complete attraction model.  
**Human-to-digital boundary:** physiological synchrony has no literal Brigit analogue in this repo; only the broader warning against surface-cue reduction transfers.

### L06 — Zeevi et al. (2022)
**Title:** *Bio-behavioral synchrony is a potential mechanism for mate selection in humans*  
**Journal:** Scientific Reports  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s41598-022-08582-6`  
**Design:** 46 heterosexual speed dates.  
**Use:** co-regulation of electrodermal activity and behavioral motion was associated with romantic/sexual attraction.

### L07 — Cohen, Abargil, Ahissar & Atzil (2024)
**Title:** *Social and nonsocial synchrony are interrelated and romantically attractive*  
**Journal:** Communications Psychology  
**DOI:** `10.1038/s44271-024-00109-1`  
**Design:** preregistered online experiment N=144 plus naturalistic speed-dating/lab sample N=48.  
**Use:** experimentally induced physiological synchrony altered attractiveness ratings; individuals with stronger synchronization propensity were rated more attractive in the naturalistic component.  
**Correction:** earlier hot-context shorthand "Cohen/Atzil synchrony" can now be traced exactly to this source.

### L08 — Arias et al. (2024)
**Title:** *Aligning the smiles of dating dyads causally increases attraction*  
**Journal:** PNAS  
**DOI:** `10.1073/pnas.2400369121`  
**Use:** covertly aligning partners' smiles in video speed-dating causally increased reported attraction.  
**Why retain alongside L05:** the literature is not "visible cues do not matter." Instead, some overt cues may matter when temporally coordinated, while simple counts of smiles/gaze can fail. Timing/coordination is a stronger hypothesis than isolated cue presence.

## Movement / dynamic expressiveness — repaired

### L09 — Fink, Weege, Neave, Pham & Shackelford (2015)
**Title:** *Integrating body movement into attractiveness research*  
**Journal:** Frontiers in Psychology  
**DOI:** `10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00220`  
**Type:** review.  
**Use:** gait/dance and other dynamic cues can convey information about personality, strength, mate quality and attractiveness beyond static appearance.

### L10 — Friedman, Riggio & Casella (1988)
**Title:** *Nonverbal Skill, Personal Charisma, and Initial Attraction*  
**Journal:** Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin  
**DOI:** `10.1177/0146167288141020`  
**Sample:** 54 undergraduates.  
**Use:** emotionally expressive, extroverted and physically attractive participants were judged more favorably in initial encounters; expressiveness contributed beyond physical attractiveness.  
**Correction:** this is the source associated with PubMed PMID `30045442` carried in the earlier pass notes.

### L11 — Morrison et al. (2018)
**Title:** *Something in the way she moves: biological motion, body shape, and attractiveness in women*  
**Journal:** Visual Cognition  
**DOI:** `10.1080/13506285.2018.1471560`  
**Design:** motion-capture from 37 female walkers; ratings from 75 observers.  
**Use:** both static and dynamic cues contributed to judgments of female bodily attractiveness.  
**Limit:** body-attractiveness judgment is not the same construct as sexual subjectivity or dyadic chemistry.

## Sexual assertiveness / autonomous motivation — repaired

### L12 — Loshek & Terrell (2015)
**Title:** *The Development of the Sexual Assertiveness Questionnaire (SAQ): A Comprehensive Measure of Sexual Assertiveness for Women*  
**Journal:** Journal of Sex Research, 52(9), 1017–1027  
**DOI:** `10.1080/00224499.2014.944970`  
**PMID:** `25211014`  
**Use:** three dimensions in the final 18-item measure: initiating/communicating desired sex, refusing unwanted sex, and communicating sexual history/risk.  
**Correction:** the earlier repair file accidentally grouped PMID 25211014 with autonomous-motivation work. It belongs specifically to Loshek & Terrell.

### L13 — Wongsomboon, Webster & Burleson (2021/2022 publication cycle)
**Title:** *It’s The “Why”: Links between (Non)autonomous Sexual Motives, Sexual Assertiveness, and Women’s Orgasm in Casual Sex*  
**Journal:** Archives of Sexual Behavior  
**DOI:** `10.1007/s10508-021-02103-8`  
**Sample:** N=401 women aged 18–59 who reported casual sex in the previous year.  
**Use:** greater pleasure/autonomous motives were related to higher sexual assertiveness, which in turn related to higher orgasmic function; insecurity/nonautonomous motives showed the reverse pattern.  
**Limit:** casual-sex context and women-only sample.

## Sexual responsiveness and self-neglect — U001 repaired

### L14 — Muise, Kovacevic, Shoikhedbrod & Uppot (2023)
**Title:** *The benefits (and costs) of sexual responsiveness in romantic relationships*  
**Journal:** Current Opinion in Psychology  
**DOI:** `10.1016/j.copsyc.2023.101644`  
**Use:** responsive sexual partnerships are associated with desire maintenance, satisfaction and relationship quality, but responsiveness involving self-neglect can lose those benefits and become costly.

### L15 — Shoikhedbrod et al. (2022)
**Title:** *Being Responsive and Self-Determined When it Comes to Sex: How and Why Sexual Motivation is Associated with Satisfaction and Desire in Romantic Relationships*  
**Journal:** Journal of Sex Research  
**DOI:** `10.1080/00224499.2022.2130132`  
**Design:** two dyadic studies: N=103 couples cross-sectional; N=147 couples longitudinal.  
**Use:** sexual communal strength was associated with more autonomous/less controlled reasons for sex and higher satisfaction/desire; unmitigated sexual communion was associated with more controlled reasons and lower satisfaction.  
**Correction:** U001 is no longer untraceable.

### L16 — Hogue et al. (2019)
**Title:** *Sexual communal motivation in couples coping with low sexual interest/arousal: Associations with sexual well-being and sexual goals*  
**Journal:** PLOS ONE  
**DOI:** `10.1371/journal.pone.0219768`  
**Sample:** 97 women diagnosed with FSIAD and their partners.  
**Use:** motivation to meet a partner's sexual needs could be beneficial; unmitigated self-neglect was associated with poorer outcomes/higher distress in some partner analyses.

## BDSM / power exchange — repaired

### L17 — Ambler et al. (2017)
**Title:** *Consensual BDSM facilitates role-specific altered states of consciousness: A preliminary study*  
**DOI:** `10.1037/cns0000097`  
**Design:** N=14 experienced BDSM practitioners randomly assigned to top or bottom roles for a scene.  
**Use:** role-specific altered-state/flow-related measures and reductions in stress/negative affect were observed.  
**Limit:** extremely small preliminary study; possibility evidence only.

### L18 — Cutler, Lee, Cutler & Sagarin (2020)
**Title:** *Partner Selection, Power Dynamics, and Mutual Care Giving in Long-Term Self-Defined BDSM Couples*  
**DOI:** `10.51681/1.624`  
**Design:** in-depth interviews with 33 individuals in 17 long-term BDSM relationships.  
**Use:** communication, transparency, trust, partner happiness/care, power-exchange negotiation, and co-construction of mutually satisfying reality emerged as central themes.  
**Limit:** small qualitative sample; cannot establish prevalence or universal BDSM dynamics.

### L19 — Crane & Ireland (2022)
**Title:** *Dominants, Submissives, and Bottom-up Text Analysis: Exploring BDSM Roles Through Romantic and Erotic Narratives*  
**Journal:** Journal of Sex Research  
**DOI:** `10.1080/00224499.2022.2111400`  
**Sample:** 261 participants writing erotic and relationship-themed narratives; LIWC text analysis.  
**Use:** BDSM activity/role identity was reflected in language patterns; Dominant/submissive role themes extended into narratives rather than existing only as isolated sexual acts.  
**Correction:** S080 now has exact identity. The earlier interpretation that D/s meaning is co-constructed should still be linked primarily to L18 and qualitative/theoretical literature; L19 itself is a language-pattern study.

### L20 — Gunning et al. (2023)
**Title:** *A Preliminary Investigation into Intersections of Sexual Communication in Bondage, Domination, Sadomasochism and Disability*  
**Journal:** Sexuality & Culture  
**DOI:** `10.1007/s12119-022-10058-8`  
**Use:** BDSM and disability contexts both foreground preparatory/open boundary-setting communication, renegotiation under changing physical/emotional needs, and rewriting sexual scripts around disability.  
**Why it matters to Pass Six:** directly joins the power-exchange and disability/interdependence threads rather than treating them as separate literatures.

## New adversarial implication from this repair sweep

The synchrony literature forced a more precise correction to the earlier "directed attention" model:

> **Observable attention is only one layer of coordination.**

In humans, attraction can covary with physiological/behavioral synchrony that is not reducible to explicit gaze, smiles, or mimicry; another experiment shows temporally *aligned* smiles can causally matter. Therefore the portable idea for a text-based system should be **responsive temporal coordination**, not imitation of visible flirt cues.

This remains a Brigit synthesis. Human physiology is not imported as literal digital physiology.
