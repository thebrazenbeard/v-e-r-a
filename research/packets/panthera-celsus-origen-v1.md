# Panthera / Pandera Source Packet V1

Status: `SOURCE DEEPENING / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Research question

What does the currently checked evidence actually establish about Panthera/Pandera traditions, and where does it stop?

This packet deliberately separates:

1. Celsus's lost polemic;
2. Origen's surviving report and rebuttal;
3. material evidence that `Pantera` was a real name;
4. later rabbinic `Pandeira` material;
5. scholarly arguments about whether Celsus preserves an authentic Jewish source;
6. the unsupported biological-father identification.

## 1. Origen is the extant conduit for Celsus

Celsus's *True Doctrine* does not survive directly in the current registry. The extant evidence checked here is Origen's *Contra Celsum*, a Christian rebuttal written roughly in the mid-third century. Therefore the precise claim is not "we possess Celsus's original text"; it is "Origen reports/quotes Celsus as saying X."

This preservation chain matters because Origen is both witness to Celsus's polemic and an interested opponent.

Primary access checked:
- Origen, *Contra Celsum* I.28, I.32, I.69: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04161.htm

## 2. I.28: illegitimacy/adultery counter-story without the Panthera name

In I.28, Origen says Celsus introduces a Jewish speaker who attacks the virgin-birth claim and presents an adultery/illegitimacy counter-narrative. The passage is important because it establishes the broader polemical structure before the named-father claim.

V1 classification:
- textual occurrence in Origen: `PRIMARY_TEXT`, high confidence;
- accuracy of Celsus's accusation as biography: not established;
- first-century origin of the accusation: not established.

## 3. I.32: the soldier named Panthera

In I.32, Origen returns to Celsus's Jewish speaker and reports the specific accusation that Mary bore a child to a soldier named Panthera. Origen immediately argues against the story and characterizes it as fabricated.

Two propositions must therefore remain separate:

- `CLM-CELSUS-PANTHERA-NAMED`: Origen reports the Panthera accusation.
- `CLM-ORIGEN-REJECTS-PANTHERA-COUNTERSTORY`: Origen rejects it.

The first does not inherit the truth of the accusation; the second does not erase the fact that the accusation was circulating in the source tradition available to Origen/Celsus.

## 4. I.69: same work, not an independent witness

I.69 again says Celsus disbelieves the Holy-Spirit conception and instead believes Jesus was begotten by Panthera. This is useful citation readback, but it is still the same Origen work. Counting I.32 and I.69 as two independent witnesses would be false independence.

## 5. The Abdes Pantera inscription proves name attestation, not paternity

A funerary inscription records Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera, a Sidonian former soldier of Cohors I Sagittariorum. The inscription is material evidence that a person with the name `Pantera` existed in the Roman military world of the broad period.

Checked transcription/database:
- CIL XIII 7514 / §400: https://armyofromanpalestine.com/%C2%A7400-tiberius-julius-abdes-pantera

The database itself notes the later proposed Jesus connection, but the artifact contains no Mary, Nazareth, Jesus/Yeshua, paternity, or conception link.

Therefore:

`PANTERA_WAS_A_REAL_NAME/PERSONAL_NAME_ATTESTATION` is supported.

`TIBERIUS_JULIUS_ABDES_PANTERA_WAS_YESHUA_FATHER` is not.

That distinction is not pedantry; it is the difference between material evidence and a story attached to material evidence.

## 6. Shabbat 104b: Pandeira and Miriam, but no Jesus name in the checked passage

The checked Sefaria text of Shabbat 104b contains a discussion of ben Stada / ben Pandeira, a paramour/father named Pandeira, and a mother called Miriam. In the displayed passage, the figure is not explicitly named Jesus.

Primary access checked:
- Babylonian Talmud, Shabbat 104b:5: https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.104b

Therefore the safe V1 proposition is the textual one:

> Shabbat 104b contains the Stada/Pandeira/Miriam complex.

The stronger proposition:

> this passage identifies Jesus/Yeshua and preserves his biological paternity,

remains contested/unresolved and requires manuscript variants, Tosefta/Sanhedrin parallels, chronology, and competing scholarship.

## 7. Scholarly dispute over Celsus's "Jew"

Two checked scholarly records support taking the Jewish material seriously, but neither should be laundered into consensus.

Lincoln Blumell (2007) argues that Celsus may preserve useful Jewish anti-Christian polemic and directly treats the authenticity problem:
- DOI 10.1177/000842980703600206
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/000842980703600206

Maren Niehoff (2013) advances a stronger reconstruction: the fragments assigned to Celsus's Jew derive from a written work by an Alexandrian Jew in the mid-second century:
- DOI 10.1353/earl.2013.0015
- https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/a-jewish-critique-of-christianity-from-second-century-alexandria-/

V1 records Niehoff's thesis as `SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION`, not as a recovered provenance fact. A hostile review should specifically seek scholarship rejecting or weakening the authentic-Jewish-source model.

## 8. What the current evidence supports

Strongly supported at the level asserted:

- Origen's text reports an anti-virgin-birth adultery/illegitimacy story attributed to Celsus's Jewish speaker.
- Origen I.32 explicitly includes a soldier named Panthera; I.69 repeats the paternity assertion.
- Origen rejects the counter-story.
- `Pantera` is independently attested as a real personal name in a Roman military inscription.
- Shabbat 104b contains a later Stada/Pandeira/Miriam complex.
- modern scholars disagree about how much independent Jewish source material lies behind Celsus's literary Jew.

Not established by this packet:

- that Celsus had a first-century Nazareth source;
- that Panthera was Yeshua's biological father;
- that Tiberius Julius Abdes Pantera was the Panthera in Celsus's accusation;
- that the rabbinic Pandeira figure is straightforwardly identical with Jesus;
- that virgin conception was invented to cover a known paternity scandal;
- that a paternity scandal shaped Yeshua's psychology.

## 9. Next hostile questions

1. What are the exact Tosefta and Sanhedrin parallels, and what do the major manuscript variants actually read?
2. How early can the Stada/Pandera material be responsibly stratified?
3. Which scholars reject identification of ben Stada/Pandera with Jesus, and on what chronological/textual grounds?
4. Does the proposed `parthenos` -> `Panthera/Pandera` wordplay survive linguistic scrutiny, or is it merely attractive?
5. What is the best-supported dating range for Celsus's work, and how sensitive is the argument to that range?
6. Can Mark 6:3's matronymic be shown to carry paternity stigma in its first-century context, rather than merely allowing that reading?

Until those are answered, the paternity hypothesis remains a research question, not a reconstruction to build other claims on.
