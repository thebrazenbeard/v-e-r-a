# Physical and Computational Constraints on Simulation Hypotheses V1

Status: `SOURCE-BASED CONSTRAINT MATRIX / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Purpose

This packet asks a narrower question than `Are we simulated?`:

> If a universe were implemented as a simulation of a specified kind, what constraints or observable consequences would follow?

The implementation qualifier is essential. A constraint on a cubic lattice simulator does not automatically constrain an analog simulator, a quantum substrate, a demand-driven rendering system, a mathematically fundamental universe, or a parent universe with different physical laws.

## 1. Beane, Davoudi, Savage: cubic lattice numerical simulation

Source:
- Silas R. Beane, Zohreh Davoudi, Martin J. Savage, `Constraints on the Universe as a Numerical Simulation` (2012).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1847

### Model actually studied

The paper asks what could follow if the observed universe were generated by a numerical simulation using a **cubic space-time lattice/grid**, motivated by extrapolating lattice gauge-theory computation.

The model therefore contains architecture-specific commitments:

- discrete lattice spacing;
- lattice geometry;
- finite ultraviolet cutoff;
- potential rotational-symmetry breaking inherited from the grid;
- lattice field-theory behavior.

### Proposed consequences

The authors derive bounds on the inverse lattice spacing and discuss the possibility that the highest-energy cosmic-ray distributions could display rotational-symmetry patterns reflecting the underlying lattice.

### What a positive result would mean

An observed cubic-symmetry artifact with the predicted energy behavior could be evidence for lattice-like microstructure.

It would still require argument to distinguish:

- a simulated lattice;
- fundamental discrete spacetime;
- an effective lattice-like physical theory;
- another mechanism producing similar symmetry breaking.

### What a null result means

A null result constrains the studied lattice implementation family or pushes its characteristic scale beyond observation.

It does **not** falsify all possible simulations.

Project classification:
- `EMPIRICAL_TEST_PROPOSAL`
- narrow scope: `CUBIC_LATTICE_NUMERICAL_IMPLEMENTATION`

## 2. Gordon McCabe: universe creation on a computer

Source:
- Gordon McCabe, `Universe creation on a computer`, *Studies in History and Philosophy of Modern Physics* 36 (2005).
- DOI: 10.1016/J.SHPSB.2005.04.002
- http://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/1891/

McCabe treats computer-universe claims both epistemologically and metaphysically.

Relevant V1 themes include:

- a perfect simulation may be experientially indistinguishable from the simulated target;
- a computer-program universe may have empirical consequences if implementation constraints are specified;
- spatial topology and information requirements can become relevant;
- digital simulation and analog representation should not be treated as equivalent metaphysical cases;
- exact representation of an entire universe raises questions about what it means for the representing system and represented system to be distinct.

Project guard:

McCabe's willingness to explore empirical predictions does not establish that those predictions are generic to every simulation hypothesis.

## 3. Wolpert: computer-science theory and simulation

Source:
- David H. Wolpert, `Implications of computer science theory for the simulation hypothesis` (2024).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16050

### Self-simulation

Wolpert uses Kleene's second recursion theorem to show mathematical possibility results involving a universe/computer participating in a form of self-simulation.

The philosophical consequence is subtle: identical computational instances can make the question `which copy is really us?` ill-posed under some descriptions.

Project guard:

`mathematically possible self-simulation` != `our universe self-simulates`.

### Rice's theorem and limits

Wolpert brings undecidability-style results into the simulation discussion. Rice's theorem constrains what nontrivial semantic properties of arbitrary programs can be decided algorithmically.

For On-Theo, the important implication is not `simulation is impossible`, but that some questions an embedded or external computer asks about arbitrary simulated systems may be formally undecidable.

### Fully homomorphic encryption

Wolpert also considers encryption as a conceptual complication: a computation may be carried out while important properties of its state are inaccessible to the computing party in ordinary semantic terms.

This weakens naive assumptions that `simulator` entails `transparent omniscient observer`.

### Nested simulation graphs

Simulation relations need not form a simple one-direction stack. Formal graph questions arise around self-loops, cycles, and chains.

Again, graph-theoretic possibility is not physical evidence.

## 4. Vazza: energy/information and astrophysical constraints

Source:
- F. Vazza, `Astrophysical constraints on the simulation hypothesis for this Universe: why it is (nearly) impossible that we live in a simulation` (2025).
- https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08461

### Cases studied

Vazza analyzes several physically motivated cases, including:

- simulation of the visible universe;
- simulation of Earth;
- reduced-resolution Earth simulation constrained by high-energy observations.

### Main result

Under information-energy relations and astrophysical constraints using physics sufficiently like ours, the required energy/power is extraordinarily large or incompatible with available physical resources.

The paper argues especially strongly against the idea that a universe sharing our physical properties could straightforwardly simulate ours at the required fidelity.

### Critical limitation

The paper itself leaves open parent universes with substantially different physical laws/properties.

Therefore:

`Vazza strongly constrains same-physics parent-universe simulations` does **not** entail `Vazza disproves every simulation hypothesis`.

A hypothesis that escapes by positing arbitrary parent physics becomes harder to constrain, but that is an epistemic cost rather than evidence in its favor.

## 5. Resource accounting and demand-driven simulation

A frequent reply to resource objections is that a simulator need not compute every degree of freedom at full fidelity at all times.

Possible strategies include:

- local rendering around observers;
- adaptive resolution;
- procedural generation;
- coarse-graining unobserved regions;
- lazy evaluation;
- reusing statistical approximations instead of microscopic simulation;
- simulating minds and sensory streams rather than a whole cosmos.

These proposals may reduce resource requirements, but each introduces new assumptions and potentially new empirical/causal problems.

Examples:

- How are distant observations generated consistently across multiple observers?
- How are entangled or causally connected events handled?
- What prevents adaptive detail changes from leaving statistical artifacts?
- Does observer-targeted rendering require knowing in advance what counts as an observation?

A resource objection to a full-universe simulation therefore cannot simply be transferred to a sparse simulation, but a sparse simulation must be evaluated as its own model.

## 6. Finite precision and discretization

A finite computational implementation may motivate questions about:

- minimum length/time scales;
- finite information density;
- quantized state spaces;
- finite precision;
- ultraviolet cutoffs;
- symmetry breaking from update structure.

But known discreteness or quantization in physics is not automatically evidence for computation.

Quantum mechanics already includes discrete spectra in many contexts without thereby establishing a digital external simulator.

Likewise, the Planck scale is not a known `pixel size of reality`.

Project guard:

`physical discreteness` != `digital computation` != `external simulation`.

## 7. Information bounds

Physical theories contain limits and relationships involving entropy, information, energy, and horizons.

These can constrain physical information storage/processing under the theories that generate them.

They do not by themselves demonstrate that the universe is information in the computational/software sense.

A simulation argument using an information bound must specify:

- which physical theory supplies the bound;
- whether the bound applies to the simulated universe, parent universe, or both;
- whether exact microstate representation is required;
- whether compression/coarse-graining changes the target;
- whether the bound concerns storage, communication, thermodynamic entropy, algorithmic information, or another quantity.

## 8. Analog, digital, and quantum implementation

The phrase `computer simulation` hides implementation diversity.

### Classical digital

Finite symbolic states updated algorithmically.

Potentially susceptible to finite precision, lattice, memory, and complexity constraints.

### Analog

A physical system whose continuous dynamics represent another system.

An analog simulator need not exhibit the same artifacts as a digital lattice simulation.

### Quantum

A simulator using quantum states and operations.

Quantum simulation can efficiently reproduce classes of quantum systems inaccessible to classical computation, but calling the universe a `quantum computer` may be a reformulation or ontological interpretation rather than evidence of an external simulator.

### Unknown parent substrate

If parent physics is unconstrained, almost any resource objection can be evaded by stipulation.

That decreases falsifiability.

## 10. What physics can and cannot establish

### Physics can potentially establish

- that a particular model predicts an observation;
- that a specific implementation is inconsistent with observed physics;
- that a proposed resource budget is impossible under specified laws;
- that spacetime exhibits or does not exhibit particular symmetries/structures;
- that some computational models reproduce physical laws more naturally than others.

### Physics cannot establish merely from those facts

- that an external simulator exists;
- the simulator's purpose;
- whether the simulator is conscious;
- whether the simulator is morally/theologically God-like;
- whether parent-universe physics resembles ours;
- whether computational description implies ontological implementation.

## 11. V1 project position

Physical and computational research makes the simulation question more precise mainly by **splitting it into falsifiable subclasses**.

That is epistemically valuable even if no generic simulation test exists.

Current classifications:

- cubic lattice universe: `SPECULATIVE_MODEL / EMPIRICALLY_CONSTRAINABLE`.
- same-physics full-universe simulator: `SPECULATIVE_MODEL / STRONGLY RESOURCE-CONSTRAINED`.
- radically different parent physics: `SPECULATIVE_MODEL / WEAKLY CONSTRAINED`.
- self-simulation: `FORMALLY POSSIBLE IN SELECTED COMPUTATIONAL FRAMEWORKS / NOT PHYSICALLY ESTABLISHED`.
- generic external simulation: `SPECULATIVE_MODEL / UNDERCONSTRAINED`.
