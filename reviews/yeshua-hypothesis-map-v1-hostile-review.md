## Review purpose

This review attempts to break the project inferences in `research/yeshua/hypothesis-map-v1.md`. It does not evaluate Christian truth claims, disbelief claims, or confessional desirability.

## H1. Paternity-polemic crystallization

Subject inference: ambiguous early family/paternity traditions plus later explicit virginal-conception claims may have generated increasingly specific hostile counter-polemic, culminating in named Panthera traditions.

### Strength

The current source order is compatible with this model: Mark 6:3 is ambiguous; John 8:41 does not name an alternative father; Matthew/Luke make divine conception explicit; the checked named Panthera accusation is later and polemical. The model also avoids circularly reading later Panthera material back into Mark.

### Hostile challenge

Chronological appearance in surviving sources is not equivalent to chronological origin. A second-century named accusation could preserve an earlier rumor. Conversely, Matthew/Luke need not be responses to paternity polemic; their infancy narratives have multiple literary/theological functions.

Absence of an earlier named Panthera witness is therefore evidence limitation, not proof of late invention.

### Status

PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS. Keep as PROJECT_INFERENCE / SPECULATIVE_MODEL. It is currently preferable to a confidently asserted continuous Panthera biography only because the latter lacks an independently attested early chain.

## H2. Contingent vindication / future-role model

Subject inference: Jesus may have expected imminent divine intervention, come to anticipate death, and still expected postmortem vindication plus a role in the consummation.

### Strength

Recent historical-Jesus scholarship makes this configuration historically plausible within Jewish apocalyptic expectation. Ferda's model and Allison's contingent-eschatology proposal independently make versions of the transition thinkable without requiring fully developed post-Easter doctrine during Jesus's ministry.

### Hostile challenge

The model depends on reconstructing pre-Easter Jesus from traditions written after Easter. Future-Son-of-Man sayings, passion predictions, and parousia traditions are precisely the materials most vulnerable to post-resurrection reinterpretation.

Ferda's economy argument is not a proof of origin in Jesus; post-Easter communities could also generate coherent expectation rapidly from scripture plus resurrection conviction.

### Status

PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS. Historical possibility strengthened; direct attribution to Jesus remains contested.

## H3. Parallel-category fusion model

Subject inference: early high Christology may emerge from multiple Jewish interpretive trajectories operating in parallel rather than a single linear ladder from human teacher to deity.

### Strength

The wider corpus independently establishes distinct Danielic, Adamic, Mosaic, Wisdom/Logos, messianic, exaltation, lordship, and apocalyptic categories in the relevant cultural environment. Early Christian texts demonstrably deploy more than one such grammar.

### Hostile challenge

Availability is not use, and use is not simultaneity. A modern synthesis can accidentally combine categories from different communities and dates into a synthetic earliest-Christology system no single group held.

Some categories may be retrospective literary/theological developments rather than causes of the earliest devotion.

### Status

PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS. Strong as a model of plural developmental trajectories; weak if converted into a claim that the earliest Jerusalem group already held the whole fused system.

## H4. Roman execution as historical pivot

Subject inference: crucifixion under Pilate is a hard historical anchor; later narratives may redistribute responsibility and theological meaning around that event.

### Strength

Roman crucifixion is among the most stable facts in the historical reconstruction. A Passover-period prophetic/kingdom movement and Temple confrontation provide plausible Roman threat perception without requiring an armed-insurgent model.

### Hostile challenge

The exact Roman rationale cannot be reconstructed directly from an extant execution dossier. Gospel trial narratives are theological narratives, and later source criticism can overcorrect by assuming every Jewish-authority role is secondary.

### Status

PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS. Execution by Roman authority is strong; detailed motive and responsibility allocation remain historical reconstruction.

## H5. Family prominence and possible dynastic authority

Subject inference: Jesus's family, especially James, may have mattered more to the earliest Jerusalem movement than later Christian memory commonly emphasizes.

### Strength

Pauline and Josephus traditions make James's prominence difficult to dismiss, while gospel sibling traditions indicate a remembered family network.

### Hostile challenge

Family prominence does not by itself establish dynastic succession ideology. James could become authoritative for many reasons: leadership ability, resurrection tradition, kinship prestige, local continuity, or retrospective status.

Later Marian interpretations of Jesus's siblings do not by themselves reveal what the earliest community thought their biological relationship was.

### Status

PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS. Family prominence strong; dynastic model remains a hypothesis.

## H6. Simulation-theory cross-check

Simulation theory makes miracle, revelation, incarnation-like embodiment, resurrection, and world transformation conceptually implementable by an external operator.

That fact does not improve the historical evidence for any Yeshua event because the current corpus contains no established discriminator selecting simulation/intervention over Jewish apocalyptic interpretation, unknown internal causation, ordinary historical development, literary memory, or other alternatives.

### Status

NO_HISTORICAL_PROMOTION.

## H7. Strongest surviving synthesis

After hostile review, the most robust minimal model remains:

- Yeshua was a first-century Jewish Galilean religious figure embedded in apocalyptic/kingdom expectation.
- His movement attributed unusual eschatological significance to him.
- Roman authority crucified him under Pilate.
- His followers very early became convinced that God had vindicated/raised/exalted him.
- Early interpretation of that conviction developed through multiple Jewish scriptural and conceptual grammars.
- Later Christian and anti-Christian memories grew more specific and polemical over time.

The clauses above do not settle supernatural truth, exact self-conception, biological paternity, empty-tomb historicity, or the mechanism behind resurrection belief.
