## Synopsis and beat map
Charlie tells Dennis that he may have cancer and asks him not to tell anyone. Dennis immediately tells Mac and Dee, and the group turns Charlie's supposed illness into a project to get him laid before he dies. They identify the Waitress's Livestrong bracelet as evidence that pity might make her receptive. Dennis tells her about Charlie, but she instead sleeps with Dennis. Mac then pays her $250 to sleep with Charlie. In parallel, Dee tries to solve the bar's labor problem by recruiting Artemis through a Coyote Ugly-style concept, while Mac meets Carmen and is attracted to her before learning she is transgender. Charlie eventually reveals that the cancer was fabricated specifically because he expected the Waitress to respond sympathetically. The Gang reveals that they paid her; Charlie reveals they never actually had sex; and Mac's interaction with Carmen ends with him accidentally striking her and fleeing men who interpret it as a hate crime. The Waitress keeps the money.

## Character state
- **Charlie:** commits one of his earliest major manipulations of the Gang and the Waitress, showing that his apparent vulnerability can itself be strategic.
- **Dennis:** violates Charlie's confidentiality almost instantly and turns the illness into a sexual logistics problem; he also sleeps with the Waitress despite knowing Charlie's fixation.
- **Mac:** his attraction to Carmen collides with his rigid ideas about gender/sexuality, creating a pattern that later becomes highly significant to his own sexuality arc.
- **Dee / Artemis:** Artemis debuts through Dee's acting-world connection, immediately establishing her fearless performance style and lack of conventional embarrassment.

## Themes / comic mechanism
Compassion is treated as exploitable currency. Charlie weaponizes imagined illness; the Gang monetizes pity; the Waitress monetizes their desperation. The episode also introduces a recurring Sunny move: a supposedly serious moral boundary is rapidly overwhelmed by vanity, sex, resentment, and transactional thinking.

## Continuity / later relevance
