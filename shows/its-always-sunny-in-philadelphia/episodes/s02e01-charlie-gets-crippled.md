## Synopsis / analysis
Frank Reynolds abruptly re-enters Dennis and Dee's lives while Dennis, drunk, accidentally runs over Charlie. Frank says he is divorcing Barbara, giving away his money, and wants to reconnect with his children. Dennis and Dee care most about the threatened inheritance and rob the family home. Charlie discovers that temporary disability produces sympathy and attention at a strip club; Mac and Dennis then imitate disability with wheelchairs to attract women, while Dee independently adopts a brace-and-crutches performance. Frank, rejected by his biological children, gravitates toward Charlie and offers to pay rent in exchange for moving into Charlie's apartment. The episode ends with another drunk-driving collision that injures Dee and Frank and leaves the Gang collectively hospitalized.

Frank's introduction immediately changes the show's social geometry. He is not a stabilizing parent; he wants entry into the Gang's degraded freedom. Charlie is the first person who offers him a usable route into that world, establishing the Charlie/Frank domestic partnership. The Gang's response to Charlie's injury also crystallizes a recurring rule: sympathy is valuable primarily as leverage.
