## Synopsis / analysis
Dennis and Dee quit Paddy's to pursue grand ambitions, immediately discover unemployment pays more than their bar work, then decide to qualify for welfare by presenting Dennis as a crack addict and Dee as disabled. When paperwork requires evidence, they actually buy crack and rapidly become addicted. At Paddy's, Mac and Charlie try to replace them through a Work for Welfare program while Frank attempts to bond with the men. Charlie is temporarily entrusted with Frank's hidden money, and he and Mac rationalize stealing from it as their deserved “vig,” escalating into extravagant spending and hired sex workers. Frank freezes the account; Dennis and Dee reach withdrawal; Charlie and Mac return to work to cover their debts. Frank is impressed by Charlie's theft and promotes him.

The episode turns every supposed social system—career ambition, unemployment, welfare, subsidized work, banking, even Frank's attempt at fatherhood—into something the Gang tries to arbitrage. Dennis and Dee do not fake dysfunction from a safe distance; their shortcut makes the dysfunction real.
