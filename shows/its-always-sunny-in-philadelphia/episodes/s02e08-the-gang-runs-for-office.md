## Synopsis / analysis
The Gang notices the money and corruption surrounding local politics and recruits Dennis to run for comptroller—not to govern, but to create leverage. Frank initially manages Dennis, then switches to Dee after Dennis fires him. Mac attempts to bribe union representatives and discovers they take coercion more seriously than he does. Dennis tries conventional retail campaigning while Charlie helps make an inept video. Frank and Dee attempt blackmail against the actual comptroller; he instead dates Dee. Frank and Mac create a smear tape accusing Dennis of rape, but Charlie values the physical tape for his Garbage Pail Kids collection. Dennis finally quits when he learns the office would require actual work.

Politics is treated as a scheme until the Gang encounters institutions and actors with more practiced corruption than theirs. Dennis's candidacy is another identity fantasy: he likes the authority image, not the job. The episode also strengthens Charlie's idiosyncratic object/value hierarchy—campaign strategy loses to collectible trash-culture artifacts.
