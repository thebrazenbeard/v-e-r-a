Barbara Reynolds dies. Her will leaves money to Bruce Mathis, the house to Dennis, and nothing to Dee. Dennis responds to inheriting a mansion by trying to manufacture a social life through a party; Mac, Charlie and Dennis distribute disturbingly ambiguous friendship flyers and attract only a couple of college men whom Mac and Dennis harass. Frank and Dee attempt to undermine Bruce by pretending to be engaged, but Bruce counters by bringing adopted children, insisting on their marriage, and exposing the hollowness of the performance.

Death again produces status maneuvering rather than grief. Dee's exclusion from the will sharpens her family resentment; Dennis treats property as proof that he should have friends; Frank converts bereavement into another contest with Bruce.
