Trapped as the cruise ship appears to be sinking, the Gang cycles through blame, attempted escape, resignation and an unexpectedly harmonious shared musical moment. Facing death briefly reduces the need to dominate one another, but survival restores the ordinary hierarchy almost immediately. The two-parter therefore permits genuine tenderness without pretending it is permanent character reform: attachment is real, and so is the system that normally buries it.
