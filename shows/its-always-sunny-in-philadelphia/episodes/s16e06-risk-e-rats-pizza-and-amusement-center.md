The Gang returns to a childhood amusement center expecting the dangerous, permissive environment they remember and finds it sanitized by liability rules, corporate caution and changed cultural standards. They interpret removed hazards as evidence that fun itself has been destroyed, then recreate the old risk with predictable consequences. The nostalgia is unreliable but not wholly false: the place really did change. The failure lies in treating childhood danger as the essence of authenticity rather than one feature of a system adults later had reasons to modify.
