## Pali transmission

SuttaCentral's Dīgha Nikāya publication introduction notes the traditional Sri Lankan account of the Pali canon being written down around the first century BCE after oral transmission.

Use:
TRANSMISSION_HISTORY.

Do not infer:
first-century-BCE writing = first composition of all included discourses.

## Material history

### Metropolitan Museum — South Asia chronology
The Met's South Asian historical timeline places Aśoka in the third century BCE and treats Mauryan patronage, pillars, inscriptions, and stupas as major early Buddhist historical evidence.

Use:
MATERIAL_EVIDENCE / HISTORICAL_CONTEXT.

## Source hierarchy for claims

A claim about early Buddhism should preferably be triangulated across:

1. EARLY_PARALLEL_TEXTS.
2. PHYSICAL_MANUSCRIPT_WITNESSES.
3. CONTEMPORARY_OR_NEAR-CONTEMPORARY_INSCRIPTIONS.
4. MATERIAL/ARCHAEOLOGICAL_CONTEXT.
5. LATER COMMENTARIAL TRADITION.
6. MODERN SCHOLARLY_RECONSTRUCTION.

Later layers can preserve earlier material.

They do not automatically outrank earlier direct evidence.

## High-risk source mistakes

- treating a Pali commentary as contemporary with the Buddha;
- treating a Chinese translation date as the original composition date;
- treating manuscript date as composition date;
- treating Mahāyāna doctrinal maturity as present in earliest discourse layers;
- treating Aśokan legends as equivalent to Aśokan edicts;
- treating later biographies as neutral historical reportage.
