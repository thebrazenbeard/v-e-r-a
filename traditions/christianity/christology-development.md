# Christology development

## Purpose

Track how claims about Yeshua's identity move from Jewish messianic/eschatological categories into increasingly explicit claims of pre-existence, incarnation, cosmic agency, and later doctrinal formulations.

This is not a claim that early Christians began with a merely human Jesus and mechanically "upgraded" him. Elevated claims appear early. The development is about **forms, relations, vocabulary, and synthesis**.

## Jewish starting vocabulary

Early followers inherit categories such as:

- Messiah / anointed one;
- Son of God / royal or elect sonship;
- Son of Man / Danielic and human imagery;
- Wisdom traditions;
- divine Word/Logos traditions;
- exalted agents and heavenly figures;
- resurrection and vindication.

These categories make high claims possible without requiring that every speaker already possesses the later Nicene/Chalcedonian conceptual system.

## Resurrection / exaltation

One early interpretive pattern understands resurrection as divine vindication/exaltation: the executed one has been raised and enthroned by God.

This differs conceptually from saying that the earthly Yeshua was a pre-existent divine person who had always shared God's identity, even though later Christianity can hold both together.

## Pauline material

Paul's letters contain strikingly elevated language about Christ and are earlier than the canonical Gospels. This blocks any simplistic claim that divine/high Christology only appears very late.

At the same time, Paul's preferred formulations and narrative interests are not identical to John's later Logos prologue or fourth-century conciliar definitions.

## Synoptic Gospels

Mark, Matthew and Luke portray Jesus through overlapping Jewish categories: Messiah, Son, Son of Man, prophet, teacher, healer, apocalyptic agent, suffering/vindicated figure.

Matthew and Luke add miraculous-conception narratives, giving divine causation to Jesus's conception while still embedding him in Davidic/Israelite history.

## Johannine Logos

John 1 presents the Logos/Word as pre-existent, with God, involved in creation, and becoming flesh.

This is a major conceptual bridge for the project because it combines:

- pre-existence;
- creation;
- revelatory mediation;
- embodiment/instantiation;
- return to divine glory.

### Systems analogy — SPECULATIVE MODEL

John's Logos can be compared structurally to **authoritative information/expression instantiated inside an environment**.

Do not reverse the analogy. `Logos` is not Greek for software, and John is not describing executable code.

## Later doctrinal consolidation

Centuries of dispute over Jesus's relation to God, humanity and Spirit produced increasingly technical theological vocabulary. Nicene and later formulations should therefore be recorded as later **solutions to inherited interpretive conflicts**, not silently inserted into the mouth of the historical Yeshua.

## Research questions

- Which elevated claims are earliest?
- Which are Jewish developments versus Hellenistic philosophical expression versus mixed environments?
- Does "pre-existence" mean the same thing in every text?
- When does "agent of God" become "ontologically within divine identity" in particular communities?
- How much later orthodoxy preserves, synthesizes, or overrides earlier ambiguity?

No single evolutionary ladder should be assumed before the texts are compared.
