# Noncanonical and "Gnostic"-adjacent material

## Why preserve it

The later New Testament canon is not the entire surviving record of early Christian imagination, authority disputes, sayings traditions, infancy expansion, cosmology or spiritual interpretation.

Noncanonical does not mean secretly true. Canonical does not mean historically neutral. This lane records both without using canon status as a historical truth meter.

## Gospel of Thomas

Thomas is a sayings collection rather than a narrative Gospel. It famously includes sayings about:

- finding meaning and not tasting death;
- seeking and discovery;
- the Kingdom being "inside" and "outside";
- self-knowledge and relation to the living Father.

Its date, sources, and relation to the Synoptic traditions remain debated. Some sayings parallel canonical material; others do not.

### Project relevance

Thomas is especially important to the **substrate-skepticism** inquiry because it emphasizes discovery, self-knowledge, hidden meaning and a Kingdom not reducible to a distant geographical destination.

That makes it symbolically compatible with a layered-reality reading. It does **not** prove Yeshua taught simulation theory or that Thomas preserves pristine secret teaching.

## Gospel of Mary

The surviving text is fragmentary. Mary receives/communicates revelation and faces challenge from Peter/Andrew; Levi defends her. It is central to questions of:

- apostolic authority;
- gender and leadership;
- revelation inaccessible to other disciples;
- competing memories of who understood Jesus best.

The exact identity of "Mary" is itself a scholarly issue; Magdalene identification is common but should not be treated as completely question-free.

## Infancy Gospel traditions

Later infancy works fill the enormous narrative silence around Mary and the child Jesus. They demonstrate a recurring information dynamic:

> canonical silence -> community curiosity -> narrative elaboration -> later tradition may become culturally familiar enough to feel ancient.

This is useful far beyond infancy stories; it is a general transmission warning.

## "Gnosticism" caution

`Gnosticism` can falsely suggest one organized alternative religion with one doctrine. The texts usually grouped under that label are diverse.

Use more specific descriptions when possible:

- salvation through knowledge/revelation;
- material/cosmic hierarchy;
- divine spark or origin traditions;
- hostile/ignorant creator figures in some systems;
- ascent through cosmic layers;
- secret teaching/authority traditions.

Do not import one text's cosmology into another merely because both are called Gnostic.

## Structural relevance to the GOD model

Some late-antique texts make unusually explicit distinctions between:

- visible material order;
- hidden higher reality;
- ignorance versus awakening;
- origin outside the apparent world;
- liberation through knowledge.

Those are genuine structural parallels to modern simulation/layer metaphors.

Transmission questions must remain open:

- Did a later community transform earlier Jewish/Christian apocalyptic ideas?
- Did philosophical dualism intensify them?
- Do similar metaphors arise independently because "appearance versus reality" is a recurring human problem?

Similarity is the beginning of the research question, not its answer.
