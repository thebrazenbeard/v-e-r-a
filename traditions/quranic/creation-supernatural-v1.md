# Qur'anic Creation and Supernatural World V1

Status: `PRIMARY-TEXT-CENTERED / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Scope

This packet reconstructs Qur'anic claims about creation and nonhuman agents without importing later kalam, tafsir, hadith demonology, or philosophical cosmology unless explicitly labeled.

## Creator and creation

The Qur'an repeatedly presents God as originator/creator of heavens, earth, living beings and humans.

Primary anchors:
- Q 6:101-102: originator of heavens and earth, creator of all things;
- Q 42:11: originator of heavens and earth and incomparable to created things;
- Q 2:255: ownership and sustaining preservation of heavens and earth.

Creation functions rhetorically as evidence for:
- exclusive worship;
- divine knowledge and power;
- providence;
- resurrection;
- judgment.

## Human creation

Different Qur'anic passages use several creation idioms, including:
- dust/earth;
- clay;
- a drop/fluid;
- embryological stages;
- creation of Adam;
- reproduction of later humans.

V1 does not assume these are competing scientific descriptions. Their literary/rhetorical function and chronology must be studied passage by passage.

## Adam and earthly vocation

Q 2:30-39 presents:
- God's announcement to angels of a human `khalifa`/successive authority on earth;
- angelic concern about corruption/bloodshed;
- Adam being taught names;
- angelic prostration;
- Adam and spouse in the garden;
- transgression, descent and divine guidance.

Other Adam/Iblis cycles occur in Q 7, 15, 17, 18, 20 and 38 with differing emphases.

The project should compare these internal retellings before importing Genesis, Enochic, Syriac or later Islamic parallels.

## Angels

Qur'anic angels are divine servants/messengers appearing in several functions:
- worship/praise;
- communication of divine messages;
- participation in Adam narrative;
- support or punishment;
- taking souls;
- judgment/eschatological service.

`Angel` is not interchangeable with `jinn`, even though Iblis appears within an angelic-prostration scene.

## Iblis

Iblis is the named rebel in Adam-prostration narratives.

Q 18:50 is a critical semantic control because it explicitly says Iblis was `of the jinn`.

Primary:
- https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=18&verse=50

The project therefore rejects a careless inference:

`Iblis appears among angels -> Iblis is textually identified as an angel`.

Later exegetical attempts to resolve the narrative belong to tafsir history.

## Shaytan / satans

The Qur'an uses `shaytan`/plural `shayatin` in contexts of temptation, deception, hostility and rebellious unseen agency.

V1 keeps these categories distinct:
- Iblis = named figure;
- shaytan = adversarial/tempting category that may include Iblis and others depending on passage;
- jinn = broader class of beings with morally diverse members.

## Jinn

Q 72 is especially important because jinn hear Qur'anic recitation, evaluate it, believe or disbelieve, and speak of their own diversity.

Q 72:1-2 depicts a group of jinn hearing a wondrous recitation and affirming monotheistic belief.

Primary:
- https://quran.com/al-jinn

Therefore:

`jinn` != `demon` as a blanket translation.

Some jinn are believing; some are not. The category is morally heterogeneous in the Qur'an.

## Solomon and nonhuman agents

Qur'anic Solomon traditions involve winds, jinn and other beings/resources under divinely granted authority.

These passages should later be compared with Jewish, Christian, Arabian and Late Antique Solomon traditions, but V1 records Qur'anic functions first rather than assuming dependence.

## Cosmic signs

Natural phenomena function as `ayat`/signs:
- alternation of night/day;
- rain and vegetation;
- stars/celestial bodies;
- animals;
- winds;
- seas;
- human generation;
- historical ruins and destroyed communities.

This joins cosmology to epistemology: created order is repeatedly presented as interpretable evidence.

## Divine throne/seat and heavenly order

Q 2:255 refers to God's `kursi`; other passages use `arsh` (throne).

V1 rule:
- record lexical occurrence and immediate proposition;
- do not resolve later debates over literal/metaphorical ontology inside the primary-text layer;
- later Sunni, Mu'tazili, Shi'i, philosophical and mystical interpretations belong to reception history.

## Death and intermediate states

The Qur'an speaks of death, souls being taken, graves and resurrection, but later highly detailed barzakh/grave-punishment cosmologies require later textual evidence.

Where `barzakh` occurs, its Qur'anic use must be studied directly rather than assuming the full later doctrine.

## Comparison guards

Before comparing with another mythos, preserve these distinctions:

- Adam narrative != Genesis narrative by default;
- Iblis != Satan of every Jewish/Christian text;
- jinn != fallen angels;
- angels != daimones;
- throne/seat != identical to any one biblical or Iranian heavenly-throne system;
- cosmic sign theology != Greek natural theology automatically.

Allowed first-pass comparative labels:
- `LEXICAL_PARALLEL`;
- `NARRATIVE_PARALLEL`;
- `STRUCTURAL_PARALLEL`;
- `CONTACT_ENVIRONMENT_SHARED`;
- `TRANSMISSION_HYPOTHESIS` only after chronology/carrier tests.
