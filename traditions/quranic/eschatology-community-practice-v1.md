# Qur'anic Eschatology, Ethics, and Community Practice V1

Status: `PRIMARY-TEXT-CENTERED / REVIEW REQUIRED`

## Scope

This packet keeps three connected but distinct Qur'anic dimensions together:

1. resurrection and final judgment;
2. moral/accountability language;
3. practices and regulations addressed to the believing community.

Later hadith, fiqh, ritual manuals, creed and state law are separate reception/institutional layers.

## Eschatology is structurally central

The Qur'an repeatedly frames present conduct through future resurrection and judgment.

The Oxford Handbook survey of Qur'anic eschatology identifies major clusters:
- signs of the Hour/end;
- cosmic termination/transformation;
- resurrection of the dead;
- divine judgment;
- reward/punishment;
- God's final sovereign order.

Scholarly control:
- https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34388/chapter-abstract/291614203

## Bodily resurrection

Q 75:3-4 directly answers doubt that human bones can be reassembled and insists on divine ability even to restore fine physical detail.

Primary:
- https://quran.com/75/3-4

This is strong primary evidence that resurrection is not merely an immortal-soul metaphor in the Qur'anic argument.

Other passages invoke:
- revival of dead earth;
- initial creation as analogy for recreation;
- emergence from graves;
- restored individual accountability.

## Cosmic end

Short and middle-length suras frequently use dramatic cosmic images:
- sun/stars altered;
- sky split or changed;
- mountains moved/crushed;
- graves overturned;
- earth discloses burdens/news;
- trumpet/blast imagery.

These are eschatological rhetoric and cosmology simultaneously.

V1 will not prematurely decide whether every image is literal physical prediction, poetic imagery, visionary symbolism, or combinations thereof. Passage-specific literary analysis comes first.

## Judgment and record

The Qur'an uses multiple judgment mechanisms/images:
- books/records;
- weighing/scales;
- witnesses;
- bodily testimony;
- public disclosure;
- divine knowledge;
- individual responsibility.

The combination creates a moral universe in which actions are remembered and adjudicated even if socially hidden.

## Paradise and punishment

Reward imagery includes gardens, shade, water, fruit, peace, companionship and divine favor.

Punishment imagery includes fire, heat, confinement, regret, thirst and exclusion.

Later theological debates over eternality, intercession, grave punishment, exact geography and visionary vs bodily experience must remain separate unless the Qur'anic passage itself bears the claim.

## Intercession

Q 2:255 is an important control: intercession is not presented as autonomous power against God; it occurs only by divine permission.

Primary:
- https://quran.com/2/255

Other intercession passages must be mapped before later saintly/prophetic intercession systems are imported.

## Moral agency

Qur'anic ethics repeatedly tie belief to action.

Major domains include:
- justice;
- truthful testimony;
- care for poor/orphans/travelers;
- almsgiving;
- keeping covenants;
- sexual/family regulation;
- commercial fairness;
- murder/violence limits;
- forgiveness and retaliation;
- humility vs arrogance;
- gratitude vs ingratitude;
- hypocrisy;
- communal solidarity.

This is not a simple `faith versus works` system; that dichotomy imports a later Christian theological frame.

## Prayer

The Qur'an repeatedly commands prayer (`salat`) and often pairs prayer with almsgiving (`zakat`).

It refers to bodily orientation and prayer times/conditions in multiple passages, but the complete later five-prayer ritual manual is not laid out as one self-contained Qur'anic chapter.

Method rule:
- record Qur'anic prayer commands/practices first;
- later hadith/fiqh supplies later procedural elaboration and must be labeled as such.

## Qibla and sacred geography

Q 2:144 instructs orientation toward the Sacred Mosque.

Primary:
- https://quran.com/2/144

The verse is primary evidence for a prayer-direction instruction and communal sacred geography.

Later reports specifying the exact date, prior direction and circumstances belong to historical tradition unless independently corroborated.

## Fasting

Q 2:183-187 prescribes fasting, explicitly relating it to those before the Qur'anic community and connecting the practice to Ramadan/Qur'anic revelation in the passage.

Primary:
- https://quran.com/2/183-187

The Qur'anic passage itself establishes:
- obligation;
- limited days/allowances;
- relation to previous communities;
- Ramadan;
- eating/drinking/sexual timing boundaries.

Later juristic detail is a later layer.

## Almsgiving and economic ethics

`Zakat`, almsgiving and spending recur throughout the Qur'an.

Q 9:60 names categories for alms distribution, while many other passages emphasize voluntary spending, care of vulnerable people and condemnation of hoarding/exploitation.

V1 distinguishes:
- Qur'anic almsgiving language;
- later fiscal/legal zakat systems;
- caliphal tax administration.

## Pilgrimage and sanctuary

The Qur'an associates Abraham, the House/Sacred Mosque and pilgrimage rites in several passages, especially Q 2 and Q 22.

Primary-text analysis should distinguish:
- sanctuary theology;
- Abrahamic framing;
- pilgrimage commands;
- sacrificial/ritual elements;
- later sira accounts of pre-Islamic and Muhammadan ritual history.

## Food and purity boundaries

The Qur'an regulates food through prohibited categories and rules concerning slaughter/food and relations with People of the Book.

These should later be compared with Jewish/Christian/Arabian practices only after exact verses and dates/contexts are registered.

## Marriage, divorce and inheritance

Longer/community-oriented suras include substantial family regulation:
- marriage eligibility;
- divorce procedures;
- waiting periods;
- maintenance;
- inheritance shares;
- guardianship/orphans;
- sexual conduct.

This is evidence of a community whose proclamation includes social/legal ordering, not only individual apocalyptic warning.

The shift is relevant to inner-Qur'anic chronology, but `later legal density` remains a scholarly chronology observation, not verse-level self-dating.

## Warfare, treaty and peace

The Qur'an includes passages on fighting, defense, treaty, sanctuary restrictions, prisoners, spoils and relations with hostile/non-hostile groups.

Method guard:
- no single `sword verse` may silently erase the rest of the corpus;
- no pacific verse may silently erase armed-conflict material;
- later abrogation (`naskh`) systems are reception/jurisprudential models and require separate historical analysis;
- each war/peace proposition must retain textual and likely chronological context.

## Community categories

The text differentiates, in varying passages:
- believers;
- hypocrites;
- disbelievers;
- associators;
- Jews;
- Christians;
- People of the Book;
- allies/hostiles/non-hostiles in more particular legal-political settings.

These categories can change rhetorical function across the corpus.

A later legal status such as the fully developed `dhimmi` institution must not be projected automatically into every Qur'anic `People of the Book` passage.

## No complete later Islamic system yet

The Qur'an provides foundational religious, ethical and legal material, but V1 distinguishes it from later Islamic institutions:

- canonical hadith collections;
- sira/maghazi biographies;
- Sunni/Shi'i authority systems;
- madhhab jurisprudence;
- kalam creeds;
- Sufism/tariqa institutions;
- caliphal administrative law;
- medieval tafsir.

These are not dismissed; they are future historical layers.

## Internal chronological development

Neuwirth's literary-historical model emphasizes an early proclamation centered strongly on judgment and a later increasingly complex communal/scriptural discourse. Sinai's work supports treating inner-Qur'anic chronology as a serious critical problem.
