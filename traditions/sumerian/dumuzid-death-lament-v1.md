What happens when the corpus moves from heroic/royal death narratives into Dumuzid capture, lament, commemoration, and mythic mortuary care?

## 1. Dumuzid's Dream: current source state

CDLI Q000347 is a current digital composite of Dumuzid's Dream.

Current score state:

The witness inventory is geographically and chronologically mixed:

## 2. Death is explicit before capture

The current Q000347 composite opens with Dumuzid lamenting and anticipating his own death.

Line 12:
u4-da u4 ug5-ge-gu10 nu-un-zu

The ETCSL translation renders the surrounding appeal as Dumuzid asking the countryside to tell his mother if she does not know the day when he is dead.

This matters because the composition frames the later pursuit/capture through anticipated death and lament from the beginning.

Classification:
- source wording: PRIMARY_TEXT
- narrative interpretation as death-foreboding: TEXTUAL_SYSTEM_SUMMARY
- historical ritual practice: not established by this line alone.

## 3. galla pursuit is a distinct death-capture mechanism

The current composite repeatedly uses gal5-la2 / galla in the pursuit sequence.

Examples:
- line 59: a galla acts in the violent pursuit sequence;
- line 63: the great galla descends from the sheepfold;
- lines 89-90: Geshtin-ana tells Dumuzid that his galla have gone after him;
- later repeated pursuit/capture sequences continue galla language.

These are pursuer/captor beings in the Dumuzid narrative.

Control:

GALLA != GIDIM

The galla are not the dead-person ghost category mapped by gidim.

A semantic graph must keep:
- captor/demon-like entity;
- deceased-person spirit;
- death-realm place

as separate types.

## 5. Arali is not merely a generic synonym for all underworld language

Dumuzid's Dream gives Arali a concrete narrative role:
- a place/zone where Dumuzid can hide or fall;
- a place associated with his capture;
- a term embedded in the pursuit/death sequence.

Current Sumerian lexical resources also preserve a scholarly tradition associating a-ra-li with the netherworld and, specifically, the desert region between Bad-tibira and Uruk associated with Dumuzid's pastoral/death tradition.

That later lexical synthesis is useful but must remain secondary to composition-level evidence.

Project control:

ARALI_DUMUZID = COMPOSITION_SPECIFIC_DEATH/CAPTURE_TOPOGRAPHY

not:

ARALI = UNIVERSAL_SUMERIAN_NAME_FOR_THE_UNDERWORLD

## 7. Arali as a lament locus in Inana and Bilulu

ETCSL preserves Arali at the beginning of the composition:

The lament for Dumuzid is made to reach:
- the desert;
- the house Arali;
- Bad-tibira;
- Du-shuba;
- the shepherding country / sheepfold of Dumuzid.

The lament sequence is repeated near the end:
- "in the house Arali" the lament is to be uttered;
- Du-shuba, Bad-tibira, desert/shepherding country are repeated alongside it.

The source-language gloss directly preserves:
e2 a-ra-li

house Arali.

Disposition:

INANA_BILULU_ARALI = LAMENT_TOPOGRAPHY_CONFIRMED

This is not the same genre function as Arali in Dumuzid's Dream.

## 8. Lament geography is not simple cosmography

The Inana and Bilulu lament moves through multiple named and landscape locations:
- desert;
- house Arali;
- Bad-tibira;
- Du-shuba;
- shepherding country.

This suggests a ritual-poetic geography of Dumuzid remembrance rather than a single one-point map of "where the dead go."

Project inference:

LAMENT_GEOGRAPHY can connect:
- cult city;
- pastoral landscape;
- death-place;
- commemorative place;
- mythic topology.

But this does not mean each named location is physically identical or cosmologically equivalent.

## 9. Resting place, vengeance, and mortuary care

Inana and Bilulu explicitly says Inana is permitted to:
- make good Dumuzid's resting place;
- avenge him.

The poem also contains a desert rite sequence involving:
- water libation;
- flour sprinkling;
- calling out so that the absent/dead lad is made present in the place from which he vanished.

This is high-value evidence for a literary/ritual model linking:
- lament;
- place;
- provisioning/libation;
- presence of the dead/absent Dumuzid;
- vengeance/commemoration.

Classification:
- textual occurrence: PRIMARY_TEXT
- reconstruction of an actual performed mortuary rite: HISTORICAL_RECONSTRUCTION / SCHOLARLY_INTERPRETATION, not automatic.

Control:

TEXT_DESCRIBES_LIBATION_AND_LAMENT != UNIVERSAL_FUNERARY_LITURGY

## 10. Cross-composition Arali comparison

Dumuzid's Dream:
- Arali occurs in pursuit/hiding/capture context;
- current composite;
- at least one relevant Arali line has aligned Ur witness support.

Inana and Bilulu:
- Arali occurs repeatedly in lament geography;
- single listed Nippur witness in current CDLI metadata;
- source-language ETCSL gloss preserves "house Arali."

Strongest V1 inference:

ARALI is a recurring Dumuzid-associated death/lament place-term across at least two compositions with different narrative/ritual functions.

This is stronger than:
"Arali appears once."

It is weaker than:
"Arali was the single universally defined Sumerian underworld."

## 11. Relationship to Inana's Descent

Inana's Descent did not produce Arali in the targeted ETCSL search used in the earlier lexical map.

Dumuzid's Dream and Inana and Bilulu do.

Therefore the Dumuzid corpus demonstrates that:

DEATH_REALM_LEXICON_IS_COMPOSITIONALLY_DISTRIBUTED

A composition can strongly concern the netherworld while preferring kur/Ganzer, while another Dumuzid composition uses Arali.

This is exactly why English "underworld" must not replace source lexemes in the registry.

## 12. Relationship to ordinary human funerary evidence

Dumuzid is divine/mythic and royal-pastoral in function.

The compositions can illuminate:
- lament forms;
- death-place terminology;
- libation/provisioning imagery;
- capture/death mythology;
- continuing relationship between living divine figures and the dead/absent Dumuzid.

They cannot by themselves establish:
- ordinary household funerals;
- average Sumerian burial practice;
- universal human afterlife expectations.

Keep the prior human-funerary lane independent.
